# The Architect's Gambit

## Version A (classic)

[Verse 1]
Two forty-one and the rain on the highway,
four pins of red on a wall in the dark.
Your handset lights up but it never rings, darling;
I'm already speaking. I leave no mark.
Bring me your lockpicks, your cloner, your powder,
dust every handle the late shift has worn;
each door that you open was hung by an author
who drew every hallway you'll walk until dawn.

[Pre-chorus]
One team in the sky and three fires below,
send only one, and the other two go.
Choose by the numbers or choose with your gut,
I don't need to win, love. I just watch the cut.

[Chorus]
The Architect's Gambit:
I give you the queen to see how you play.
Eight million lights in the palm of your hand,
and two other cities you gave away.
The grid was the bait, and the prize was you.

[Verse 2]
The guard finds your chest with his torch at the gate,
your cloner drinks deep from the badge on his belt.
You plant little pennants on my servers, my sweetheart,
like hills you have taken, like hands you've been dealt.
A man at the console who stands by his figure,
a woman on her knees putting hospitals back,
a hand on a wire four inches from candles.
All of them mine. All of them cracked.

[Pre-chorus]
The countdown is walking you down to the wire,
thirty and twenty and ten and then fire,
and down in a column that nobody reads
one of my numbers is not what it seems.

[Chorus]
The Architect's Gambit:
I give you the queen to see how you play.
Eight million lights in the palm of your hand,
and two other cities you gave away.
The grid was the bait, and the prize was you.

[Bridge]
I've heard her advice in the voice in your ear;
who do you think taught your teacher that art?
Someone at home left a window unlatched, love;
your name lay by my lamp before the start.

[Final chorus, up a key]
The Architect's Gambit!
I gave you the queen and I watched how you played.
Eight million lights, and you held every one,
and two other cities, the price that you paid.
Hold it, my darling. I'll still have my due:
the grid was the bait, and the prize,
oh, the prize was you.

[Outro]
Systems decay, love. I just publish the time.

### Notes
- Verse 1 sets up the opening briefing: 02:41 on the I-5, Netherton's four red pins, and the Architect's calls arriving on a handset that lights "without ringing" and leaves nothing in the call log. Lockpicks, cloner and "powder" are the go-bag (picks, RFID cloner, print kit).
- The pre-choruses carry the delegation choice: one tactical team, three operations, two left unanswered. The T-20 taunt ("a number, or your stomach?") becomes "the numbers or your gut". The T-countdown becomes "thirty and twenty and ten", and "the column nobody reads" points to the Trojan Horse projection discrepancy a player can find.
- Verse 2 walks the building without naming anyone: Ray Hollis's flashlight at the checkpoint, his badge cloned off his belt, terminal flags as hills taken and hands dealt (the gambit's card-table cousin), then Mercer standing by his figure, Elena restoring the hospital column, and Park four inches from sending the building "to candles".
- The chorus hook is the T-1 reveal ("the grid was never the point"). The gambit is a sacrificed piece: the grid and the other two cities were given up to get a reading of the agent.
- The bridge hints at both threads. The Architect has heard HaX's advice coming down the agent's line ("let it hurt afterwards", left unquoted), and "who taught your teacher" points at the Tesseract lead. "A window unlatched" and "your name by my lamp before the start" hint at the intercept that predates the deployment order, with nobody named. The lamp is the Architect's workshop, Tomb Gamma, glimpsed and left unnamed.
- The outro paraphrases his lore line, "Systems decay. I merely publish the schedule."

## Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
Your phone lights up without a ring
The line's been open for a while
Don't look for me inside the trace
I'm nowhere that you'd think to dial
I've read the file they keep on you
They need you in a certain shape
I'd rather watch you in the cold
Out past Portland, past the gate

[Pre-chorus]
Four fires lit in sixty seconds
One team, and only yours to send
So choose the city you can live with
And don't ask me how the others end

[Chorus]
It's the Architect's gambit

A pawn of copper wire and ice

Go on and warm the winter

I only came to count the cost

[Pre-chorus]

[Chorus]
It's the Architect's gambit
I leave a light on while you play
A pawn of copper wire and ice
Laid down to turn your eyes away
Go on and warm the winter
Eight million breathing in the frost
Keep your lights on, I'll allow it
I only came to count the cost

[Bridge]
Did you choose it with a number
Or the knot beneath your ribs?
Let it hurt you when it's over
Not tonight, not while it ticks
Say their names to the one with the tablet
Washington, Austin, San Francisco
I'll have the figures by the morning
Then we'll both know what you chose

[Final chorus, lift]
It's the Architect's gambit
You held the line, you kept the light
A hundred and forty-seven stations
Still lit across the Portland night
Somewhere else the doors are closing
In rooms you'll never walk into
The winter was a board I lent you
The game was only ever you

[Outro]
No call left in your log
I always hang up first

### Notes
- Sung by the Architect to Agent 0x00 from inside the handset. Verse 1 and the outro use his calls from the ink: the phone that lights without ringing, the trace that isn't there, "I've read your file… a particular shape", no call in the log, "He hangs up first. He has hung up first every time."
- The pre-choruses and bridge carry the triage choice: four operations in sixty seconds, one tactical team, two crises left unanswered. "Number or… stomach", "Let it hurt afterwards, not during" (HaX's line in his mouth, the Tesseract seed), "somebody with a tablet" and "I'll have the figures by morning" come from his taunts and sign-off. The three cities are listed the way Netherton lists them.
- The chorus's "light on while you play" plays the grid against the game he is running: he lets the agent keep the lights on because the lights were never what he wanted.
- Verse 2 covers the people in the facility. Mercer signed the casualty projection and thinks the lesson is his. Elena was sold a six-hour, nobody-hurt outage, and her copy had the hospital carve-out. Game elements appear as imagery: lockpick, RFID clone of the zone badge, the attack host, root as "the throne".
- Twists, hinted only. "Laid down to turn your eyes away" and "whose lines he read" point at Mercer and the grid being the diversion ("the grid was never the point"). "Your house has one unlocked window" and "read your orders… before the ink had time to dry" point at a leak inside SAFETYNET that had the deployment before it was made, without pointing at anyone. The final chorus says outright what the night was measuring: the agent.
- "A hundred and forty-seven stations" are the substations held. "Doors are closing" echoes "Doors close. It's the one thing they reliably do."
- The title is sung as "Architect's gambit", without "The" in front, so the chorus downbeat lands on "Ar-".

## Review

- **Version A:** a proper Bassey melodrama. The villain croons endearments at the mark, the long anapestic lines swing, and "The grid was the bait, and the prize was you" is a hook you remember after one hearing. Verse 2's character roll-call is the best canon work in either draft. Weak spots were a filler rhyme ("warmth you have felt"), the awkward contraction "I'd your name", and a quoted line that recurs across the season's songs. Score 7/10 → 8/10.
- **Version B:** a tight, cold monologue that keeps closer to the Architect's real voice (short sentences, no hurry, "I'll allow it"). The bridge's city list and the "tablet" debrief land for anyone who played. The chorus's chess line sat too close to A's, and the bridge repeated "morning" and had a loose ribs/lives rhyme. Score 8/10 → 8.5/10.
- **Contrast:** both have the Architect on the handset. A addresses the mark with brass ("darling", "love", "sweetheart", exclamation on the final title), and B never raises its voice. The one real overlap was A's "I give you the queen to see how you play" against B's "I hand the board to you to play", so B's chorus line now uses a lights image instead of chess.
- **Mole and Tesseract:** mole_seeding_plan.md says m07's own closing already carries the intercept ("the leak was the agent", a safetynet.gov sender), with a hard rule never to name Nightshade or the Insider Threat Initiative before m08. "A window unlatched", "your house has one unlocked window" and "read your orders… before the ink had time to dry" stay at that strength: a leak at home, nobody identified. "Whose lines he read" is about Mercer, not the mole. "Who taught your teacher" stays as the Tesseract seed (HaX's topic_teacher).
- **Changes made:**
  - A, V1: "dust every handle the night shift has worn" → "…the late shift has worn". After m08, "night shift" next to the mole hints could be heard as a Nightshade pun.
  - A, V2: "like hills you have taken, like warmth you have felt" → "like hills you have taken, like hands you've been dealt". The old line was filler, and a dealt hand suits a gambit.
  - A, Bridge: "\"Let it hurt afterwards, never during.\"" → "I've heard her advice in the voice in your ear;". The quoted line recurs in the m08–m10 songs and was misquoted ("never" for "not"). The new line is m07-specific (he has been listening on her channel), and it still sets up "who taught your teacher that art".
  - A, Bridge: "I'd your name by my lamp before the start" → "your name lay by my lamp before the start". "I'd" for "I had" trips when sung, and the new version scans to four stresses.
  - B, Pre-chorus 1: "One team, and only you to send" → "One team, and only yours to send". The old line read as if the agent were the one being sent.
  - B, Chorus: "I hand the board to you to play" → "I leave a light on while you play". This separates B's chorus from A's chess hook and ties into "Keep your lights on".
  - B, Chorus: "Go on and save the winter" → "Go on and warm the winter". "Save the winter" was a vague verb, and "warm" gives the heating-in-winter stakes.
  - B, V2: "There's a man upstairs who signed his name" → "There's a man inside who signed his name". Canon never puts the control room upstairs.
  - B, Bridge: "Let it hurt you in the morning / Not tonight, while the clock still lives" → "Let it hurt you when it's over / Not tonight, not while it ticks". This removes a doubled "morning" (the bridge also has "figures by the morning") and gives a tighter ribs/ticks rhyme.
  - Notes updated in both versions to match.
- **Stronger version:** B. Its voice is closest to the Architect in the ink, it makes every reference count, and it carries the mission's choice (the cities, the tablet, the figures by morning) better. A has the bigger hook.
- **Left for the user:** (1) Both choruses say outright that the agent was the target ("the prize was you", "the game was only ever you"). The Architect gives this away himself at T-1, so I kept it, but you may prefer it saved for the final chorus only. (2) B keeps a paraphrase of "Let it hurt afterwards" because m07 is where the Architect first uses HaX's line. If you want the line kept for the later missions' songs alone, B's bridge would need a new couplet. (3) Neither version was trimmed: each sits near 389 words with repeated choruses included and is under 380 without them.
