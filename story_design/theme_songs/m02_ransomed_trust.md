# Ransomed Trust

## Version A (classic)

[Verse 1]
Two forty-seven and the screens have all gone red,
the wards are breathing on a diesel heart instead.
You never heard me coming, darling, I was always near,
I've had my ear against your walls since the fire bell rang clear.
Seven letters on your table, seven times you looked away,
you bought yourselves a shining eye and let the old door stay.
So I've written you an invoice, signed it "yours sincerely, Ghost",
with a price for every hour, love, and the hours that cost the most.

[Pre-chorus]
Who holds the keys tonight?
Not the card upon your chest.
Every door is just a question
of whose hand you trusted best.

[Chorus]
Ransomed trust!
Seven warnings left to gather dust.
You can pay me in the dark or bleed out in the light,
but somebody held the door for me tonight.
Ransomed trust,
every lock you never turned has gone to rust.

[Verse 2]
You came in from the rain with your name upon the log
and a badge that opens nothing in the diesel and the fog.
You sweet-talked Bernadette for keys, you won a weary man's card,
then you turned my own back door on me and caught me off my guard.
Four seconds on your network and your handler didn't know,
I'll be here when you have swept me, there is nowhere I can't go.
I'll lay my keys before you, friend, as free as summer rain,
but my keys will turn my doors, love, and I'll walk back in again.

[Pre-chorus]
Who holds the keys tonight?
Not the one who signs the cheque.
Every hand that keeps you safe, love,
wears a lanyard round its neck.

[Chorus]
Ransomed trust!
Seven warnings left to gather dust.
You can pay me in the dark or bleed out in the light,
but somebody held the door for me tonight.
Ransomed trust,
every lock you never turned has gone to rust.

[Bridge]
So drag me to the dock and I will stand and tell it all,
I showed my working, line by line, in chalk upon the wall.
And the nine who signed it off by day, with minutes and a pen,
will watch me from the gallery and never stand again.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Ransomed trust!
Seven warnings left to gather dust.
Will you pay me in the dark or burn it in the light?
There's a kind face holding open doors tonight.
Ransomed trust,
ransomed trust,
every lock you never turned has gone to rust.

[Outro, spoken over the last chord]
Forty feet away, a machine breathes for her.
Count them, darling.

### Notes
- The opening reprises the mission's own facts: the 02:47 encryption, the 47 patients on generators, the listening device planted during a fire drill six weeks earlier (the "fire bell" line), Gary Whitlock's seven ignored emails, and the £3.2m MRI scanner approved while the £85k fix was deferred ("a shining eye", "let the old door stay").
- "signed it 'yours sincerely, Ghost'" and "the hours that cost the most" come from Ransomware Incorporated's businesslike ransom demand and from Ghost pricing the ransom against deaths per hour.
- Verse 2 follows the player's route: the rain on arrival, the visitor log, the paper badge that opens nothing (the access control server is encrypted), Bernadette's override key, Gary's server-room card, turning the ProFTPD backdoor on its owner ("my own back door", kept as imagery), Ghost's four-second demonstration ("your handler didn't notice"), Ghost staying in the network after the sweep, and the free keys with "my keys, my doors" in the small print.
- "pay me in the dark or bleed out in the light" covers the moral choice: pay quietly, refuse and restore by hand, or go public. The final chorus turns it into a question for the listener.
- Insider twist, hinted only: "somebody held the door for me", "whose hand you trusted best", "wears a lanyard round its neck" (Reeves's hand goes to his lanyard when he's unmasked) and "a kind face holding open doors" (the courteous man who held the doors during the drill). Nothing names him, his post or his job.
- The bridge is Ghost's line about standing in the dock while the "nine people who deferred it in writing" sit in the gallery, plus the briefing's "the patients are their chalkboard". The outro returns to Mrs Hargreaves on ECMO, "forty feet" from the agent. Ghost is never gendered, matching the ink.

## Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
Ten till six, I keep the quiet,
walk the rounds and hold the line,
every corridor a promise
that the building's sleeping fine.
A drill nobody scheduled,
two men walking through the hall.
I only held the door for them.
I only held it. That was all.

[Pre-chorus]
Then the screens went black at quarter to three,
and the wards went back to paper and pen,
and the nurses count the minutes
you would never count for them.

[Chorus]
So call it ransomed trust,
what you sold me cheap and long ago:
seven letters in a drawer,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never picked your locks,
I just stood where I was told to stand.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only switched the screen off in your hand.

[Verse 2]
Then you came in on a paper badge
and a booking signed in someone's name,
and someone struck it from the book
in a pen that wasn't quite the same.
Eleven words down a telephone,
no bruise, no bolt, no blade,
just a stranger with no standing
and the twenty minutes that it made.

[Pre-chorus]
And she stands at bed two like an alarm,
twenty years of watching in her hands,
while the board goes looking down the sofa
for a ghost's demands.

[Chorus]
So call it ransomed trust,
what you sold me cheap and long ago:
seven letters in a drawer,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never picked your locks,
I just stood where I was told to stand.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only switched the screen off in your hand.

[Bridge]
Say my number. Say it slow.
Watch the kindness leave me like a lamp.
There's a voice still on the line tonight,
the one thing in here still awake,
and it'll sell you back the morning
for the price of what you'd break.
Pay, and every board learns paying works.
Refuse, and do the long night by hand.
Either way you'll know what trust is worth
by the time the generators stand.

[Final chorus, lift]
So this is ransomed trust,
and the bill was written long ago:
seven letters, one unread,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never turned your key,
but I knew which drawer it slept in, and I'm here.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only held the door. I held it clear.

[Outro]
Mind how you go, now.
Somebody has to watch the door.
I'll be here.

### Notes
- Narrator is Graham Reeves, the plain-clothes night supervisor, singing a confession nobody has asked for yet. He is never named. "Ten till six" (his shift), "hold the line" (the comms relay, and a guard's job), "stood where I was told to stand" (his post) and the courteous outro are all true of him, but also fit Val, Bernie or any night staff until the badge and the post assignment are put together. Before that he sounds like any spy song's mole.
- "A drill nobody scheduled, / two men walking through the hall / I only held the door": the fire drill six weeks ago that facilities never called, the two men in high-vis Bernie and Val remember, and his "I held a door open. Be precise about that."
- Verse 2 is the burned cover: the paper badge, the booking struck through in a different pen on the visitor log, his "eleven words" to security control, no violence, and the twenty minutes it cost on a twelve-hour generator. "Someone" stays vague about who struck the book.
- "Seven letters in a drawer, the scanner bright, the servers low": Gary Whitlock's seven warnings and the board deferring £85k of server work for a £3.2m MRI. "Switched the screen off" borrows Ghost's line about the dead ward monitoring.
- Pre-chorus 2 and the bridge cover the ward and the choice: Sister Doyle at bed two ("twenty years of training, and tonight I'm an alarm"), Ghost's "down the back of a sofa" jibe, Ghost's voice as the one thing still awake, pay against the long manual restore, the generators running down. "Say my number" is the badge SC-4471 reveal, and "kindness leave me like a lamp" is the ink's "the courteous expression switches off, cleanly, like a lamp." Ghost is "it" (the voice), never "he" or "she".
- "I knew which drawer it slept in" hints at the forgotten offline keys without saying where they are.

## Review

- **Version A:** a proper belter with a sticky three-rhyme chorus (trust, dust, rust). Ghost's voice is right and the canon detail is dense and accurate. The second half of the insider hints ("lanyard", "kind face by the telephone") narrowed things to the man at the relay, and one line in Verse 2 didn't parse. Score 7 → 8.
- **Version B:** the stronger idea. The insider's grievance turns the title into a double meaning (the trust they bought cheap from him) and "I only held the door" hooks well. It gave Reeves away on first meeting (narration and dialogue quoted almost word for word), made Ghost male in the bridge and merged two separate drills. Score 6.5 → 8.5.
- **Changes made:**
  - A, Verse 2: "you won a weary man his card" → "you won a weary man's card" (the old line said the player gave Gary his card).
  - A, Verse 2: "I could stay for four more hours" → "I'll be here when you have swept me" (Ghost's canon threat is staying in the network after the sweep. "Four more hours" undersold it and muddled the four-hour board clock).
  - A, final chorus: "There's a kind face by the telephone tonight" → "There's a kind face holding open doors tonight" (the telephone pointed straight at the comms-relay post. The door image keeps the hint and echoes chorus 1).
  - A, second chorus: "[Chorus, repeat]" written out in full. Final chorus: "and the locks have gone to rust" → "every lock you never turned has gone to rust", restoring the full closing line that was cut to save words.
  - B, Verse 1: "hands clasped loosely by the line" → "walk the rounds and hold the line" (the old line was the narration from when you first meet Reeves, so it identified him on sight).
  - B, Verse 1: "Fire drill on a Friday" → "A drill nobody scheduled" (the two men came through during the drill six weeks ago. Friday's door-hold was the night of the attack. The new line is the ink's own phrase).
  - B, Pre-chorus 1: "ten to three" → "quarter to three" (02:47 is nearer quarter to).
  - B, Bridge: "and he'll sell you back the morning" → "and it'll sell you back the morning" (the ink never genders Ghost. "It" refers back to "a voice").
  - B, Outro: "Do bring the wards back, won't you. / People are relying on it." → "Mind how you go, now. / Somebody has to watch the door." (the old lines quoted Reeves's goodbye almost word for word, so a player would spot him the first time he spoke. The new lines keep his courtesy and the door motif. "I'll be here" stays because Bernie and Gary say it too, so it works as a red herring).
  - Notes in both versions updated to match.
- **Stronger version:** B. It sings from inside the twist, without stating it, and its chorus earns the title through the narrator's grievance. A is the better arena chorus but tells the story from outside.
- **Left for the user:**
  - B's "I only switched the screen off in your hand" gives Ghost's act to the insider. I kept it as complicity, but it could read as Reeves overclaiming.
  - B's "I knew which drawer it slept in" suggests Reeves knew where the offline keys were, which the ink doesn't say (and they're in a PIN safe, not a drawer).
  - A's "fog" is invented weather. Canon only has rain, which is harmless.
  - Word counts with the choruses counted once: A 378 (right at the limit, because its verses run long), B 349.

### Cross-season pass

- A, V2: "I'll lay my keys before you, darling, free as summer rain," → "I'll lay my keys before you, friend, as free as summer rain,". This takes A from three uses of "darling" to two, and Ghost's businesslike "friend" goes with the invoice.
- Noted, not changed: "held the door" is this mission's own twist (both versions) and comes back in m08 A's chorus ("He'll hold the door"). I left m08 alone. The echo between the two insiders can be heard as deliberate.

## Version C (agent)

[Verse 1]
Rain on the car park, red behind the glass,
a generator beating out the time.
They gave me a card with my name in pen
and not one reader left to call it mine.
The building lost its memory in a minute,
every name behind the same black screen,
so I walk your halls on borrowed say-so,
smiling like a face you've seen.

[Pre-chorus]
HaX in my ear says a pick is a confession,
the people are the lock,
so I ask, and I wait, and I thank the helpful ones,
and I trust whoever answers when I knock.

[Chorus]
Ransomed trust,
the only key I'm carrying tonight:
a name in someone's book
that's only good while someone's kind.
I won't pick you, I can't buy you,
all I've got's a name and half a lie.
Ransomed trust,
lend it me, and I'll give you back your eyes.

[Verse 2]
On the ward the screens have gone to biro,
every hour initialled, every hour kept,
and a man in bed four asks if I'm the one,
and I say yes, and hope my hands can count his breath.
Then a tired man lets go his one live card
from a squared-up stack of being right.
He's waited half a year for someone to agree.
I fold it in my pocket with the night.

[Pre-chorus]
There's a cursor in the dark that talks like a ledger,
says it's easier to hate a thing that blinks.
HaX says keep it out of my head.
It's in. It's sums I've seen before. It asks me what I think.

[Chorus]
Ransomed trust,
the only key I'm carrying tonight:
a name in someone's book
that's only good while someone's kind.
I won't pick you, I can't buy you,
all I've got's a name and half a lie.
Ransomed trust,
lend it me, and I'll give you back your eyes.

[Bridge]
Then the cursor wears a hood,
a shape against the light,
holds the keys out, free of charge,
if I'll put the board in print tonight.
Pay, and another ward goes red.
Refuse, and watch the counters crawl.
Take it free, and never know
whose doors I've opened after all.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Ransomed trust,
the only key I'm carrying tonight:
a name in someone's book
that's only good while someone's kind.
If somebody signs their name for mine,
I won't make that kindness lie.
Ransomed trust,
lend it me, and I'll give you back your eyes,
lend it me, and I'll give you back your eyes.

[Outro]
Rain on the car park.
The badge gone soft as tissue on its clip.
I owe them all tonight.
I owe them all.

### Notes
- Tradition: a blend. Verses and pre-choruses are modern and close-up, sung inside the agent's head at a slow 6/8; the final chorus lifts a key for a classic belt. Season position: the second field operation, keen but tested for the first time. Ghost's body-count arithmetic is "sums I've seen before" (Derek's casualty projections from m01, which the briefing ties to this cell).
- Angle: with the access control server encrypted, the agent's only credential is other people's belief: the paper badge Dr Kim signs ("a card with my name in pen"), the visitor log Bernie keeps ("a name in someone's book"), and a cover story that is "half a lie". "Only good while someone's kind" is HaX's warning that one of the people you're rude to will have to vouch for you. "Give you back your eyes" is the monitoring the agent is trying to restore (Mrs Hargreaves: "can't see me screen").
- Game and story references: rain and red emergency lighting on arrival, the generator, every reader dead, HaX's "it's a confession if anyone sees you" and "this is a mission about people", the biro obs initialled on the ward, Mr Pryce in bed four ("you the one") and the hand bag clipped to his bed, Gary's one working card and his printed stack of emails, Ghost as the cursor that is "easier to despise", the hooded video, and the three-way choice (pay, refuse and restore by hand while the counters climb, or Ghost's free keys in exchange for publishing).
- Branches sung as hopes, so every route fits: saving Mr Pryce ("hope my hands can count his breath"), Bernie vouching ("If somebody signs their name for mine"; Bernie and Dr Kim both sign for the agent, and the insider never signs anything), and Gary's card, which "lets go" on every route without the blame-route skid.
- Dramatic irony the agent can't see: "I thank the helpful ones / and I trust whoever answers", "whose doors I've opened after all" (Ghost's keys, Ghost's doors) and the outro "I thanked them all", which a player hears as including him. Nothing names the insider, his post, the telephone or the badge.
- Ghost is "it" and "the cursor". The agent has no name, pronouns or body detail. About 325 words with each chorus counted once.

## Review (C)

- **Version C:** a clear third song. The title read from the agent's side (trust as the only key you carry, lent by strangers) is new, and "lend it me, and I'll give you back your eyes" is the best closing hook of the three. The voice and the ungendered, nameless agent hold throughout, and the insider stays hidden. It sang three optional branches as fact (bagging Mr Pryce, Gary's blame-route skid and quote, Bernie's vouch), and two images came too close to B's: the nurse standing in for the monitor, and the pay/refuse "by hand" bridge couplet. Its chorus "strike" line also repeated B's struck-booking image. Score 7.5 → 8.5.
- **Changes made:**
  - Chorus (all three): "that any passing pen could strike" → "that's only good while someone's kind" (B's Verse 2 already sings the booking struck through in a different pen. The new line is HaX's "you'll need one of them to vouch for you" and sets up the final chorus's "kindness").
  - Verse 2: "On the ward a nurse is standing in for a screen, / her biro keeps the watch the wires kept" → "On the ward the screens have gone to biro, / every hour initialled, every hour kept" (B's "she stands at bed two like an alarm" is the same image. The new lines use the initialled biro obs on Mr Pryce's chart and drop the gendered nurse).
  - Verse 2: "and I say yes, and squeeze, and count his breath" → "and I say yes, and hope my hands can count his breath" (bagging him is optional and can fail. Sung as a hope it fits every route).
  - Verse 2: "Then a tired man skids his one live card / across a squared-up stack of being right: / 'When they blame me, you'll have known.'" → "Then a tired man lets go his one live card / from a squared-up stack of being right. / He's waited half a year for someone to agree." (the skid and the quote happen only if you blame Gary, the route HaX warns against. The card changes hands on every route, and the new line is his six months of being ignored.)
  - Bridge: "Pay, and the next ward pays. / Say no, and count the hours by hand. / Take it free, and never know / whose doors I've opened, or where I stand." → "Pay, and another ward goes red. / Refuse, and watch the counters crawl. / Take it free, and never know / whose doors I've opened after all." (B's bridge has "Pay, and every board learns paying works. / Refuse, and do the long night by hand". The new couplet uses the red floor plan from the briefing and Ghost's watched restore counters, and "after all" carries the my-keys-my-doors threat.)
  - Final chorus: "Somebody signed their name for mine" → "If somebody signs their name for mine" (Bernie's vouch is optional, so it is sung as a hope. "Signs" fits Bernie's log and Dr Kim's badge and not the insider, who never signed the book).
  - Notes rewritten to match.
- **Stronger version:** B stays the strongest of the three for its twist-from-inside. C is the best fit for the player and the most singable chorus after A's.
- **Left for the user:**
  - Outro "I thanked them all" is past tense, so a rude or violent run makes it untrue. I kept it because a player hears the insider in "all". "I meant to thank them all" would make it true on every route but loses the sting.
  - "I won't pick you" is a resolve, and plenty of runs do pick locks. I read it as the agent's intention, in line with HaX's advice.
  - "not one reader left to call it mine" ignores the one reader that still works (Gary's, which takes only his card). This seemed fine as imagery.

### Cross-season pass (C)

- Outro: "The badge gone soft as tissue in my hand. / I thanked them all tonight. / I thanked them all." → "The badge gone soft as tissue on its clip. / I owe them all tonight. / I owe them all." **Every outcome.** "I thanked them" was untrue on a rude or violent run. Every run relies on the staff who vouch, so "owe" holds on all of them. A player still hears the insider in "all", and the debt suits a ransom song. "On its clip" thins hands, which appear in all ten C songs (m10 keeps them as its hook).
- Noted, not changed: "HaX in my ear" was in m02–m05 C. m02 keeps it as the first, and m03, m04 and m05 were varied. The bridge's pay/refuse/take-it-free couplet is the same choice device A and B use. It was already reworked in review and sings consequences rather than naming the endings, so I left it.
