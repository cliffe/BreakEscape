# Critical Failure

## Version A (classic)

[Verse 1]
You came through the gate with a contractor's grace
And an order number nobody could trace.
You were the man who kept a city alight;
Now you sit at my bench and you cost out the night.

[Pre-chorus]
The screen swears I'm cool at a calm twenty-eight,
But the old iron dial in the dark knows my fate:
Sixty-one, and climbing, love,
Sixty-one, and climbing...

[Chorus]
Critical failure,
Oh, the heat of your hand,
You've a whole sleeping city at your command.
You've counted us down to one decimal place,
Critical failure, and it's there in your face.

[Verse 2]
Now a stranger comes calling four hours too soon
With a badge for the gate and a lie for the room,
Takes a card's quiet echo across a man's desk,
Takes a print from a panel, and the door does the rest.
The hydrogen glows amber down the long rack row,
And a voice on the line whispers, "Carefully. Go."

[Pre-chorus]
You'd set it for eight, you were cool, you were sure,
But a klaxon is singing them out the fire door,
Nine in hi-vis, and your clock's running, love,
Nine in hi-vis, and running...

[Chorus]
Critical failure,
Oh, the heat of your hand,
One keystroke from ruin, and the ruin was planned.
You signed for the number, you know it by heart,
Critical failure, it was there from the start.

[Bridge]
There's a heart on the wall that you never could own,
Red as a warning, and wired alone.
"The button, or me. And you'd better be quick.
You don't get to have both." And the seconds still tick.
Lift the flap, or go for him, you can't have the two,
While the dock door stands open and the night's coming through.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Critical failure,
Take the heat from my hand,
Find the red on the wall while the last racks stand.
They'll call it an accident come morning light,
But critical failure...
Not tonight.

[Outro]
Two hundred and forty thousand
Sleep on through the night,
And never know whose hand
Left on their light.

### Notes
- The narrator is the Albion Energy Storage plant, singing to Voltage as a lover he has turned on. He is the ex-grid engineer who "kept a city alight", and the title doubles as a love affair breaking down and a grid failing. "It was there from the start" echoes his line that ENTROPY "didn't make it fragile", they "just stopped pretending it wasn't".
- Verse 1 and pre-chorus 1: Critical Mass's OptiGrid cover and the order number Vance never found; the faked SCADA reading (28°C on the screen) against the mechanical dial (61°C). The song says a dial exists but not where it hangs.
- Chorus and verse 2: Voltage's casualty model ("I know it to one decimal place", "I countersigned"), the one-keystroke trigger, and the player's regulator cover arriving four hours early, the prox card copied across Vance's desk, the print that opens the HV door, the amber hydrogen panel, and Agent HaX on the phone ("Go carefully").
- Pre-chorus 2 hints at a consequence without spelling it out: sounding the evacuation gets the nine night crew out of Hall 2 through the fire doors, and "your clock's running" suggests it also starts Voltage's clock early.
- Bridge: the plant-room choice, "The button, or me... You don't get to have both", and the hardwired red mushroom-head ESD under its hinged flap, "wired alone" off the network. The open dock door hints that he gets away if you go for the button.
- Final chorus and outro: the debrief choice between disclosure and calling it an accident ("Nobody gets to call it an accident"), "the last racks" (Bank B can go, A and C holding), and the 240,000 on the feed who never knew.

## Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
I used to keep this city breathing,
I knew the hum of every line.
I sent my warnings up the ladder;
they stamped them "later", every time.
So I came back in borrowed colours,
a firm that never sent a bill,
three nights we waited on the floodplain
where the racks lie warm and still.

[Pre-chorus]
The screen reads twenty-eight and smiles,
I taught it how to lie.
But the needle on the far wall's counting,
and so am I.

[Chorus]
This is a critical failure,
a slow heat under the skin,
one cell whispers to the next one
till the whole hall lets it in.
You built it on hope and gaffer tape,
I only came to show the seam.
Call it a critical failure.
Call it waking from the dream.

[Verse 2]
Now you come in on a stranger's badge,
four hours early through the frost.
You borrow a card from a tired man's lanyard,
a print from a panel he touched and forgot.
Nine in hi-vis on my cameras
walking out into the cold.
How humane. So you've done your sums too.
Let's see whose sums hold.

[Pre-chorus]
The phone on your belt says hurry,
a voice that's never been afraid.
I know the number to one decimal place,
and that's the reason that I came.

[Chorus]
This is a critical failure,
a slow heat under the skin,
one cell whispers to the next one
till the whole hall lets it in.
You built it on hope and gaffer tape,
I only came to show the seam.
Call it a critical failure.
Call it waking from the dream.

[Bridge]
There's a red head on the wall behind me,
wired straight, no road to it but hands,
and here's my hand above the keyboard.
You can't reach both. You understand.
So choose: the button, or me.
Save your eleven, lose the thread,
or take me in and learn the cost
of every name I've left unsaid.

[Final chorus, lift]
Maybe I'm the critical failure,
the one cell that ran too hot,
the warning nobody answered
grown into the thing it fought.
Pull me out, or pull the power,
either way the lights grow dim.
Call it a critical failure.
Call it eight o'clock again.

[Outro]
The needle's climbing.
Ja.
I know.

### Notes
- Sung from inside Voltage's head: the former grid engineer whose ignored warnings ("stamped them later") turned him, matching his ink line "We didn't make it fragile. We just stopped pretending it wasn't." "Borrowed colours, a firm that never sent a bill" is the OptiGrid cover; "three nights on the floodplain" is his "shed on a floodplain" and "we've had three days in it".
- Pre-chorus 1 is the mission's core puzzle as imagery: the SCADA screen reading 28 while an analogue needle tells the truth. "I taught it how to lie" nods to the faked temperature sensors. Pre-chorus 2 quotes his casualty-model line ("I know it to one decimal place", "I came because of it") and puts Agent HaX on the phone.
- Chorus: "one cell whispers to the next" is thermal runaway rack to rack, with "cell" also meaning the ENTROPY cell. "Hope and gaffer tape" is Vance's phrase from the debrief, turned against him by the villain.
- Verse 2 touches the player's route in passing: the regulator cover arriving four hours early, the cloner on Vance's lanyard, the print off the panel he logs his rounds on, and the optional evacuation (Voltage's "Nine people in hi-vis on my cameras... Very humane").
- The bridge foreshadows the plant-room choice: the hardwired red mushroom ESD against Voltage at the trigger laptop ("The button, or me"). "Save your eleven, lose the thread" echoes "You save your eleven and your forty" and hints that pressing the button lets him walk, taking the route to The Architect with him.
- The final chorus turns the title on the narrator: the critical failure might be Voltage himself. The trailing "Ja" fits his Johannesburg voice and his one verbal tic in the ink.

## Review

- **Version A:** brassy and well built. The plant-as-jilted-lover conceit carries the title's double meaning, and the bridge and outro land hard. It had a few canon slips and leant toward a route list in verse 2. Score 7/10 → 8/10.
- **Version B:** the stronger voice. Voltage sounds like his ink (flat, costed, "Ja"), and the "cell" double meaning in the chorus is the best line in either song. Its slips were small. Score 8/10 → 9/10.
- **Extra checks:**
  - **28 and 61 (A):** neither number is a code the player types. They are a reading the game hands over, and the mission description already says to check the telemetry against a physical instrument, so the numbers give a player something to recognise without spoiling anything. What made it read like a walkthrough was the location ("the old iron dial by the racks") plus "the dial does the rest" in verse 2. Both are changed.
  - **"Hope and gaffer tape" (B):** no spoiler. The line carries no plot information, and the underfunded plant is set up early in the mission (Vance: "short-staffed, short of money"; Voltage: "budget cuts, ageing cells"). Players who reach the debrief get a nice echo.
- **Changes made:**
  - A, pre-chorus 1: "But the old iron dial by the racks knows my fate" → "But the old iron dial in the dark knows my fate". This drops the pointer to where the dial hangs.
  - A, verse 2: "Takes a print from the panel, and the dial does the rest" → "Takes a print from a panel, and the door does the rest". The dial opens nothing; the print opens the HV door. It also stops the verse reading as three steps in order.
  - A, pre-chorus 2: "You told them eight o'clock" → "You'd set it for eight". Voltage never told anyone the time. SAFETYNET got 0800 from intercepted traffic.
  - A, final chorus: "Find the red in the dark where the cold racks stand" → "Find the red on the wall while the last racks stand". The ESD is on the plant-room wall, not among the racks, and "cold racks" fought the song's heat imagery. "Last racks" nods to Bank B going while A and C hold.
  - B, verse 1: "three nights alone out on the floodplain" → "three nights we waited on the floodplain". Voltage had Cipher, Relay and Static with him ("we've had three days in it").
  - B, verse 2: "an old man's lanyard" → "a tired man's lanyard". Nothing in canon gives Vance's age; he is short-staffed and overworked.
  - B, verse 2: "Let's see what your sums hold" → "Let's see whose sums hold". This tightens the scansion and sets his casualty model against the player's.
  - B, pre-chorus 2: "and I came here anyway" → "and that's the reason that I came". The ink line is "I came because of it", which is colder and truer to him. "Afraid / came" is a slant rhyme.
  - Chorus repeats: none had been cut, so none needed restoring.
- **Stronger version:** B. Its narrator is plainly Voltage, and its chorus sticks after one hearing.
- **Left for the user:**
  - B's backstory (warnings sent "up the ladder" and "stamped later") is invented. It fits "former grid engineer" and doesn't contradict canon, but no source states it.
  - Both verse 2s still name the card clone and the print lift in passing. I judged these catchable references rather than instructions. Cut further if you want the songs to give away less before the mission.

### Cross-season pass

- A, V2: "Takes a card off a lanyard across a man's desk," → "Takes a card's quiet echo across a man's desk,". Both m04 versions had the same card-off-a-lanyard line, and lanyards recur all season. "Echo" is the clone as an image.
- B, Pre-chorus 2: "The phone in your pocket says hurry," → "The phone on your belt says hurry,". "In your pocket" for HaX is already in both m01 songs.
