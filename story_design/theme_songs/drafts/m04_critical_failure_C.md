# Critical Failure: Version C (agent)

[Verse 1]
Frost on the wire and a badge that I borrowed,
a regulator nobody's expecting till day.
The halls hum low on the edge of the floodplain,
a city's whole winter stacked up in the grey.
I've picked my locks, I've copied cards,
I've talked my way past a desk or two,
but tonight, says the briefing, there's more of them
than I've ever walked through.

[Pre-chorus]
Every screen in the building sings one number,
and a room that agrees has been told what to do.
So I'll go by my hands and one honest old gauge,
and the people who taught me what's true.

[Chorus]
Critical failure,
the part that gives under load.
Tonight a whole city is leaning
on the thinnest wire it owns,
and I'm scared the thinnest wire is me,
out here on my own.

[Verse 2]
Vance says "aye" and the gate swings open
on one tired man and a short-handed shift.
The cloner purrs once at my hip, and it's over:
his card's in my pocket. He never feels the lift.
There's a switch on his desk that would empty the halls,
and the man at the bench would hear it ring too.
Do I spend my surprise on a crew I won't meet?
That's arithmetic nobody taught me to do.

[Pre-chorus]
HaX in my ear keeps the orders plain:
lives first, and the answers can wait.
I said it back like I believed it.
Tonight's when I learn what it weighs.

[Chorus]
Critical failure,
the part that gives under load.
Tonight a whole city is leaning
on the thinnest wire it owns,
and I'm scared the thinnest wire is me,
out here on my own.

[Bridge]
He doesn't look up. Says he'd budgeted for more,
like I'm a line on a sheet he signed before.
Take him, and maybe the name over him slips;
reach the wall, and the long halls cool in the dark.
Lives first. I said it, by heart.
Now let my hands mean what I said with my lips.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Critical failure,
the part that gives under load.
So let the whole city lean on me,
the thinnest wire it owns.
I'll hold till the morning comes,
out here on my own.

[Outro]
I send home the reading I took by hand
to the one who swore the physics won't lie.
And neither would he.

## Notes
- Tradition: a blend. The verses and pre-choruses are modern and inward (*Skyfall*, *No Time to Die*), and the chorus is built to be belted classic-style, with a key change at the end. Agent 0x00 sings every line, nameless and ungendered, four missions in: past the keen first days, facing a bigger fight than before ("More of them than you've faced before") and a choice that costs lives either way.
- Title: the chorus takes "critical failure" in its engineering sense, the single part that gives under load, and the agent fears being that part ("the thinnest wire it owns"). The final chorus turns fear into resolve. This keeps clear of A's failing love affair and B's Voltage-as-failure.
- Mission references: the regulator cover arriving early, the floodplain site (Voltage's "shed on a floodplain") and "it's winter, and this is the site"; the falsified SCADA telemetry ("a room that agrees has been told what to do") checked against a physical gauge; Vance's "aye" and his short-staffed shift; the cloner buzzing at the hip ("didn't even have to work for it"), with "lift" doubling as pickpocketing and the lifted print; the evacuation switch on Vance's desk, which Voltage hears too; HaX's "Lives first. Intelligence second"; Voltage's opening line "One of you. I'd budgeted for more" and his costed casualty sheet; the ESD on the wall against Voltage, and "the name over him" for Blackout and The Architect.
- Dramatic irony: "the people who taught me what's true" and the outro's "the one who swore the physics won't lie... And neither would he" come from Nightshade's briefing line, "The physics doesn't lie and neither do I", and the reading the agent sends him. To the agent this is trust in a colleague. After m08 ("Every word I taught you was true") it reads differently. No name, so nothing is given away.
- Foreshadowing within m04: "the man at the bench would hear it ring too" hints that the alarm starts Voltage's clock early, and the bridge's "take him... / reach the wall" sets up the plant-room choice without saying that going for the button lets him walk.
- Avoided A and B's hooks and images: no 28/61 figures, dial or needle, "one decimal place", "the button, or me", cells spreading rack to rack, "gaffer tape", klaxon, hi-vis, lanyard, dock door or the 240,000. No season-wide repeats (clock on the wall, "darling", flags as candles, "let it hurt afterwards").
