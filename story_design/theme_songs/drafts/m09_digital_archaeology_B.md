# Digital Archaeology: Version B (modern)

*Slow 6/8. Piano and a low cello line, a ticking that turns out to be a shredder's motor. Sung by the Architect, close to the mic, to the agent he has been reading.*

[Verse 1]
Sodium light on a grain-store wall,
one name painted over another's scrawl.
You hear the teeth before you find the door,
a shredder eating paper, drawer by drawer.
Behind the mesh she feeds it, calm and small;
people are records, and she keeps them all.

[Pre-chorus]
Bring your picks and a borrowed print,
and the gift a friend pressed into your hand.
Didn't it open too easily?
Didn't it fit like it was planned?

[Chorus]
So dig me up, dig me up,
brush the dust from every lie I told;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
where only you would go.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
is the one that tells you where I'll be.

[Verse 2]
There's a file in the stacks with your number on,
I kept it like a diary while you were gone.
Every door you forced and every hand you stayed,
I read you like the weather, and I wasn't afraid.
There's a wren up in the rafters, singing low,
small enough to love and close enough to know.

[Pre-chorus]
A voice on the line has a name to sell
for a reel she'd rather see erased.
Save one, lose one, the spools are turning;
didn't it feel like it was placed?

[Chorus]
So dig me up, dig me up,
brush the dust from every lie I told;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
where only you would go.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
is the one that tells you where I'll be.

[Bridge]
Nine years back I wrote a letter, never sent.
They stamped it *keep*, and that is where the warning went,
where all the warnings go.
And they never asked me for my key;
my name still turns in their own locks for me.
Ask the one who sat in my front row
what "let it hurt afterwards" means; I taught her so.
Tell her I'll call. Tell her you'll come.
Six in the morning, and the sum is done.

[Final chorus, up a tone]
So dig me up, dig me up,
turn the last page, where I wrote your name;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
and you came, you came.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
brings you down the road to me.

[Outro]
Sodium light. The shredder stops.
Your hand, my key.

## Notes

- Setting and purge: the sodium light, the grain warehouse with one name stencilled over an older one, and the shredder heard before the door all come from Act 1's loading dock. "Behind the mesh she feeds it" and "people are records, and she keeps them all" are Sorensen in the restricted cage, almost word for word her own line.
- Game kit: "picks and a borrowed print" is the lockpicks and the print lifted off Sorensen's stamp. "The gift a friend pressed into your hand" is Kowalczyk's PIN cracker, and the "too easily / like it was planned" pre-chorus hints that both the gift and the open doors were meant to happen. "Save one, lose one, the spools are turning" is the tape-deck choice at the end of the Recruiter's call, and "a voice on the line has a name to sell" is her offer.
- WREN: "a wren up in the rafters... small enough to love and close enough to know" points at a plant on the raid team, someone likeable and close by, without naming anyone. Only a player who has finished m10 will connect it to the cracker in the first pre-chorus.
- The main twist (the purge order spares the Tomb Beta server on purpose because he wants to be found and wants them to come) is the chorus hook: "they burn the rest, but the one I left alone / is the one that tells you where I'll be." "Six in the morning" is the Publication's hour.
- The identity proof and the kept flaw: "my name still turns in their own locks" is the staff credential nobody revoked. "A letter, never sent... stamped it *keep*" is the Albion memo, the vendor notice he never sent and the "retain: operational value" stamp. "The one who sat in my front row" is HaX ("same classroom"), and "Tell her I'll call" foreshadows his call to her at the close.
- The Reading: Verse 2's "file in the stacks with your number on" and the final chorus's "turn the last page, where I wrote your name" are the Architect's file on 0x00. "Your hand, my key" hints at its last-page prediction (that 0x00 presses the Publication's key) without saying so.
