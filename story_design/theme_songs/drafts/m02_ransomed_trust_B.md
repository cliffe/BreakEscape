# Ransomed Trust: Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
Ten till six, I keep the quiet,
hands clasped loosely by the line,
every corridor a promise
that the building's sleeping fine.
Fire drill on a Friday,
two men walking through the hall.
I only held the door for them.
I only held it. That was all.

[Pre-chorus]
Then the screens went black at ten to three,
and the wards went back to paper and pen,
and the nurses count the minutes
you would never count for them.

[Chorus]
So call it ransomed trust,
what you sold me cheap and long ago:
seven letters in a drawer,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never picked your locks,
I just stood where I was told to stand.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only switched the screen off in your hand.

[Verse 2]
Then you came in on a paper badge
and a booking signed in someone's name,
and someone struck it from the book
in a pen that wasn't quite the same.
Eleven words down a telephone,
no bruise, no bolt, no blade,
just a stranger with no standing
and the twenty minutes that it made.

[Pre-chorus]
And she stands at bed two like an alarm,
twenty years of watching in her hands,
while the board goes looking down the sofa
for a ghost's demands.

[Chorus]
So call it ransomed trust,
what you sold me cheap and long ago:
seven letters in a drawer,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never picked your locks,
I just stood where I was told to stand.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only switched the screen off in your hand.

[Bridge]
Say my number. Say it slow.
Watch the kindness leave me like a lamp.
There's a voice still on the line tonight,
the one thing in here still awake,
and he'll sell you back the morning
for the price of what you'd break.
Pay, and every board learns paying works.
Refuse, and do the long night by hand.
Either way you'll know what trust is worth
by the time the generators stand.

[Final chorus, lift]
So this is ransomed trust,
and the bill was written long ago:
seven letters, one unread,
the scanner bright, the servers low.
I never turned your key,
but I knew which drawer it slept in, and I'm here.
You built the dark yourselves.
I only held the door. I held it clear.

[Outro]
Do bring the wards back, won't you.
People are relying on it.
I'll be here.

## Notes
- Narrator is Graham Reeves, the plain-clothes night supervisor, sung as a confession nobody has asked for yet. He is never named. "Ten till six", "hands clasped loosely by the line" (the comms relay), "stood where I was told to stand" and the outro, which reworks his "Do get those wards back, won't you" and "I'll be here", all point at the polite man in the boardroom. A player hears the twist only once they've met him. Before that he sounds like a spy song's mole.
- "Fire drill on a Friday, two men walking through the hall / I only held the door": how ENTROPY got in, taken from his "I held a door open. Be precise about that."
- Verse 2 is the burned cover. The paper badge, the booking struck through in a different pen on the visitor log, his "eleven words" call to security control, "no violence" and the twenty minutes it cost. "Someone" is left vague about who struck the book out.
- "Seven letters in a drawer, the scanner bright, the servers low": Gary Whitlock's seven ignored emails and the board deferring £85k of server work for a £3.2m scanner. "Switched the screen off" is Ghost's line about the dead ward monitoring.
- Pre-chorus 2 and the bridge cover the ward and the moral choice. Sister Doyle at bed two ("tonight I'm an alarm"), Ghost's "down the back of a sofa" jibe, Ghost as the one thing in the building still awake, pay versus the long manual restore, the twelve-hour generators. "Say my number" is the badge SC-4471 reveal, and "kindness leave me like a lamp" is the ink's "courteous expression switches off, cleanly, like a lamp."
- "I knew which drawer it slept in" hints at the forgotten offline keys without saying where they are.
