# First Contact: Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
You came in with the morning crowd,
a lanyard and a borrowed name,
the auditor that no one saw.
I saw you all the same.
I watched you kneel beside the locks
and ease the pins in one by one;
you listen like a lover would.
I listened too, when I was young.

[Pre-chorus]
Every door here has a number,
every number has a hiding place:
a voicemail on a desk phone,
a safe behind a smiling face.
Go on and find them. I don't mind.
I wrote *verify everything*
and buried it among the lies.

[Chorus]
This is first contact,
a message in the dark,
a voice you've never heard before
that knows just where to start.
It says the hospital is closed,
it tells you where to run,
and you send it to everyone you love
before the night is done.

[Verse 2]
I did the sums by lamplight,
forty-two to eighty-five.
I rounded it to sixty
and I've never felt so alive.
The last one who looked closely
was walked out before noon;
her briefcase still sits waiting
in an empty corner room.
There's a tired man behind a keypad
with my sins beneath his name,
and someone whispering through the walls
who thinks I've never heard her name.

[Pre-chorus]
Plant your little flags, my darling,
on the hill I made.
Your handler's in your pocket.
Mine is further down the line.

[Chorus]
This is first contact,
a message in the dark,
a voice you've never heard before
that knows just where to start,
and you send it to everyone you love
before the night is done.

[Bridge]
So here we are, the lights turned low,
one code between my teeth.
You can take me in, or take the stage,
or take me underneath.
Show the world the numbers.
Let them read what I designed.
Trust is the one lock left unbolted,
and it can't be patched in time.

[Final chorus]
This is first contact,
your first night in the field,
and I'm the first you've met, my love.
I won't be the last revealed.
There's an architect who drew this room
before you learned to pick,
and every cell's another window
when the glass begins to crack.
This is first contact.

[Outro]
Verify everything.
Trust no one.
Not the voice on the phone.
Not this song.

## Notes
- The narrator is Derek Lawson, who tells the agent at the confrontation "I've been watching you": the lockpicking, the server access, the IT-contractor cover with its visitor badge ("lanyard and a borrowed name").
- Pre-chorus 1 points at the game's number trail: the IT room PIN left in a voicemail on Sarah's reception phone, PIN pads, safes. "Verify everything" is one of the entries in Derek's own password list and also his creed. Pre-chorus 2 brings in the VM flags submitted at the drop-site terminal ("plant your little flags") and Agent HaX on the phone, with The Architect as Derek's own handler further up the line.
- The chorus is Operation Shatter told as imagery: fake emergency alerts (hospital closures), forwarded without checking, going out at six on Sunday morning. "First contact" means both the agent's first field mission and the first message a victim trusts.
- Verse 2 covers the 42–85 casualty projection Derek rounded to sixty, and Patricia Wells, fired and escorted out with her briefcase left in the vacant manager's office. "A tired man behind a keypad / with my sins beneath his name" hints at the frame-up of Kevin (IT room keypad, reports filed in his name) without saying it. "Somebody is whispering through the walls" is dramatic irony about Maya, the informant Derek never quite spots.
- The bridge is the confrontation with the launch device and a code he has memorised. "Take me in, or take the stage, / or take me underneath" lines up with the arrest, public exposure and fight endings. "Trust... can't be patched" is lifted from The Architect's letter.
- The final chorus foreshadows the season ("I won't be the last", "other cells", "The Architect remains at large"). The outro turns "trust no one" round on the song itself.
