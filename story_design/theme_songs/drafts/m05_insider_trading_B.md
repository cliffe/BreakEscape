# Insider Trading: Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
I've got a badge that opens every door,
a face the cameras have filed before.
Nobody checks a man who's always here;
I hang my coat and disappear.
One of eight behind the lab-wing glass,
I keep my head down, let the questions pass,
and when the floor goes dark at eight
the vault reads my thumb and lifts the gate.

[Pre-chorus]
She rang me from a firm with a smiling name,
said every man has a price, and no two the same.
She only asked me what I'd take in trade.
I said a heartbeat. And the price was paid.

[Chorus]
This is insider trading,
one life bought with forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep her alive.
Nobody heard the bargain,
nobody saw me sign.
The sirens will be late tonight
and every minute's mine.

[Verse 2]
I keep a journal in a briefcase, locked:
"the greater good", "collateral", "the cost".
I read it back at night when the children sleep.
A stranger wrote it. The words are mine to keep.
My wife thinks the money's a bonus, a good year,
a good man in a hard one, still here.

[Pre-chorus]
She swore I was the only one she'd call,
but I can hear the other phones ring down the hall.
Everyone has a price, she likes to say.
She never mentions who it is that pays.

[Chorus]
This is insider trading,
one life bought with forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep her alive.
Nobody heard the bargain,
nobody saw me sign.
The sirens will be late tonight
and every minute's mine.

[Bridge]
I know you're there. I won't turn round.
The vault let two of us in tonight; I heard the sound.
Walk me out in cuffs before the bar is full,
or send me back with a second string to pull.
When your phone lights up with that smiling name,
she'll ask your price. Then tell me we're not the same.

[Final chorus, up a tone]
This is insider trading,
one life weighed against forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep the one I love alive.
So take me, or turn me, stranger,
before the window opens wide.
The sirens will be late tonight
unless you trade me back inside.

[Outro]
Ninety-nine per cent.
She thinks I'm a good man.
I used to know.

## Notes
- Sung from inside the insider's head, unnamed throughout, so the song never names Torres. The one-of-eight suspect pool, the badge that never needs to break in ("We don't break in. We're already inside"), the CCTV, the badge-reader hours and the vault fingerprint all come from the mission's evidence trail and Nightshade's briefing.
- "A firm with a smiling name" / "every man has a price" / "what I'd take in trade" is the Recruiter and TalentStack, using her own line. "She swore I was the only one" and the other phones ringing hint at the forty-seven names in her pipeline.
- "The keys to the ambulance" and "the sirens will be late tonight" cover Project Heisenberg: the dispatch network's key material and the slower 999 response. "Forty-five" is the top of the casualty projection, and "the window" is the key-rotation window.
- Verse 2 draws on Torres' journal in the locked briefcase ("collateral damage for the greater good", "I don't recognise the man who wrote it"), and the wife who "thinks the money's a bonus".
- The bridge is the data-centre confrontation, with the player as the "security consultant" Torres knows is no auditor: "the vault logged me in twice tonight", the transfer bar, the arrest-or-turn choice ("a second string to pull", "trade me back inside" for the double-agent route).
- The hidden twist: the Recruiter will ring the agent with an offer of her own (forget the envelope, keep a clean file), so the bridge ends by warning the listener that they're the next one she'll put a price on.
