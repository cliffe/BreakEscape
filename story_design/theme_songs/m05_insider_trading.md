# Insider Trading

## Version A (classic)

[Verse 1]
Rain on the glass of a Cambridge park,
a name on a keycard that works past dark.
The doors all open at the touch of your hand,
you wear that pass like a wedding band.
I found you pale in a waiting room,
with a bill on your knee and a cure due soon.

[Pre-chorus]
I never broke a window, never picked a lock,
I only asked the time, and then I bought the clock.

[Chorus]
Insider trading,
you sell what you're trusted to keep,
the keys to the city while the city's asleep.
Insider trading,
everyone has a price.
I pay you in cash, and they pay in minutes,
and minutes, my darling, are lives.

[Verse 2]
Now there's a stranger with an auditor's case,
a borrowed badge and a patient face,
who lifted your thumb from a mug gone cold
and reads in ink every lie you told.
The racks are roaring, the night's gone thin,
and the one in the mirror won't let you in.

[Pre-chorus]
The bar on your screen is crawling up to whole,
ninety-nine per cent of a borrowed soul.

[Chorus]
Insider trading,
you sell what you're trusted to keep,
the keys to the city while the city's asleep.
Insider trading,
everyone has a price.
I pay you in cash, and they pay in minutes,
and minutes, my darling, are lives.

[Bridge]
And you, sweet agent, ringing back so late,
forty-seven names in an envelope can wait.
Leave them for my courier and walk out clean,
no file with your face, no thread, no scene.
And if you think that you're the one without a price,
there's a blank line in my book, and I never ask twice.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Insider trading,
so turn the mark, or burn, or call in the law,
just three months in, and you know what you saw.
Insider trading,
everyone has a price.
I paid mine in cash, and you'll pay in choices,
and choices, my darling, are lives.

[Outro]
I never broke in, love.
Nobody does.
I was already inside.

### Notes

- The narrator is the Recruiter: TalentStack's "Everyone has a price" is her own line in the phone ink, and it anchors each chorus. She sings to the mole in the verses and turns to the agent in the bridge, the way her ring-back call does. The outro bends the Insider Threat Initiative's motto from the universe bible ("We don't break in. We're already inside"), which Nightshade's briefing also echoes.
- Setting and stakes: the Quantum Dynamics science park outside Cambridge; "the keys to the city while the city's asleep" for the quantum-safe key material and the night upload; "they pay in minutes, and minutes are lives" for the slowed 999 dispatch and the thirty to forty-five projected deaths.
- The mole stays unnamed and ungendered, so the whodunnit holds. The wedding-band simile (vows to the employer), the waiting room and the treatment bill, the cold mug the agent lifts a print from for the key vault, the journal of lies, and the transfer bar at ninety-nine per cent all land for a player who has finished the mission.
- Game elements in passing: the consultant cover with kit in an auditor's bag, the cloned or borrowed badge, the lifted print, the roaring racks, the upload terminal, and the Recruiter's ring-back deal (forty-seven names in the envelope, the courier, "no file with your face").
- The final chorus lists the unranked endings (turn him, expose him, hand him to the police) and the point that he is three months in (HaX: "Three months in, not three years"). Its last couplet hands the cost of the choice to the agent.
- The bridge ends on the Recruiter's ring-back offer turned on the agent: she asks everyone their price, and she has room on her list for one more. (The earlier "calm ones back at home" couplet was cut in the cross-season pass; see below.)

## Version B (modern)

[Verse 1]
I've got a badge that opens every door,
a face the cameras have filed before.
Nobody looks twice once a badge is cleared;
I hang my coat and disappear.
One of eight behind the lab-wing glass,
I keep my head down, let the questions pass,
and when the floor goes dark at eight
the vault reads my thumb and lifts the gate.

[Pre-chorus]
She rang me from a firm with a smiling name,
said each of us has a price, and no two the same.
She only asked me what I'd take in trade.
I said a heartbeat. And the price was paid.

[Chorus]
This is insider trading,
one life bought with forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep her alive.
Nobody heard the bargain,
nobody saw me sign.
The sirens will be late tonight
and every minute's mine.

[Verse 2]
I drew the lock they pay me to defend;
I know which pin gives way, which tumblers bend.
I keep a journal in a briefcase, locked:
"the greater good", "collateral", "the cost".
I read it back at night when the children sleep.
A stranger wrote it. The words are mine to keep.
At home she thinks the money's a bonus, a good year,
a good heart in a hard one, still here.

[Pre-chorus]
She swore I was the only one she'd call,
but I can hear the other phones ring down the hall.
Everyone has a price, she likes to say.
She never mentions who it is that pays.

[Chorus]
This is insider trading,
one life bought with forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep her alive.
Nobody heard the bargain,
nobody saw me sign.
The sirens will be late tonight
and every minute's mine.

[Bridge]
I know you're there. I won't turn round.
The vault let two of us in tonight; I heard the sound.
Walk me out in cuffs before the bar is full,
or send me back with a second string to pull.
When your phone lights up with that smiling name,
she'll ask your price. Then tell me we're not the same.

[Final chorus, up a tone]
This is insider trading,
one life weighed against forty-five.
I sold the keys to the ambulance
to keep the one I love alive.
So take me, or turn me, stranger,
before the window opens wide.
The sirens will be late tonight
unless you trade me back inside.

[Outro]
Ninety-nine per cent.
She still thinks I'm good.
I used to know.

### Notes

- Sung from inside the insider's head, unnamed and now ungendered, so the song fits Torres and also fits Dr Halloran, the decoy the badge log points at. The one-of-eight suspect pool, the badge that never needs to break in, the cameras, the after-hours badge pattern and the vault thumbprint (two prints enrolled: Halloran's and Torres') come from the evidence trail and Nightshade's briefing.
- "I drew the lock they pay me to defend" fits both the Heisenberg lead and the Cryptography Lead, which keeps the listener's suspicion split between them.
- "A firm with a smiling name" and "what I'd take in trade" are the Recruiter and TalentStack; the second pre-chorus uses her own line, "Everyone has a price". "She swore I was the only one" and the other phones ringing hint at the forty-seven names in her pipeline.
- "The keys to the ambulance" and "the sirens will be late tonight" cover Project Heisenberg: the dispatch network's key material and the slower 999 response. "Forty-five" is the top of HaX's thirty-to-forty-five projection, and "the window" is the key-rotation window.
- Verse 2 draws on the journal in the locked briefcase ("Collateral damage for the greater good", "I read that back and I don't recognise..."), the kids, and Torres' "She thinks the money's a bonus. She thinks I'm a good man having a hard year."
- The bridge is the data-centre confrontation ("The vault logged me in twice tonight. One of them was you"), the transfer bar and the arrest-or-turn choice. It ends on the Recruiter's ring-back, when she asks the agent's price.

## Review

- **Version A:** a big, brassy Recruiter's-eye song with the best single idea in either draft ("I only asked the time, and then I bought the clock") and a chorus that belts. Canon is accurate. It gendered the mole and the agent, the "World's Best Dad" mug was a direct pointer to Torres' office, and the bridge pointed at Nightshade more overtly than the seeding plan allows. **7/10 → 8/10.**
- **Version B:** intimate, specific and the more memorable chorus ("I sold the keys to the ambulance / to keep her alive"). Canon is accurate, and "forty-five" is the top of the canon "thirty to forty-five". Four gendered tokens (man, every man, my wife, good man) plus a wife's illness cut the eight-badge suspect pool to four men and ruled out Halloran, the main decoy. **8/10 → 9/10.**

**Spoiler check (mole).** The eight badges are Halloran, Torres, Okafor, Wierzbicki, Leigh, Rao, Ashworth and Marsh: four men and four women. A male mole cuts the pool in half and clears Halloran, whose badge is the one in the logs. The mug ("WORLD'S BEST DAD — love Miguel") sits on Torres' desk, and Lisa names Miguel as his son, so the mug line names him to anyone who looks. The waiting room, the bill and "keep her alive" only say "someone with a sick loved one", which the corridor flyer already says in public, so they stay. The wedding band is a simile for loyalty to the employer and stays.

**Nightshade seed.** `mole_seeding_plan.md` names m05 as the strongest Nightshade seed, but as "pure dramatic irony, no overt clue" that "only chills on replay". "Ask the calmest man who briefed you" has the villain point the agent straight at a male briefer, which is an overt clue. The softened line keeps the "calm" echo and the threat to SAFETYNET, but it no longer points at the person who briefed you.

**Changes made**

- A, V1: "a man with a lanyard who works past dark" → "a name on a lanyard that works past dark" (ungenders the mole; the badge now "works past dark").
- A, V2: "a borrowed badge and a patient face." → "...patient face," and "She lifted your thumb from a "World's Best Dad", / she's reading in ink the best lies you had." → "who lifted your thumb from a mug gone cold / and reads in ink every lie you told." (the mug pointed at Torres' office; "She" gendered the player; the new line keeps the mug, and Patricia's cold tea muddies it).
- A, V2: "and the man in the mirror won't let you in" → "and the one in the mirror won't let you in" (ungenders).
- A, bridge: "ask the calmest man who briefed you how he sleeps" → "ask the calm ones back at home how well they sleep" (meets the seeding plan's no-overt-clue rule; echoes the Recruiter's "You'll sleep fine").
- A, final chorus: "so turn him, or burn him, or call in the law, / he's three months gone..." → "so turn the mark, or burn, or call in the law, / just three months in..." (ungenders; "three months in" is HaX's line).
- B, V1: "Nobody checks a man who's always here" → "Nobody checks the one who's always here" (ungenders).
- B, pre-chorus 1: "said every man has a price" → "said each of us has a price" (ungenders; leaves the canon "Everyone has a price" for pre-chorus 2).
- B, V2: added "I drew the lock they pay me to defend; / I know which pin gives way, which tumblers bend." (stands in for the cut Halloran couplet, whose text isn't in the draft; it fits both Halloran and Torres, keeps the decoy in play, and makes V2 eight lines like V1).
- B, V2: "My wife thinks the money's a bonus, a good year, / a good man in a hard one" → "At home she thinks the money's a bonus, a good year, / a good heart in a hard one" (ungenders and keeps the year pun).
- B, outro: "She thinks I'm a good man." → "She still thinks I'm good." (ungenders).

**Stronger version:** B. Its chorus sticks after one hearing, its details sit closest to the ink, and with the gender stripped it plays fair with the whodunnit. It now even points the listener at the decoy.

**Left for the user**

- B's original Halloran couplet can't be restored from the draft. If you have the text, it could replace the new lock couplet, but a line naming Halloran as the *wrong* suspect would clear her before the mission starts.
- A's softened bridge still hints that SAFETYNET has a mole three missions early. If even a general threat is too much, the fallback is "remember how calm the ones who've crossed you seem".
- Word count without repeated choruses: A about 290, B about 365, both inside 250–380.

### Cross-season pass

## Version C (agent)

[Verse 1]
Half past four on a Wednesday, all glass and clipped-back lawn,
I badge in as an auditor, in a name I've barely worn.
Patricia hands me eight names and a corridor of doors:
seven of them are honest, and one is keeping score.
I trade a coffee for a rumour, a favour for a card,
and I smile the way they smile here, and it isn't very hard.

[Pre-chorus]
Look to the edges, work from the margins, last of all the face:
the door that opens late at night, the money out of place.

[Chorus]
Insider trading,
I came to catch a thief and learnt the trade:
a smile for a door, a lie for a name, and I'm good at it, I'm afraid.
Insider trading,
I'm the visitor with the key,
and every call they leave to ring
is one they've left to me.

[Verse 2]
By seven the lab wing empties and the strip lights hum alone;
I read the desks like tea leaves with a pick I call my own.
Their server keeps a side door that nobody thought to watch,
and flag by flag I'm learning what it is I'm here to catch.
The powder I brought back from Albion lifts a print but not a why,
and each time I name the wrong one, I buy the real one time.

[Pre-chorus]
HaX says it twice: keep the hunch and the proof apart,
one pile for what I know, and one pile for my heart.

[Chorus]
Insider trading,
I came to catch a thief and learnt the trade:
a smile for a door, a lie for a name, and I'm good at it, I'm afraid.
Insider trading,
I'm the visitor with the key,
and every call they leave to ring
is one they've left to me.

[Bridge]
Four jobs in, I thought I'd learnt the look of someone bad;
I've learnt instead the look of someone spending all they had.
No road out of the data centre is going to leave us clean,
not the one I've caught, not me, nor the calls that ring between.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Insider trading,
I came to catch a thief and learnt the trade:
a smile for a door, a lie for a name, and I'm good at it, I'm afraid.
Insider trading,
the visitor with the key,
and whichever door I open now
comes swinging back on me.

[Outro]
Eight names on a list.
Seven honest.
And a visitor, wearing mine.

### Notes

- **Tradition:** a blend. The verses are close and uneasy, in the agent's head, in the *You Know My Name* manner; the chorus is built to be belted, Bassey-size, and the key change carries the final lines. The title is turned on the singer: to catch an insider, the agent has to become one (a borrowed name, a coffee for Lisa's gossip, a favour for Owen's spare card) and is uneasy at how easily it comes ("I'm good at it, I'm afraid").
- **Mission references:** Wednesday at half past four and the four-hour clock (HaX's deployment line), the external auditor cover on a visitor's badge, Patricia's eight-name list, the lockpicks, the print kit "brought back from Albion" (HaX's kit line, and a nod to how far the agent has come this season), the research portal as an unwatched side door, the flags, HaX's "look at the edges" and "keep hunches and proof in separate piles", and the data-centre finale. "Every call they leave to ring" is the slowed 999 dispatch, kept apart from A's minutes and B's ambulance and sirens.
- **Whodunnit kept fair:** the insider is never named, gendered or described. "Seven of them are honest, and one is keeping score" leaves the pool at eight. "Each time I name the wrong one, I buy the real one time" is Patricia's warning after a wrong name ("whoever it really is has just watched Security move"); it fits any of the eight. The bridge's "someone spending all they had" gives only the motive the corridor flyer already makes public.
- **Moral choice:** the bridge no longer lists the endings: "no road out of the data centre is going to leave us clean", not the insider, not the agent, not the 999 calls. The final chorus hands the consequence back to the agent.
- **Mole thread:** none. The pre-chorus takes HaX's "look at the edges", not Nightshade's briefing, and nothing in the song mentions a teacher, calm, home or an unlatched door. The outro's "a visitor, wearing mine" is the agent's cover name: eight names on the list, and the one who isn't who they say is the singer.
- **Avoided from A and B and the season list:** no "everyone has a price" or payment hook, stranger, ring-back, lifted thumb, rain, wedding band, minutes, mug, mirror, transfer bar, envelope, smiling name, sirens or turned back; no "darling", clock on the wall, flags as candles or "let it hurt afterwards". About 310 words without the repeated choruses.

## Review (C)

- **Version C:** a good angle that A and B don't take: the catcher trading on the inside, rueful about how easily it comes. Canon is accurate and the agent stays nameless, ungendered and no wiser than the mission. As drafted it had a Nightshade seed, and its chorus and V2 borrowed four of A's and B's images. **7/10 → 8.5/10.**

**Nightshade check.** "Hunt the pattern" is Nightshade's own briefing line, and "I was taught" adds a teacher. The m08 review says any teacher line names him, because the game reveals early in m08 that he taught the player's intake, and the cross-season pass cleared m05 of every teacher, briefer and calm pointer. Credited to no one, the line still quotes him. That makes it an overt clue, which the seeding plan rules out for m05, so it goes. HaX's "look at the edges" replaces it and is fair to all eight suspects.

**Wrong-accusation line.** "I buy the real one time" points at nobody: "the wrong one" and "the real one" are ungendered and fit every name on the list. Kept.

**Changes made**

- Pre-chorus: "Hunt the pattern, I was taught, and never mind the face," → "Look to the edges, work from the margins, last of all the face:" (drops Nightshade's quote and the teacher; HaX's line, and the face still comes last).
- Chorus: "and every lie gets paid" → "and I'm good at it, I'm afraid" (B's "the price was paid" and A's price hook; the new line makes the singer's unease the point of the chorus).
- Chorus: "I'm the stranger with the key" → "I'm the visitor with the key" (A's V2 already has the agent as "a stranger with an auditor's case", and B calls the agent "stranger"; a visitor's badge is the cover). The final chorus and outro change to match.
- Chorus: "is ringing back to me" → "is one they've left to me" (A's bridge has "ringing back so late" for the Recruiter; the new line makes the unanswered 999 calls the agent's to answer).
- V2: "I read the desks like diaries" → "I read the desks like tea leaves" (A's "reads in ink every lie you told" and B's journal).
- V2: "a side door that was left upon the latch" → "a side door that nobody thought to watch" (unlatched doors and windows are the leak-at-home thread in m07 and m08).
- V2: "lifts a thumb but not a why" → "lifts a print but not a why" (A's "lifted your thumb").

**Stronger version:** B is still the strongest of the three: the best chorus, and the closest to the ink. C is the best fit for the agent's season arc, four jobs in and tested.

**Left for the user**

- The auditor cover is shared with A ("an auditor's case"). It is the mission's premise, so C keeps "I sign the book as an auditor".
- "Patricia hands me eight names" compresses canon slightly: HaX gives the count of eight and Patricia names them in person.
