# Digital Archaeology

## Version A (classic)

[Verse 1]
Sodium light on a grain-store wall,
a shredder singing down the hall,
you came by night to read the dead
in dust and tape and ribbon red.
They filed the bodies, box by box,
they filed the hearts behind the locks,
and deep in the restricted rows
lies the file of a man that they used to know.

[Pre-chorus]
So dig, darling, dig,
before the morning eats the page;
every secret has a shelf,
and a lady minds the cage.

[Chorus]
Digital archaeology!
You brush the dust and you find me,
every word I ever signed
laid out in the dark for you.
Digital archaeology,
go deep enough and you will see
the name you never dared to say
was shining through.

[Verse 2]
A prompt is waiting on a borrowed screen,
a ledger tells you where I've been:
four faces at the briefing, smiling through,
four hands you shook. Are all of them true?
A velvet voice is on the phone,
she'll sell a name to wipe her own,
and every lock that turned before
was oiled for you. Now what's it for?

[Pre-chorus]
So dig, darling, dig,
the safe is humming out its code;
there's a folder with your name on it,
and it knows the way you'll go.

[Chorus]
Digital archaeology!
You brush the dust and you find me,
every word I ever signed
laid out in the dark for you.
Digital archaeology,
go deep enough and you will see
the name you never dared to say
was shining through.

[Bridge]
Nine long winters they kept a broken lock
and stamped it *value*, and sealed the box;
I wrote the letter, never sent.
Now ask your masters what they meant.
I drew their walls when I was theirs,
and still they climb my own back stairs.
Burn every page, but leave one light,
one server humming through the night.

[Final chorus, up a key]
Digital archaeology!
You'll dig me up and then you'll see
the man who wrote your every move
was waiting at the end for you.
Digital archaeology,
the hand that buried it was me.
So dig, darling, dig,
I left the last box open,
come and find me before six.

[Outro]
Paper turns to ash, love,
tape turns to snow...
I filed you years ago.

### Notes

- Setting and the purge: the sodium-lit grain warehouse, the shredder audible from the first minute, "dust and tape" (paper stacks, tape room), the cage with Sorensen inside it ("a lady minds the cage"), and "tape turns to snow" for the deck wipe.
- Narrator is the Architect speaking to Agent 0x00. "A man that they used to know" and "I drew their walls when I was theirs" are the m09 proof that he was SAFETYNET staff (and play on his codename). The chorus's "name you never dared to say" is the identity reveal.
- Game elements: the drop terminal ("a prompt is waiting on a borrowed screen"), the flags as shelves opened, the ledger (flag 2), the Recruiter's phone offer ("a velvet voice... she'll sell a name to wipe her own": WREN's name for her own handler tape, and she sells one of her own to do it), Sorensen's safe opened with the PIN cracker ("the safe is humming out its code"), and the Reading ("a folder with your name on it, and it knows the way you'll go").
- Twist hints: "four hands you shook. Are all of them true?" raises the ledger's sleeper on the raid team as a question, without pointing at any one of the four. "Every lock that turned before / was oiled for you" says the way in was made easy on purpose. "Leave one light, one server humming" and "I left the last box open" hint that the purge spared Tomb Beta's server on purpose because he wants to be found and the raid to come.
- Bridge: the Albion flaw SAFETYNET kept for nine years ("stamped it *value*" is the "retain: operational value" mark), his unsent vendor notice, and his old place inside SAFETYNET ("I drew their walls"). "Before six" is the Publication's 06:00 UTC hour; "I filed you years ago" closes on the Reading.

## Version B (modern)

*Slow 6/8. Piano and a low cello line, a ticking that turns out to be a shredder's motor. Sung by the Architect, close to the mic, to the agent he has been reading.*

[Verse 1]
Sodium light on a grain-store wall,
one name painted over another's scrawl.
You hear the teeth before you find the door,
a shredder eating paper, drawer by drawer.
Behind the mesh she feeds it, calm and small;
people are records, and she keeps them all.

[Pre-chorus]
Bring your picks and a borrowed print,
and every door gives at your hand.
Didn't it open too easily?
Didn't it fit like it was planned?

[Chorus]
So dig me up, dig me up,
brush the dust from every lie I told;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
where only you would go.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
is the one that tells you where I'll be.

[Verse 2]
There's a file in the stacks with your number on,
I kept it like a diary while you were gone.
Every door you forced and every hand you stayed,
I read you like the weather, and I wasn't afraid.
There's a wren up in your rafters, singing low,
small enough to love and close enough to know.

[Pre-chorus]
A voice on the line has a name to sell
for a reel she'd rather see erased.
Save one, lose one, the spools are turning;
didn't it feel like it was placed?

[Chorus]
So dig me up, dig me up,
brush the dust from every lie I told;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
where only you would go.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
is the one that tells you where I'll be.

[Bridge]
Nine years back I wrote a letter, never sent.
They stamped it *keep*, and that is where the warning went,
where all the warnings go.
And they never asked me for my key;
my name still turns in their own locks for me.
Ask the one who sat in my front row
which of my lessons she kept, and which she let go.
Tell her I'll call. Tell her you'll come.
Six in the morning, and the presses run.

[Final chorus, up a tone]
So dig me up, dig me up,
turn the last page, where I wrote your name;
I buried myself in boxes, love,
and you came, you came.
Tape by tape and bone by bone,
call it digital archaeology;
they burn the rest, but the one I left alone
brings you down the road to me.

[Outro]
Sodium light. The shredder stops.
Your hand, my key.

### Notes

- Setting and purge: the sodium light, the grain warehouse with one name stencilled over an older one, and the shredder heard before the door all come from Act 1's loading dock. "Behind the mesh she feeds it" and "people are records, and she keeps them all" are Sorensen in the restricted cage, almost word for word her own line.
- Game kit: "picks and a borrowed print" is the lockpicks and the print lifted off Sorensen's stamp. "Every door gives at your hand" and the "too easily / like it was planned" pre-chorus hint that the way in was left open on purpose, which is the night's real surprise. "Save one, lose one, the spools are turning" is the tape-deck choice at the end of the Recruiter's call, and "a voice on the line has a name to sell" is her offer.
- WREN: "a wren up in your rafters... small enough to love and close enough to know" points at a plant on the raid team, someone likeable and close by, without naming anyone. All four of the team have a beat that reads differently after flag 2, so the line points at none of them.
- The main twist (the purge order spares the Tomb Beta server on purpose because he wants to be found and wants them to come) is the chorus hook: "they burn the rest, but the one I left alone / is the one that tells you where I'll be." "Six in the morning, and the presses run" is the Publication's hour.
- The identity proof and the kept flaw: "my name still turns in their own locks" is the staff credential nobody revoked. "A letter, never sent... stamped it *keep*" is the Albion memo, the vendor notice he never sent and the "retain: operational value" stamp. "The one who sat in my front row" is HaX ("same classroom"), asked which of his lessons she kept, and "Tell her I'll call" foreshadows his call to her at the close.
- The Reading: Verse 2's "file in the stacks with your number on" and the final chorus's "turn the last page, where I wrote your name" are the Architect's file on 0x00. "Your hand, my key" hints at its last-page prediction (that 0x00 presses the Publication's key) without saying so.

## Review

- **Version A:** verdict: a proper brassy Bassey number with a chorus that lands first time and a bridge that carries the kept flaw and "let it hurt afterwards" well. The draft gave away m10's whodunit: "the kindest hand... put a key in yours" picks out the one officer who hands over the cracker, and the cracker logged "to WREN" is m10's third clue. Score 6.5 → 8.
- **Version B:** verdict: intimate and controlled, and the chorus hook ("they burn the rest, but the one I left alone") carries the mission's real surprise, the Tomb Beta server spared on purpose, without stating it. The first pre-chorus cast doubt on "the gift a friend pressed into your hand", which points at Kowalczyk as plainly as A did. The wren in the rafters names only the codename, which every player learns at flag 2, and fits all four officers, so it stays. Score 7 → 8.5.
- **Changes made:**
  - A, V1: "lies the one who taught the one you chose" → "lies the one who taught the voice you know". The player doesn't choose HaX; she's the voice on the line, and he taught her.
  - A, V2: "four hands you shook, and one is mine" → "four hands you shook. Is one of them mine?" Stated flat, it gave away the flag-2 floor-drop before the mission starts. As a question it stays a hint.
  - A, V2: "she'll sell a name for one more sign" → "she'll sell a name to wipe her line". Canon: the Recruiter trades WREN's name for her own file being wiped. "Line" works as her phone line and her network both.
  - A, V2: "and the kindest hand that held the door / put a key in yours. Now what's it for?" → "and every lock that turned before / was oiled for you. Now what's it for?" Whodunit giveaway (above). The new line points at the Architect leaving the way open, which is canon and which every player finds out in m09.
  - A, Bridge: "and called it insurance on the clock" → "and stamped it *value*, and stopped the clock". The old line was filler. The new one echoes the "retain: operational value" mark.
  - B, Pre-chorus 1: "and the gift a friend pressed into your hand" → "and every door gives at your hand". Whodunit giveaway. "Too easily / like it was planned" now lands on the open doors, the night's real surprise.
  - B, Bridge: "Six in the morning, and the sum is done" → "Six in the morning, and the presses run". "The sum is done" said nothing. The presses are the Publication.
  - Notes in both versions updated to match.
- **Stronger version:** B. Its chorus sings the mission's actual twist as a double meaning, its canon detail is tighter (the *keep* stamp, the unrevoked credential, the front-row pupil), and it sounds clearly different from A.
- **Left for the user:** whether A's "four hands you shook" question is still too pointed for a title sequence (the alternative is to cut the count: "every hand you shook"). Both songs have the Architect claiming WREN as his ("one of them mine", B's wren), which matches his m10 line "You're mine", but she is the Initiative's placement, not one of his. Fine as a villain's boast; it should be checked if the m10 line changes.

## Version C (agent)

*A blend. Modern verses, low and dry, on a slow 4/4 with brushed snare and a single guitar; the classic tradition comes in at the bridge, when the strings and brass arrive, and the final chorus goes up a key. Sung by Agent 0x00, alone in the building, with HaX in one ear.*

[Verse 1]
Three days of leave, and they let me have one;
then a sheet they took off a courier's run
said *burn it all before the Publication*,
a book nobody's read, and it isn't done.
So it's picks at a dock in the Helena dark,
past a front desk where the trolleys park;
a man with a torch walks the fence alone.
She says he's nobody's. I let him go home.

[Pre-chorus]
"One of ours, once," is all they'll say,
no name till a court can hear;
and the last of ours we trusted with the vault
had it fifteen years.

[Chorus]
It's only digital archaeology,
the dead, the dark, and me;
I'm turning the ground of the life he left,
and he can't turn round to see.
So I keep my lamp low and I keep to his track,
and the dead don't look back.

[Verse 2]
Paper comes down like a wedding's end
round a careful clerk with a rubber stamp;
she inks *destroyed* on each life in a box,
and I lift her print off the handle, damp.
And back in the racks where the tapes run cold,
one machine's as warm as a hand,
and it greets me the way a good host would
who'd been told what time I'd land.

[Pre-chorus]
"Same classroom," she wrote. "Don't ask me whose."
So I don't. I let it lie.
If HaX were sure, she'd have handed it over;
she was never one to hide.

[Chorus]
It's only digital archaeology,
the dead, the dark, and me;
I'm turning the ground of the life he left,
and he can't turn round to see.
So I keep my lamp low and I keep to his track,
and the dead don't look back.

[Bridge]
There are two cards at the end of the wall,
two names, and no outcome at all;
nobody read them out loud today,
so I've said them the whole long way.
I came in keen, and I've been sold since,
and I've stopped needing a name:
whoever's behind that red-ringed "A.",
I'm digging all the same.

[Final chorus, up a key]
It's only digital archaeology,
the dead in their rows, asleep;
I'm turning the ground of the life he left,
and nobody's reading me.
I'm the one with the lamp, I'm the one on the track,
and the dead don't look back.
No. The dead don't look back.

[Outro]
Three days of leave. I got one.
I'll sleep when the digging's done.

### Notes

- **Tradition and voice:** a blend. The verses stay inside the agent's head (modern); the bridge and the lifted final chorus open into the classic sweep. By m09 the agent has been betrayed and is now hunting: tired, dry, set on the job ("I came in keen, and I've been sold since"). No name or pronoun for the agent, and no body detail beyond a hand lifting a print.
- **Story references:** the leave cut from three days to one; the intercepted order ("Burn Helena before the Publication"), which nobody can explain yet ("a book nobody's read"); picks at the loading dock; Noor's trolleys at the front desk; Cody Old Coyote on his round (HaX's "not ours, not theirs" becomes "she says he's nobody's"); the shredder's confetti ("a wedding's end"); Sorensen, the former county clerk, stamping each box *destroyed*, and her print lifted off the stamp she sets down at the hatch; the cold tape racks and Tomb Beta's warm server; the two dead agents' cards with no outcome, which nobody reads at the briefing; the circled "-- A."; Netherton's "one of theirs" and "a name I can say in court"; HaX's "Same classroom… Don't ask me whose yet" text from m08; Nightshade, fifteen years one of ours.
- **Dramatic irony (the agent sees none of it):** "he can't turn round to see", "nobody's reading me" and the hook "the dead don't look back" sound confident to the agent. Anyone who has played the mission knows about the Reading, the Architect's file on 0x00. "Greets me the way a good host would / who'd been told what time I'd land" is a figure of speech to the agent and, to a player, the server left for 0x00 on purpose. "If HaX were sure, she'd have handed it over; / she was never the kind to hide" is the agent's trust in HaX on the night she confesses she sat on the name.
- **Kept out on purpose:** WREN, the raid team, the ledger's sleeper and Kowalczyk's cracker. The Architect is not named, the two dead agents are not named (Netherton reads them at the debrief), and there are no flags, Field Guide or technical detail. The title opens every chorus.
