# Player run: fixture A "Open evening", newcomer persona

Persona: office worker doing a mandatory awareness exercise. Rarely plays games, knows nothing about
security, gets lost in jargon. Read only `player_packets/fixture_A.md`.

No judge was available in this run. Where the packet says "ask the judge", I write what I chose to do
and mark the result **[judge result unknown]**. Verdicts are the ones the persona would submit.

## Before I start: reading the brief

- "Cultists" and "ritual": fine, it's a spooky story. I get that I'm a spy at a party.
- The three "profiles" took me three reads.
  - **Keyholder**: easy. Whoever has the one brass key.
  - **Courier**: "Was in the Archive, wants the Codex, and carries no ward charm." I don't know what a
    Codex or a ward charm is. My first reading was that the ward charm is a *cult* thing, so carrying
    one is suspicious. It took a while to notice the word "no". I'm still not sure whether all three
    parts have to be true or just one.
  - **Devotee**: "Has three or more marked clues." Marked by whom? I thought it meant clues I had
    marked on the board. Later I worked out it means the little ✦ star. Nothing on the brief says
    "✦ = marked" until the board.
- "Courier" and "Devotee" sound like job titles. I kept forgetting which was which.
- HaX: "Don't do favours for anyone you haven't checked." Noted, but I didn't know what a "favour"
  would look like in the game. I assumed it meant someone asking me for money.
- Stakes: wrong name = the lodge closes ranks; miss one = they're at the ritual. Clear enough, and it
  made me nervous about guessing.

### What I think a careful player would do (worked out afterwards, reading the board properly)

After finishing I went back to the spreadsheet bit of the board. "Burner SIM: 1 in this case, 0 found."
I never found a burner SIM. Every other carried item is accounted for: ward charms (Venn, Ishikawa),
notebooks (Okafor, Ishikawa), umbrella (Delacroix), key (Okafor), ring (Marsh). Marsh has two "what they
carry" spaces and only one is filled. So the burner SIM must be Marsh's, which gives her three stars and
makes her a **Devotee**. And the "phone" I handed back is probably where that SIM was.

So I most likely missed a cultist *and* did her a favour. As a newcomer I'd never have spotted that
during play: I didn't know the hidden spaces on her card mattered, and I didn't know the count table
was a tool for elimination. Realising it afterwards was a real "oh!" moment, but a slightly bitter one.

## Confused or stuck

1. Courier's "carries **no** ward charm": I first read the charm as a cult item.
2. "Marked clues": nothing on the brief explains ✦ until you open the board.
3. Whether every profile has to be used. I went hunting for a Courier who doesn't exist (as far as I
   can tell) and nearly accused Ishikawa to fill the slot.
4. The clue-count table. It looks like admin. Nothing tells me "use this to work out clues you haven't
   found". This is how you solve Marsh, so it needs teaching, perhaps by HaX ("Check the counts: if a
   clue hasn't turned up, who has a space left for it?").
5. "Checked" in HaX's warning. I thought dusting Marsh's glass counted as checking her. A clearer line
   might be "Don't do favours for anyone you haven't ruled out."
6. The second "what they carry" space on cards. I didn't notice that two spaces meant two items, so an
   unfilled one never bothered me.

## Wording that confused me

- "Courier", "Devotee", "Keyholder" as labels. Keyholder is self-explanatory; the other two aren't.
- "Codex", "ward charm", "sigil": fine as flavour, but I needed one sentence on what a ward charm *is*
  to understand why it clears someone.
- "Filter the visitor badge log".
- "Has three or more marked clues".
- "Stakes: a wrong call will change what you can do next." What does it change? Vague and worrying.
- "Anyone still standing here stays standing." I re-read this a few times. It means un-named guests go
  free, I think.

## What I learned about spotting an insider/agent

- Don't trust what a suspect says about themselves; check other sources (the packet said "a suspect's
  own word clears nobody").
- Wanting something (the Codex, a promotion) isn't enough on its own. Lots of innocent people want
  the same thing.
- Physical evidence (CCTV, fingerprints, what's in a coat) beats gossip.
- Be careful when someone asks you to fetch something for them, even something harmless like their own
  phone. That's the lesson I *should* have learned in play, and only learned afterwards.
- Count what's missing as well as what you've found.

## What I think the stakes were

Stop the cultists before the ritual. A wrong accusation closes doors (the lodge turns on me). Missing
one means they turn up at the ritual. Both cost score. Doing a favour for a cultist presumably helps
them, though the packet never told me how.
