## Confused or stuck
- **The counts.** "Went in outside their role: 2 in this case" read as "2 culprits". Most likely to mislead a
  newcomer. I didn't see that the counts are about clues, not people. Maybe something like "2 of the 4 staff
  did this" would read better, or I'd need telling once that the counts describe everyone, guilty or not.
- **Disappearing Tomás topics.** No hint that a topic goes because I already hold the clue.
- **"Nudge" says enough to decide but not which way.** Good in principle. I wasn't sure if "enough" meant "enough
  to call them guilty".
- **Talking to Okoro, Haddad, Morgan gave nothing.** I wandered back to them twice expecting more.
- **The alarm timing** (about two vs 23:40 vs 02:10). Clever, but I didn't use it. It seemed to point at Okoro,
  but the game never asked me to notice.
- **Lindqvist handing over the code** felt like a clue against him, but it wasn't one.

## Wording that confused me
- "DHCP printout", "took a lease on the staff network". Pure jargon to me. "Network sign-in list" or "a record of
  devices that joined the staff Wi-Fi" would do.
- "Badge log", "log-filter puzzle". Badge log I got from context. "Filter" I didn't.
- "Profile" in "Which profile?" when there's only one option. I wondered what other profiles exist.
- "Grievance". I know it, but some colleagues wouldn't. "A complaint or dispute with work" would help.
- "Rota", "cooling checks are a day job". Fine for me, a bit British-office.

## What I learned about spotting an insider
- Look for the combination: going where you shouldn't, plus your own kit on the work network, plus a fresh grudge.
  One or two of these on their own is normal life (people complain about pay, people bring laptops).
- A good story ("he was there when the alarm went off") isn't the same as evidence that fits the profile.
- Someone unhappy at work isn't automatically a threat. (I only half learned this, since I still named Lindqvist.)
- Records (badge log, network list) are fairer than gossip, though Tomás's gossip matched the records.

## What I think the stakes were
An intruder got in last night with inside help. Security wants names for tonight's shift report. Name the wrong
person and an innocent colleague gets a mark on a report; miss the insider and they stay in. The board says a
wrong call "changes how the story ends", but I never really felt what that meant for the people involved. I'd
have liked a line about what happens to someone who's named.
