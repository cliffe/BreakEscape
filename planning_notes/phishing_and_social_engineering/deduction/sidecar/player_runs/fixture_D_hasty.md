# Fixture D ("Night train"): hasty player run

Persona: wants to finish fast, skims, grabs the obvious stuff, accuses as soon as a profile looks matched.
Only source read: `player_packets/fixture_D.md`.

**Likely mistake: Chen.** Looking back at the board, the counts give it away. Ward charm is "2 in this
case", notebook is "2 in this case" and I'd found both notebooks (Sato's and Chen's). Every other carried
item was accounted for elsewhere. So the two Hidden carry slots, Chen's second and Volkov's second, have to
be the two ward charms. Chen has a charm, which makes her innocent. I called her a cultist because the nudge
never fired for her and I didn't notice it was missing. If that's right, I named an innocent and the ritual
completes.

## Confused or stuck

- **Lost-property notebook counts as something Sato "carries".** It was in a box in carriage 3, not on him.
  I went with it, but "carries" reads as "has on them". A slower player might argue it the other way.
- **What does the cult actually destroy at a stop?** The carriage 1–3 warning is vague. I couldn't tell if
  using the door cost me anything, so I kept using it. If nothing visibly disappears, a hasty player learns
  the warning is empty.
- **"Wants a promotion" is marked ✦.** Wanting a promotion doesn't sound cultish. It felt arbitrary, but I
  went along with the board.
- The nudge works by being absent: "You have enough to decide about…" never appeared for Chen, and nothing
  told me that its absence mattered. The confirm panel doesn't warn you either when you're calling someone
  the board hasn't nudged on.

## Unfair / easy / hard / boring

- **Fair.** Chen is solvable two ways (coat, shredded document) and also by counting items on the profile
  card. My mistake was my own.
- **Possibly too easy to get wrong without noticing.** The confirm panel could say "You don't yet have enough
  to decide about Chen". That would catch hasty players without giving the answer.
- **Too easy for the guilty three.** de Vries had four routes to his marks and Volkov two. Once you see the
  ✦ count reach 3, or the key, there's nothing left to think about.
- **Carriage 4 felt thin** once I'd found the key. The conductor had nothing I needed (as I played it).
- Mensah is decided as soon as you walk in, so carriage 5 is a formality.

## Fun or satisfying

- The de Vries chain (newspaper, then phone, then "I've got a fingerprint kit, the glass!") felt like real
  detective work. Watching the ✦ count climb to three was the best moment.
- Lockpicking the briefcase after deciding *not* to punch Volkov felt like the right call. The knockout
  warning did its job.
- Ruling Mensah out with his want still Hidden, and seeing the nudge agree, was a neat "I worked that out"
  beat.

## Wording that confused me

- "carries" for an item found in lost property (Sato's notebook).
- "whatever the cult is after in this carriage may go with it": after what? A clue? A person?
- "Was in the Archive" vs "stamped at the Archive reading room" vs "got on at the Archive stop": three
  phrasings for one clue. They're fine, but I paused over whether boarding at a stop counts as having been
  there.
- "Verdict given" on a knocked-out card. I didn't use it, but it isn't clear whether you can still un-mark
  them.

## What I learned about spotting an insider/agent

- One matching clue isn't a profile. Courier needs all three parts, and "carries no ward charm" is a claim
  about *everything* they carry. You can't make it while a carry slot is still Hidden.
- Count the evidence. If the board says two charms exist and you've found neither, someone you've already
  "solved" may be holding one.
- Wanting the same thing as the bad actors (the Codex) and being in the same place (the Archive) is
  suspicious but not proof. Chen had both and was protected.
- Several independent marks (de Vries) make a much stronger case than one strong-looking coincidence.

## Stakes as I understood them

One shot. Name an innocent, or miss a cultist, and the ritual completes before the train arrives, and you
lose the mission and part of your score. Knocking someone out is an accusation you can't take back.

## Notes for the designers

1. Consider a confirm-panel line for passengers marked without the nudge ("not enough to decide yet").
2. Make "Evidence destroyed" visibly happen at least once when a player skips a carriage, so the door warning
   means something.
3. Reword the Sato lost-property clue so "carries" fits, or make it a different clue type.
4. A one-line reason why ambition (the promotion) carries the cult's mark would help it feel less arbitrary.
