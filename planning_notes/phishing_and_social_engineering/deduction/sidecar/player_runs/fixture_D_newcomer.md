# Player run: fixture D "Night train", newcomer persona

Persona: office worker, sent on this as an awareness exercise. Doesn't play many games and knows nothing
about security. Reads slowly, takes words at face value, gets nervous about "you will not get a second chance".

Only `player_packets/fixture_D.md` was read.

## First impressions of the brief

- Five passengers, a cult and a ritual. Fine, it's a whodunnit on a train. I like that.
- The three profiles. I read them twice. "Any of these" is the key phrase, and I nearly missed it. My first
  thought was that someone has to tick all three.
- "Keyholder: there is only one." I wasn't sure if "only one" meant one key or one keyholder. Probably both.
- "Courier" has three parts joined by "and". I wrote it down on paper as a checklist: Archive? Codex? NO
  charm?
- "Devotee: three or more marked clues." What's a marked clue? The board explains it (the ✦), but the brief
  doesn't, so for a minute I thought it meant clues *I* had marked.
- Stakes: get one wrong and the ritual completes. That made me careful, maybe too careful. It also made me
  scared to punch anyone.
- "Bring the next stop forward" plus "the cult uses each stop to get rid of evidence" are a nice
  pair: there's a reason not to rush. I decided early to search everything before I left a carriage.

## The board, first look

- The clue-count table helped. "2 in this case, 0 found" tells me how many there are to find. I used it
  later to check I'd found the second ward charm and the second notebook.
- Words that confused me: "Tesseract" (no idea; I just thought "the special key"), "Codex" (an old book?),
  "sigil" (I guessed a symbol), "Burner SIM" (I've heard "burner phone" on TV, so I guessed a throwaway phone).
  Nothing explains them, and I didn't need to know what they were, only that they had the ✦.
- "Undecided / Cultist / Not a cultist" buttons are clear.

## Play log

### Carriage 1 (Sato, the steward)

1. **Luggage rack label.** Sato was at the Archive.
   - Sato: Archive. One part of Courier. Too early to call.
2. **Steward, "Who's been asking about the Codex?"** Sato wants the Codex ✦.
   - Sato: Archive and Codex. That's two of three Courier boxes. Now I need to know whether Sato has a ward
     charm. Sato's "carry" slot is still hidden. Felt like a proper detective moment.
3. **Steward, "What about the woman in carriage 2?"** Chen wants the Codex ✦. The steward got a bit short with me
   here (the second question).
   - Chen: wants Codex. Suspicious already ("came a long way for it" sounds creepy).
4. I was worried the steward would shut me out, so I used the **kind word** (thanked them for the tea), then
   asked **"And the passenger at the back?"** Mensah wants peace and quiet.
   - Mensah: harmless so far. Nothing marked.
   - I don't actually know if I needed the kind word. The packet says they "get guarded" but I couldn't tell
     how many questions I had left. A visible mood or a count would help a nervous player.
5. "The train is slowing." This panicked me, because I'd only been there about two minutes. I'd searched
   everything I could see, so I let the stop happen. I don't know what, if anything, the cult took from
   carriage 1. Nothing on the board said "Evidence destroyed", so I think I was fine.

Beliefs after carriage 1: Sato leaning cultist (Courier, need the carry slot). Chen leaning cultist (wants the
Codex). Others unknown. No calls.

### Carriage 2 (Chen)

6. **Chen's ticket.** Chen was at the Archive.
   - Chen now matches Archive and Codex, just like Sato. At this point I was ready to mark her
     Cultist (Courier). I'd forgotten the "no ward charm" part.
7. **Chen's coat.** A ward charm on a red cord.
   - Oh. Back to my paper checklist: Courier needs NO ward charm. Chen has one, so she is not a Courier.
     She has only one marked clue (Codex), so she isn't a Devotee, and she has no key. I marked her "Not a
     cultist", reason "has ward charm". **Satisfying moment**: the clue that looked like it was about
     magic was really the thing that cleared her.
8. **Chen, "What are you reading?"** Her notebook.
   - Nothing changes. Chen has 2 carry slots and both are now known (charm, notebook), so she has no key.
     Board said "You have enough to decide about Chen." Good, that matched what I thought.

I walked to the far door but didn't use it. The warning says "whatever the cult is after in this carriage
may go with it", which scared me off. I waited for the timer instead (about two minutes, felt long and a bit
boring standing about).

### Carriage 3 (de Vries)

9. **Lost-property box.** A notebook with "Sato, 1A" in it.

     Cultist (Courier). Took me a moment to see that a notebook found in carriage 3 told me about a
     passenger in carriage 1. That's a good twist, though I nearly skipped the lost-property box.
10. **Newspaper.** de Vries circled a Deputy Keeper job. Wants a promotion ✦.
    - de Vries: one marked clue.
11. **Wine glass + fingerprint kit.** de Vries's prints and a sigil ring smudge ✦.
    - I had to think about what "dust it with the fingerprint kit" meant. I'd never have thought of doing
      that if the kit wasn't in my bag. Two marked clues now.
12. **de Vries's phone.** Unregistered SIM, one number dialled over and over. Burner SIM ✦.
    - Three marked clues: promotion, ring, SIM. That's Devotee. Pop-up: "enough to decide about de Vries".
      Marked Cultist (Devotee).
    - I didn't know what "unregistered SIM" meant, but "cheap handset, no contacts, same number over and
      over" sounded shady in plain English. That sentence did the work, not the jargon.
13. **Shredded document puzzle.** Took a few goes. Receipt for one ward charm, made out to Chen.

## Where I was confused or stuck

- "Any of these" against the "and" inside Courier. Two kinds of logic in three lines. I nearly treated the
  profiles as all-must-match, then nearly treated Courier as any-part-matches.
- **The ward charm means different things for different people.** It clears a Courier but does nothing for a
  Keyholder. After Chen, I learnt "charm = innocent" and applied it to Volkov. This is the trap I fell into.
  It's a good lesson, but nothing in the game pushed back when I marked him wrongly early on.
- I didn't know how many questions the steward/conductor would take before clamming up.
- I couldn't tell what the cult actually removed at each stop, or whether I'd lost anything. The far-door
  warning made me never use it.
- How Sato's carry slot gets filled: the clue that clears or condemns Sato sits two carriages away in a
  lost-property box. Fair, but easy to walk past.
- "What rules them out? (optional)" - I wasn't sure whether my answer would be graded.

## Fair / unfair, too easy / hard, boring

- **Fair overall.** Every verdict could be worked out from what I found, and the count table let me check.
- **Too hard for me:** the briefcase lock and the shredded document, if you aren't used to games. Volkov's
  verdict depends on opening that briefcase (or punching him, which is scary).
- **A bit boring:** standing about waiting for timed stops because the "wait by the door" warning frightened
  me. The two puzzles (shredding, backup terminal) only gave me things I already knew, which felt like
  wasted effort, though I see they're backups in case evidence goes.
- **Too easy?** Mensah and Chen are fairly obvious once you have the charm. De Vries is very obvious: three
  shady things on one seat.

## Fun / satisfying moments

- Finding Chen's charm right after I'd decided she was guilty. Good reversal.
- Sato's notebook in a different carriage completing the Courier checklist.
- Catching my own Volkov mistake when the brass key appeared.
- Checking the "found" counts before the call, like ticking off a list.

## Wording that confused me

- Tesseract, Codex, sigil, burner SIM, unregistered SIM, backup recovery terminal, "restore a phone backup".
- "Marked clues" in the brief vs "marking" a passenger on the board. Same word, two meanings.
- "Wait by the door for the next stop" sounds like something safe and patient, but the warning makes it
  sound dangerous.
- "Verdict given" on a knocked-out passenger: I wasn't sure if that meant the game had decided, or I had.

## What I learnt about spotting an insider/agent

- Don't go on gut feeling. Chen "sounded" guilty and Volkov "sounded" nice. Both were wrong reads.
- One innocent-looking thing (a charm, a mum's gift) doesn't clear someone. Check what each rule actually
  needs.
- Look for several small warning signs together (Devotee) and for the one thing only an insider would have
  (Keyholder: the key).
- Things people leave lying around (tickets, phones, glasses, lost property) give them away. And helpful
  staff will tell you a lot about other people if you're polite. I suppose that's also the lesson about
  how *I* could be the chatty steward.

## What I thought the stakes were

Name every cultist and nobody innocent before the last stop, or the ritual completes and I lose. One mistake
loses the mission and costs score. Punching someone counts as accusing them. No second go.
