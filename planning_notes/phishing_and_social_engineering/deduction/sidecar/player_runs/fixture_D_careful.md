## Before moving

I read the profiles three times. My notes:

- Keyholder: one key in the whole case. Whoever has it is a cultist, and nobody else is a Keyholder.
- Courier needs all three: Archive, Codex, **no** ward charm. A ward charm clears someone of Courier only.
- Devotee: three ✦ clues. Only "wants" and "carries" clues are marked. "Where they were" never is. So someone
  needs a marked want plus two marked carried items, or three marked carried items.
- The card shapes matter. Sato and Mensah have one carry slot each. Chen, de Vries and Volkov have two. A
  one-slot passenger can have at most two marks, so Sato and Mensah can never be Devotees.
- Counts: ward charm 2, notebook 2, Archive 2. Those numbers will let me rule things out by elimination.

Stakes as I understood them: one wrong or missed name means the ritual completes and the mission is lost,
with no retry. It also costs score.

  tell whether I was fast enough or nothing was at risk. The carriage 1 to 3 warning made me nervous
  about waiting, which is the intent, but I had no feedback on whether my caution paid off.

**Unfair / too easy / too hard / boring**

- Slightly too easy once you notice the slot counts and the totals. Carriage 5 ended up as a formality.
- The shredded receipt and the backup terminal only confirmed what I already had. That's fine as insurance,
  but a player who finishes a puzzle and gets "already known" may feel short-changed.

**Fun or satisfying**

- The Mensah nudge firing in carriage 4: deciding about someone I hadn't met, by elimination.
- de Vries piling up three marks in three quick sources felt like closing a net.

**Wording**
- "What they carry" for a lost-property item (above).
- "You have enough to decide about <name>" is good: it pushes you to think without giving the answer.
- The stakes line appears three times (brief, engine line, brief again). It's clear, but repetitive.

**What I learned about spotting an insider/agent**
- The eager, obvious one (Chen) wasn't the cultist. The quiet, organised one (Sato, who only wanted to "see
  it before it's sold") was.
- A protective token or a nice story ("my mother gave it to me") doesn't rule someone out. What matters is
  access (the key) and the pattern of motive plus opportunity plus the missing safeguard.
- Several small signs together (ambition, a sigil ring, a burner phone) build a case that no single one
  makes.
- Check what's missing as well as what's there: Sato is condemned by the charm Sato doesn't have.

**Stakes as I understood them**
Name every cultist and only cultists, in one go, or the ritual completes and the mission fails. A wrong call
also costs score. Punching someone counts as accusing them.
