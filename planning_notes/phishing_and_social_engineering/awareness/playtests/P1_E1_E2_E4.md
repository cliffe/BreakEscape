Second attempt, with only "Catherine Merrin" and "Tomás Carvalho" noted: the To field works but `sender's address: no evidence yet` and `A blank needs evidence you haven't found yet`. I switched to the pretext with no blanks ("When you get a moment, could you send me next week's diary?"); Send still refused with the same line. The only way out was reload then "Restart" on the resume overlay, which discards the game. A learner would not guess that. The brief says "Close it when you have what you need" with no hint of how many facts that is, and the browser gives no count. Evidence: `p1-e4-session.jsonl`, game 3615, stage `write` with `done:["brief","osint"]` after the reload also resumed to the same stage. Suggested direction (not applied): block the browser's Close until the minimum facts are noted, or let the write stage reopen the browser.

## Per-episode notes

### E1 invoice fraud

- Brief made sense: yes. Kiosk text: "First you're on the red team … Then you swap seats and sort a payments inbox … Only the inbox is scored." Clear, and "authorised exercise" is stated three times. Adaeze's welcome line matches it.
- Attack half: three research sites plus "Add to notes" by clicking is easy to learn; each click gives a plain `Noted:` line. The meter ("Red flags") taught me what to remove, and it let me reach 0 flags, which felt like being coached into a better lure. Good.
- Switch to defence: taught, did not surprise. The flip stage ("This is the email you just sent, as Adaeze … would see it") lands well, with the line `Acting on a message built this carefully is not carelessness; it was made to pass for the real thing.`
- Fairness: one grumble (m1). The inbox mixes real mail well: the genuine Harlow invoice, Mei Lin's PO, and Adaeze's phone-verified bank change all need "Trust", which stops the lesson being "bank change means phish". That is a strong design choice. I got 97% and still felt the explanation was fair.
- Debrief and Monday card: the Monday line is memorable and concrete: `if an email asks you to change bank details or to pay in a hurry, don't reply and don't use any number in the email. Ring the person on a number you already hold, then report the email the way your organisation asks you to.` The end screen then says only "You've seen both sides of invoice fraud…", fine.
- Confusing UI: the "Research" stage never says how many facts are enough (see B1/M1 for the cost elsewhere). The mail composer's "Send as … You can only send as yourself" row did not appear here. The red-team inbox message "Authorised exercise" is useful, but nothing points at it.

### E2 CV lures

- Brief made sense: yes, but the NPC and kiosk both say "research the vacancy and the recruiter" and do not mention the hiring manager, who turns out to be needed (M1).
- Attack half: the pretext choices are well written and the meter ("A blank needs evidence you haven't found yet") is a good nudge, but it only nudges after the point of no return in my first run.
- Switch: weaker than E1/E4 (m3).
- Fairness: m2 and m8. Otherwise the inbox is fair; the real agency mail, the real PDF CV, the real docx CV (Darius, "on your website") and Colin's mail are all things a recruiter does daily, and the feedback for each real message explains why it is real.
- Debrief and Monday card: `read CVs in the viewer, and if a document asks you to enable editing or content, don't.` Memorable and usable. The "Nobody is marked down for a miss here … A miss here is a sign of a busy inbox, nothing more." line is kind.
- Confusing UI: m6; "Attach" lists "CV (Word document) (document)" and "Portfolio (zip archive) (archive)" with the type repeated.

### E4 exec whaling

- Brief made sense: yes. On resume the "Mission Brief" notes window reappears with `Click edit to add your observations...` and a "Continue" button, which is fine but is a second brief.
- Attack half: the strongest of the three. Choosing between an urgent and a calm pretext, and the advisory `That feels rushed.` after sending the urgent one, teach the point.
- Switch: taught. The flip stage plus "Now you're the PA" is clear. M2 and m4 undercut it.
- Fairness: all five inbox items are fair. The voicemail items are the highlight: the fake says `don't ring back, my phone's going off`, the real one says `Ring me on the mobile if anything comes up`. A voice is not proof. Note: keyless server has no TTS, so I judged the transcripts only; the audio is untested here.
- Careless run: I trusted everything. Result `Score 40 percent. Worth practising: here's where to look first`, `Phish caught 0 of 3`, `You didn't name any flags on these messages, so there's nothing to grade skill by skill.` Each wrong choice told me what would have happened (for example `£24,600 would go to an account the sender controls. The real Catherine never asked for it.`). This is a fair and gentle result. The careless learner still gets full task completion and the same end screen, which is intended.
- Debrief and Monday card: the "A voice is not proof" panel and `Ring them on the number already in your contacts, or ask finance, then report the message the way your organisation asks you to.` are memorable. The best single teaching moment across the three episodes.
- Confusing UI: B1 and m6. Verify gave 100 on both phish but "Reported rather than just deleted 1 of 3" (t2).

## What a rushed learner gets

E4, clean careless run (84 s): trusted all five, ticked nothing, got 40% and three "Not this time. This one was a phish." messages, each with the missed tells listed. The feedback is clear and blame-free. The only harsh outcome is the dead end in B1, which a rushed learner hits when they close the browser early; that is the part of the careless run that went wrong badly (three restarts needed to reproduce).
