# Course overview

## What this is

A cyber security awareness course for all staff in an organisation, built from short Break Escape games and hosted in Hacktivity. It is designed to meet the training requirements of the common standards and laws (ISO/IEC 27001, PCI DSS, NIS2, DORA, UK GDPR, the NCSC Cyber Assessment Framework, the NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit and others; see `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`) while being something staff would choose to play.

## Who it is for

- **All staff**, from new starters to the board, including people who rarely use a computer at work.
- **People in roles with extra risk:** finance, IT and service desk, developers, people managers and HR, customer-facing staff, procurement, and leaders and boards.
- **Sector staff** with sector rules, starting with healthcare.
- **The organisation's security, compliance and HR teams**, who assign the course, chase completion and show the evidence to auditors.

## What makes it different

- **Practice, not slides.** Staff spend most of each module doing a realistic piece of work while threats arrive unannounced, then see what happened.
- **Built for reporting.** The core habit is *Pause. Check. Report.* Reporting quickly, including after a mistake, is the behaviour the course rewards most.
- **Short.** 15 to 20 minutes a module, with 3 to 5 minute drills through the year.
- **Evidence by design.** Every assessed decision maps to a learning outcome, and every outcome maps to a requirement.
- **Accessible.** Every module can be taken as a text route with the same decisions and the same record.

| Pathway | Who | Modules | Time |

| **Sector packs** | Staff in the sector | sector_01 (healthcare); others later | 20 minutes each |

### Topic boundaries

Some topics touch. To avoid two modules teaching the same thing differently:

| Topic | Taught in | Touched in |
|---|---|---|
| Recognising phishing emails | core_01 | core_11 (payment requests), core_15 (AI-written phishing), core_17 |
| Calls, texts, QR codes, people at the door | core_02 | core_10 (in person at the building), role_03 and role_06 (callers at work) |
| What to do after clicking or entering a password | core_04 | core_01, core_03 |
| Personal data and the law | core_05 | core_06 (handling), role_05 (HR data) |
| Classification, sending, sharing and disposal | core_06 | core_05, core_13 |
| Software updates | core_07 | core_08, core_09 |
| Downloads, fake sites, browser warnings | core_08 | core_07 |
| Devices outside the office | core_09 | core_10 |
| Deepfake voice and video | core_15 | core_11, role_01, role_02 |
| Sharing credentials, leavers taking data | core_14 | core_03, role_05 |
| Policy acknowledgement | core_16 | every module's reference card |

The concepts file for the module that teaches a topic is the source of truth; modules that touch it point to it rather than restating it.

## Course-level completion

An organisation decides which pathways each person must complete and by when (HA-03). A module counts as complete when the learner has:

1. played it through (full module, text route, or a passed test-out where the organisation allows it);
2. passed its knowledge check (default 80%, set by the organisation);
3. shown each critical behaviour, or been taken back through the practice for it;
4. acknowledged any policy the organisation attached to it.

The record keeps the module version, dates, time on task, the per-outcome results and the score (HA-05).

### Phase 0: foundations

Engine: BE-01 assessed decisions, BE-02 debrief screen, BE-03 text route, BE-04 accessibility baseline, BE-05 organisation profile, BE-08 visual aids, BE-09 intranet terminal, BE-12 short sessions, BE-18 work task and interruptions, BE-19 minigames opened from conversations, BE-20 authorable tutorial and title screen, MG-20 knowledge check, EX-01 mentor explainer, EX-04 in-world documents.

Platform: HA-01 organisations, HA-03 assignments, HA-04 due-soon and overdue emails, HA-05 records, HA-06 certificates, HA-07 policy acknowledgement, HA-09 dashboards, HA-11 organisation profile, HA-15 data protection, HA-17 learner home, HA-19 version stamp on records, HA-23 accessibility pass, HA-26 setup checklist.

### Phase 1: the MVP course (Induction Essentials)

core_00, core_01, core_03, core_04, core_05, core_16, with MG-01 inbox (built in slices, so core_00 and core_01 aren't held up by compose), MG-03 domain drill (inline), MG-07 MFA prompts, MG-08 password manager, MG-09 incident report form, MG-12 redaction, MG-26 incident spread map and MG-27 work desktop. This is enough to sell to an organisation as its induction training and to pilot with real staff.

### Phase 2: the core library and first role paths

core_02, core_06 to core_11, core_15, core_17; role_01, role_02. Platform: HA-02 single sign-on, HA-04 reminders and escalation, HA-08 question banks, HA-24 API and webhooks, HA-10 LMS interoperability, HA-13 facilitated sessions, HA-19 versioning, HA-20 video hosting, HA-21 and BE-07 monthly drills, HA-22 and BE-14 xAPI. Engine-recorded videos (EX-02, MG-21) arrive here.

### Phase 3: everything else

core_12 to core_14; role_03 to role_07; sector_01; localisation (BE-06, HA-16); simulation and report-button data import (HA-12); evaluation (HA-14); licensing (HA-18); the partner role for resellers (HA-25).

## How each module was planned

Each module folder holds four files (`MODULE_TEMPLATE.md`): a module card, the concepts (the source of truth), the game design and the assessment. The writing and review process for turning a plan into a game follows the existing loops in `AGENTS.md`: plan, adversarial review, implement, playtest. The awareness course is a fourth kind of game alongside the campaign, the Security-Informed Safety games and the labs; its aims are set out in `01_LEARNING_DESIGN.md` and should go into every reviewer's prompt.
