Episode shape: full = 6 stages (`brief, osint, write, flip, defend, debrief`), identical to built E1 plus the ENG-01 flip aftermath; defend-only = 3 stages. One defend set `e8_used_account`, 7 items (4 real signs: sent-item takeover, unrequested reset, MFA push, ransomware note; 3 genuine: colleague slip, planned maintenance, parcel). ## What works

- **One lesson, stated for Monday.** Every real sign gets the same first move: report it and leave the device as it is. The Monday card and both host completion lines say it in a sentence a clerk can repeat. The variety of signs (sent item, reset, push, ransom note) all resolve to that one move, so the spread does not fracture the lesson.
- **The flip teaches, it does not restate.** ENG-01's aftermath moves the learner into Ayaan's seat and shows the consequence of the click (a sent item they did not write, a sign-in from abroad). The debrief then adds genuinely new procedure, the five questions a report answers and the first-steps/recovery card, rather than repeating the attack beat.
- **It fixes the built set's urgency trap (C1-M2).** The relevant pretext comes in a calm low-urgency variant (`e8_pretext_reauth_calm`) as well as a high one, and the ask has a calm variant, so a careful learner can both land the phish and clear the `urgencyCalibrated` advisory (target `advisoryMax: "medium"`). In the built episodes the only landing pretext was always high, so the meter nagged about something unfixable. E8 does not repeat that.
- **The flip blames the right person (avoids C1-M1).** Attack target and flip victim are both Ayaan Warsame; `flipDefenderLine`, `flipIntro` and `flipAftermath.victimName` all name him. No mismatch.
- **Facts match the module.** The five-questions card matches `core_04/concepts.md` ("Say what, when, where, who, and what you've done", and the five-question list); the first-steps card matches the "leave it as it is / don't switch off unless told / unplug the cable or turn off Wi-Fi and leave it on" guidance; row 5 stops the ransomware item at "report at once and leave it", with "core_07 owns the rest", exactly as the module boundary requires. The colleague-slip item mirrors "help a colleague report their own slip".
- **No-blame register is clean.** Nothing says "you fell for it". Every `ifTrusted`, the `noBlameLine`, the host lines and the genuine-item `whyFine` describe consequences or reassurance without judging the reader. Genuine items score full on Trust or Report, so a cautious learner is never marked down.
- **The awkward genuine item is present.** Row 6 (planned maintenance from IT's real address, also on the intranet) is the calibration mirror against row 5's ransom note: both are "about IT and your systems", so "anything IT is bad" does not win. A softer second pair (row 2 reset vs row 6 maintenance, both from the genuine service-desk address) reinforces it. (Caveat under MI below: because the `awareness` profile scores genuine items full whether trusted or reported, the calibration point is carried by the immediate feedback, not by the score; this is by design and matches the built sets.)
- **Validators and dialoguelint pass**, and the local-tell plumbing (`channelTells` + `flagChips` + `skillNames` + per-message `factorStrings.<id>.redFlag`) is wired the way the spec's local-tell pattern prescribes, with no change to `CHANNEL_TELLS`/`SKILL_NAMES`.

## Blocker

None. The episode validates, dialoguelint passes, the lesson is single and clear, the no-blame contract holds, and both variants are solvable. The three majors below should all be fixed before ship, but none breaks solvability, scoring or the register.

## Major

**MA1 (orchestrator finding 1). The composed phish reads "the the SSO move this month ... this month".** Episode-local.
`e8_pretext_reauth_calm` (`scenario.json.erb:540`, attack fragment `e8/attack.json:52-55`) is "We are moving **the** `{portal}` to a new provider **this month**, so you may be asked to sign in again." The `{portal}` slot is filled by `sso_migration`, whose label is "**the SSO move this month**" (`:481`). The two collide:

> We are moving the the SSO move this month to a new provider this month, so you may be asked to sign in again.

`e8_pretext_reauth` (high, `:527`) has the same "the `{portal}`" and so also composes "the the SSO move this month to a new provider ..." (it lacks the trailing "this month", so only the article doubles). I checked every template × fact combination: `{portal}` is the only reference slot and `sso_migration` is its only filler, so these two pretexts are the whole exposure; the From slots compose cleanly ("Quayholt IT Service Desk <it-servicedesk@quayholt.example>"). This is worse than a stray typo because the hints ("Keep the ask calm"), the calm-path design and the bridge all steer the learner to `e8_pretext_reauth_calm`, the exact variant that doubles both words, and the sentence is then shown again in the flip. Fix (content, both the erb and the fragment, keep them in step): make the label a plain noun phrase and drop the leading article from the templates, e.g. label `"single sign-on"` and pretexts "We are moving `{portal}` to a new provider this month ..." → "We are moving single sign-on to a new provider this month ...". The `reduceNote`/display label "the SSO move this month" can stay as intel copy, but the slotted `label` must read grammatically inside "We are moving ___ to a new provider".

**MA2 (orchestrator finding 2). `flipIntro` is ungrammatical.** Episode-local.
`strings.flipIntro` (`scenario.json.erb:404`, fragment `e8/defend.json:192`): "This is your email as Ayaan, the claims handler you sent it to, met it, and this time he signed in." The "as" binds to "your email", so it first reads "your email as Ayaan", then "met it" has no clear subject. Intended sense: the email, as Ayaan met it. Shown at a key beat (the hand-over into the victim's seat). Fix (content): recast, e.g. "This is your email as Ayaan met it. He is the claims handler you sent it to, and this time he signed in." or "Here is your email as it reached Ayaan, the claims handler you sent it to. This time he signed in."

**MA3 (orchestrator finding 4, second half). The aftermath sign-in prompt card renders dark, low-contrast.** Engine (flip-view/CSS).
In `flip-view.js` the push item (`fa_push`) is drawn by `renderKindSurface`, which emits the inbox-triage push markup (`.tri-kind-push` / `.tri-push-details`, `inbox-triage-minigame.css:421-425`), whose label colour is `var(--ui-text-dim, #a8b2c8)` (a pale value meant for the triage minigame's dark surface). `awareness-skin.css:26-30` already re-points `--ui-*` inside `.aw-flip`/`.aw-debrief` precisely because "embedded surfaces ... landed dark-on-dark navy (E5-4)", but that re-point does not fully cover the push card surface dropped into the aftermath, so the prompt (account, "Unknown location, outside the UK", device) reads low-contrast. This is the card that shows the headline sign (a sign-in from abroad), so legibility matters. Fix (engine): extend the flip/debrief `--ui-*` and surface-background re-point in `awareness-skin.css` to the aftermath's `.aw-aftermath-item .tri-kind-push` surface (the same class of fix as the E5-4 note already in the file), or give the aftermath push its own light styling. Confirm against a render.

## Minor

**MI1 (orchestrator finding 3). Aftermath heading time disagrees with the Sent item's time.** Episode-local.
`flipAftermath.heading` "Now you are Ayaan, and it is ten past three" (`scenario.json.erb:374`, `full_only.json:12`) is 15:10, but the `fa_sent` item's time is "15:08" (`:384`), as is defend row 1 (`:139`). Two minutes apart in the same view. Fix: set the heading to "just gone three" (vague, survives a later time change) or set `fa_sent`/`e8_sent_invoice` time to "15:10".

**MI2 (orchestrator finding 4, first half). The aftermath Sent address is in the pixel font, the flip's other addresses are not.** Engine (flip-view/CSS).
`aftermathEmailRow` in `flip-view.js:103-111` renders the `to` value in a plain `<dd>` styled only by `.aw-aftermath-fields dd` (`awareness-skin.css:99`), which sets no `font-family`, so it inherits the flip's `--se-font-readable`, which `fonts.css:63` defines as the pixel font GNF (`--se-font-readable: var(--se-font-pixel)`). Every other address in the same view, the mail-reader's From/To and the triage cards' `.tri-address`, uses `--se-font-address` (Source Sans 3, `fonts.css:66`). So `claims-client@marsden-holloway.example` lands in pixel type beside non-pixel addresses. Fix (engine): give the aftermath email row's address/`to` field a class set to `font-family: var(--se-font-address)`, matching `.tri-address`. (Aside, not this review's scope: the `--se-font-readable: var(--se-font-pixel)` default means the awareness skin's "no pixel font" intent relies on a fallback that never fires; worth a separate look, but it is not E8-specific.)

**MI3 (orchestrator finding 5). Seven items against a target of about six; cut the parcel.** Episode-local.
The set has 7 items; the spec (§6, §11, open question 2) targets "about 6 to 7" and marks row 7 (`e8_parcel`) as cuttable. Recommendation: cut it. Its lesson, a plain-ordinary genuine item ("not everything in a bad hour is an attack"), overlaps the calibration already carried by row 6 (maintenance) and the general "genuine scores full" feedback; three genuine of seven over-weights the ordinary; and the full episode is tight at 15 minutes with OSINT, write, flip and a 7-card debrief. Cutting it leaves 4 real signs + 2 genuine, keeps the row 5/row 6 mirror and the awkward genuine, and trims the defend round and the debrief. If the parcel stays, time the full episode in a playtest and confirm 15 minutes holds.

**MI4 (the §8b side-check). The spec's generator host line is stale; the generator is already fixed.** Needs-user (spec/doc).
The built defend-only host "before" line is 30 words ("You're covering a colleague's inbox. They clicked a convincing email, so someone else is using their account. Some signs are real, some are ordinary. Report anything that doesn't sit right.", `gen_e8_e17_shells.py:213`, matching the built ink), under the dialoguelint 30-word cap (commit 61eab210e). But spec §8b `LINES[('e8','defend')]` still shows the original 50-word version. So **yes, the spec needs the same edit**: update §8b's `before` string to the 30-word line the generator now holds, so the pilot's spec and generator agree. No shipped file is affected; this is doc hygiene for the next reader.

**MI5 (validator). `phish_takeover_victim_landed` is set but never read.** Episode-local.
The validator warns (issues 23–24) that `phishTargets[0].outcome.setGlobal.phish_takeover_victim_landed` is declared (`:78`) and set (`:792`) but nothing reads it: the task mappings key off `phish_takeover_victim_attempts > 0` instead (`:859`). A dead global that "looks like it matters". Fix: drop the setter (and the declaration), or wire a line/mapping to it, as the validator suggests (cf. m05 `mission_priority`).

**MI6 (C1-m10 analogue). The conclusion screen and a host line assume a send happened.** Episode-local.
Aim 2 `conclusionScreen.body` "you sent one email, then sat in the colleague's seat ..." (`:54`) and the host completion line "you sent the email, then you sorted what it set off" (full ink `hub`) read as true only on a landed/sent path, yet the runner has `flipNoSend`/`flipMissed` strings for the other paths. If a learner can reach the debrief without sending, the wording is wrong for them. Fix: word it for every path ("You've seen both sides of a click ...") or confirm in a playtest that the full episode cannot conclude without a send.

**MI7. "Report it and leave it be" undersells the password step for the takeover signs.** Episode-local.
Both host completion lines and the defend-host "after" line give the single takeaway as "the one move that works for every real sign is the same, report it and leave it be" (full ink; `gen` LINES after-line). For the account-takeover signs (row 1 sent item, row 2 reset) the correct move is report **and change your password on the real site**, which concept card B and the Monday card both state. "Leave it be" fits the device/ransomware signs but reads oddly against "change your password". Fix: either widen the headline ("report it and let the team act") or keep "leave the device as it is" for the device signs and not as the universal move.

**MI8 (C1-m27 analogue). The defend-only shell carries dead flip strings.** Episode-local (generator).
`strings.flipMissedHeading` and `strings.flipLandedNoTells` are present in the defend-only shell (`awareness_e8_after_the_click_defend/scenario.json.erb:316-317`), which has no flip stage and never shows them. Harmless but dead; a later author could wire them by mistake. This is generator behaviour inherited from the built defend-only shells, so fix it in the generator's string copy if the built shells are ever cleaned up, not by hand-editing E8 alone.

## Note (by design, not a finding)

Under `scoringProfile: "awareness"` a genuine item scores full whether trusted or reported, so a learner who reports every single item still scores 100. "Refusing everything" therefore wins on the score; the calibration lesson (don't over-report the maintenance notice) is carried entirely by the immediate per-item feedback and the debrief reasoning cards, not by the number. This is the deliberate no-blame contract shared by every built awareness set, not an E8 defect. Flagged only so the user knows the "awkward genuine item so refusing everything doesn't win" criterion is met pedagogically, not through scoring.

## Verdict

Ship-worthy as the pilot once the two content majors and the two engine findings are cleared; none is structural, and the design is in several respects ahead of the built set (it fixes the C1 urgency trap and names the flip victim correctly). MA1 and MA2 are quick content edits to the fragment and the full erb (keep them in step); MA3 and MI2 are flip-view/CSS fixes that the ENG-01 owner should take with a render to confirm, since this review did not run the browser. MI3 (cut the parcel) and the 15-minute timing want a playtest. MI4 is a one-line spec sync. The lesson is clear and repeatable, the facts match core_04 and core_01 LO6, the no-blame register is clean, and both variants validate and pass dialoguelint. Recommend a C2 pass after the fixes, with a timed full-episode playtest to confirm the duration and the two rendering fixes.
