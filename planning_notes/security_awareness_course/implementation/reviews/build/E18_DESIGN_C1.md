## What works

- **Content rules are honoured, on screen.** The spray console shows opened accounts as a count ("3 of 5 accounts opened"), never a named colleague; the stuff rows mask the password (`......`) and use generic account-type labels ("A work email account", "A supplier portal login", "A staff wiki login"), not people; no row ever pairs a specific candidate with a specific account, so no working credential is revealed. The candidate strings on screen (`Calderbank2026`, `Verrow!`, `Autumn2026`) are deliberately-predictable fictional guesses built from the practice's own website words, which is the teaching point, matching E9's guesser that "tries them first". The scope note and brief state "No real password is ever typed or stored" in plain sight. No tool, command, rule syntax or lockout threshold appears anywhere; the spray note says "no single account ever locks" and the debrief says accounts "lock after a run of bad sign-ins", both without a number.
- **It teaches what E9 does not.** E9 (core_03, single-person) guesses one named colleague's personal facts and shows one reuse step. E18 adds the organisation-scale layer: harvesting a whole roster and the address pattern from public pages, building a word list from the company website, password spraying (one password across every account, no single lockout) and credential stuffing at scale. The two concept cards (spraying, stuffing) and the campaign console carry concepts E9's rig never shows, and the attack half does not repeat the single-person guesser. The E9 overlap is clean.
- **One lesson, stated for Monday.** The habit resolves to one sentence: a different password for every account, not a word off the website, kept in a manager. The "one habit" concept card, the flip defender line, the no-blame line and both host completion lines all land on it, and the Monday card turns it into three concrete home actions (manager, breach check, 2SV on email first) that match the core_03 reference-card home section word for word.
- **Facts match the module.** Spraying, stuffing, the "different password per account" fix, the breach-check-uses-email-not-password point, Have I Been Pwned, and the organisation controls (lockout, MFA everywhere, banned-password list, breach monitoring) all track `core_03/concepts.md` ("Why reuse is the big one", the glossary, the organisation slots). The organisation controls are voiced as the provider's ("These are set up for you; you don't have to switch them on"), not as staff duties, exactly as the spec and role_03 require.
- **No-blame register is clean.** Nothing says "you fell for it". The no-blame line normalises reuse as "ordinary and common ... not a sign anyone was careless"; every `whyFine`, `falseAlarmCost`, `ifTrusted` and host line describes consequences or reassurance without judging the reader. Under the `awareness` profile a cautious learner who reports a genuine notice still scores full, and the cards' `whyFine` say so.
- **The mirror pair does its job.** Rows 1 and 2 are the same surface (a lockout email), one genuine (provider, asks for nothing, names the service-desk route) and one fake (look-alike `merrin-sallow.example`, unlock-through-a-link under a deletion threat), so "a lockout alert arrived" is not itself the tell. Rows 3/4 (sign-in alert) and 5/6 (breach) repeat the real-against-fake shape. The discriminator is correctly "genuine notices never need you to type a password to unlock or check", stated in the brief, the defend intro and every fake's per-flag note.
- **`truth.kindLine` / `truth.missLabel` are correctly absent.** These keys exist for a set where a genuine item should still be reported (a genuine-but-report design). E18 scores all three genuine items full on Trust or Verify and is never-marked-down on Report, so no item is genuine-but-report, and the keys are rightly not used. No finding here; flagged only because the orchestrator asked.
- **Validators and dialoguelint pass, and the local-tell plumbing is wired correctly:** the three `fake_*` ids sit in `channelTells` with `flagChips` labels, set-level `skillNames`, and a per-message `factorStrings.<id>.redFlag` sentence on each flagging card, with no change to the shared `CHANNEL_TELLS`/`SKILL_NAMES`, exactly as the local-tell pattern prescribes.

## Blocker

None. The episode validates, dialoguelint passes, both variants are solvable, the no-blame contract holds, and every content rule (no technique, no real breach data, no working credential, masked stuffing rows, count-only opens) is honoured on screen. The two majors below are both visible text defects on the full-episode flip screen; neither breaks solvability, scoring, the register or a content rule, but both should be fixed before ship.

## Major

**MA1 (orchestrator finding 1, known). The flip step on the strip reads "Your message", but E18 writes no message.** Episode-local.
`social.awarenessEpisode.stepLabels` (`scenario.json.erb:319-321`, `e18/full_only.json:61`) sets only `"guess": "Campaign"`. The `flip` step therefore falls to the strip default, `flip: 'Your message'` (`episode-runner/step-strip.js:10`), and the render confirms it: the strip shows "4 Your message" in both the flip and the debrief shots. E18 sends no email and makes no call; the flip is the learner's own inbox after the sweep (heading "Now it's your inbox", aftermath "A few minutes later, at your desk"). "Your message" is simply wrong for this episode and is on screen for the whole second half. Fix (content): add a `flip` label to `stepLabels` in both the full erb and `full_only.json`, matching the own-seat framing, e.g. `"flip": "Your inbox"` (or "Your seat" / "The alerts"). The defend-only shell has no flip stage, so it needs no change.

**MA2. The flip subtitle shows the generic ENG-02 default, which misdescribes the attack and clashes with the own-seat framing; the authored `flipIntro` is never shown.** Episode-local.
On the `guess` flip path the view prints `strings.flipGuessIntro` as the lead paragraph (`flip-view.js:206-209`) and uses `strings.flipIntro` only on the non-guess path (`:215`). E18 authors `flipIntro` ("You watched one password open the predictable accounts across the whole practice ... your own seat, a few minutes later, with the alerts starting to arrive.") but not `flipGuessIntro`, so the authored line never renders and the default shows instead (`flip-view.js:53`): **"One reused password was enough. This is what the owner of that account sees next."** The render confirms this is the on-screen subtitle. Two problems: it attributes the whole result to *reuse*, when E18's lead attack is *spraying* a company word across everyone (reuse is only the stuffing half); and "the owner of that account sees next" is a third-person, named-victim framing that contradicts E18's own-seat design (heading "Now it's your inbox", `flipAftermath.victimName: "you"`, aftermath "the first two that reached you"). Fix (content, both the erb and the fragment): author `strings.flipGuessIntro` with the own-seat text that `flipIntro` already carries (the `flipIntro` wording is good; it is just keyed wrong for a guess flip). Either rename `flipIntro` to `flipGuessIntro`, or add `flipGuessIntro` and drop the now-dead `flipIntro`. Confirm against a render that the subtitle then matches the heading.

## Minor

**MI1. One defend-only host line is exactly 30 words; the brief asks for under 30.** Needs-user (generator-owned).
The defend-only "before the task" line (`scenarios/awareness_e18_credential_attacks_at_scale_defend/ink/ama_owusu.ink`): "Your own seat now. After a sweep the alerts come thick and fast, some real, some fakes. Report the fakes, and never type a password to unlock or check anything." is 30 words; the reviewer brief sets host lines "under 30 words". Dialoguelint's threshold is `>30`, so it passes the lint, but it sits on the line. The ink is generated by `gen_e8_e17_shells.py` (`LINES[('e18','defend')]`), which the Do-not list says not to edit, so this is a user/generator call. Fix (two words): e.g. drop "and fast" ("the alerts come thick, some real, some fakes") to land at 28.

**MI2. The word list builds three slot candidates but the spray only tries two of them.** Episode-local (observation).
`guess.wordList.slots` builds `project` (`Calderbank2026`), `motto` (`DesignThatLasts1`) and `place` (`Verrow!`), all shown as "found" in the console, but `guess.spray.passes` runs only `pass_project` (the project slot) and `pass_season` (the baked `Autumn2026` literal), so `DesignThatLasts1` and `Verrow!` are listed and never tried. It reads as an attacker assembling a list bigger than the passes shown, which is defensible, but a learner may wonder why two "found" candidates are skipped. Optional fix: add a third pass for one of them, or trim the word list to the slots that are actually sprayed. Not blocking; the point (a word off the website opens accounts) lands either way.

**MI3. Timing not verified live.** Needs-user.
The ~15-minute full / ~8-minute defend budgets rest on the spec's per-stage table and the render, not a timed run. The campaign console is new (ENG-02b) and the OSINT stage collects five facts across five pages; worth one timed playtest to confirm the full episode holds at 15 minutes before ship. No change implied if it holds.

## Verdict

Ship after MA1 and MA2. The episode is on-model: the content rules are kept on screen (count-only opens, masked stuffing rows, no working credential, no technique), it teaches genuinely new material over E9, the lesson is single and repeatable, the no-blame register is clean, the mirror pair is fair, and both shells validate and lint green. The two majors are both text defects on the flip screen (a wrong step label and a generic subtitle that misreads the attack and the seat), both one-line content fixes in the erb and the fragment. The three minors are polish and a timing check. No blocker; no content-rule slip.
