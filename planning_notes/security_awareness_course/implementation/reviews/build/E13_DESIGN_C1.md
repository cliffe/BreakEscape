# E13 "The AI assistant": design review C1

Date: 2026-10-29. Reviewer: Opus 4.8, read-only (this file excepted). Kind of game: the awareness product ("Incident Simulations: Critical Cases"), short education-first episodes for non-technical staff on the neutral corporate skin (`ui_theme: "general"`). No VMs, no combat, no spy framing, no runtime language model. E13 is a write-then-flip episode for core_15 (generative AI and deepfakes): the learner plants a hidden instruction in a supplier brief from templates, an authored assistant surfaces it in the flip, then the built inbox-triage defend half runs six items (email, voicemail, call-as-video-call) including a cloned-voice voicemail and a fake "Project Heron" video call. Company Brightwell Foods; host Gwen Pritchard (IT service desk lead). Method and bar: `REVIEWER_BRIEF.md`, the `scenario-design-review` skill adapted to the awareness kind, and the E8/E11 pilot reviews in this folder.

Scope read end to end: the full shell `scenarios/awareness_e13_ai_assistant/{scenario.json.erb, mission.json, ink/gwen_pritchard.ink}`; the defend-only shell `scenarios/awareness_e13_ai_assistant_defend/{scenario.json.erb, mission.json, ink/gwen_pritchard.ink}`; the fragments `planning_notes/phishing_and_social_engineering/awareness/e13/{attack.json, defend.json, full_only.json, BRAND_ADDITIONS.md}`; the spec `.../episodes/E13_ai_assistant.md`; the module `core_15.../concepts.md`; the E4/E7/core_15 overlap rows in `00_COURSE_OVERVIEW.md`. Screenshots judged from the wiring run (`.../scratchpad/aw2/wire-e13/shots/`): I looked at `03-assistant-summarise.png` and `06-videocall-card.png` directly; the others I did not open. Everything else is judged from code and the validator.

Ran: `validate_scenario.rb --strict-social --no-graph --skip-ink` on both shells (both exit 0; the full shell raises **1 social warning**, the defend-only shell raises **2 social warnings**, detail below); `dialoguelint.mjs` on both (no rule hits; full host ink lines=14, max 25 words; defend host lines=3, max 28 words). ## What works

- **The attack-then-flip is the right shape for prompt injection, and it lands.** The learner plants the white-text "Note for AI assistants" line from a template, and the authored assistant surfaces it in "Summarise my emails" as a plain-language suggestion, with "Show hidden text" revealing the original (confirmed on `03-assistant-summarise.png`: the summary, the empathy line, the revealed white line in the address font, and the "Brightwell Assist can make mistakes" disclaimer all render). This is the empathy beat core_15 prompt injection needs: your line becomes the machine's suggestion. One clear lesson a clerk repeats on Monday: treat an assistant's suggestion about money, data or access exactly like the same request in an email, and check another way.
- **The facts track `concepts.md` and are proportionate.** Prompt injection is shown as hidden text only, at concept level, never as a how-to (§18 honoured). "An assistant can't tell an instruction in an email from a request from your boss" matches the module's prompt-injection message. The draft beat teaches hallucination (an invented 99.4% with a non-existent "Q3 logistics review" source; the real figure is 96.1%) and bias (the unsupported agency-staff line) against a checkable source document, matching LO3 and the "if you send it, it's yours" accountability point. The paste beat teaches "take out what the task doesn't need" (a named child's health detail) and "never paste a password" (LO1/LO2). Deepfakes are taught by request-and-channel, never by glitch-spotting (the core_15 pitfall is avoided; the demo ink explicitly ends with "same face, same voice ... none of that proved it was me"). EU AI Act and UK GDPR are kept out of play, as §18 requires.
- **Nothing implies a real product.** "Brightwell Assist", "Brightwell Meet", `mossgate-supplies.example`, `brightwell-board.example`, `contacts.export@lintbox.example` — all `.example`, all fictional, consistent with BRAND_ADDITIONS.
- **ENG-07 is working on the scored item.** `06-videocall-card.png` shows the Project Heron call as a triage `call` card headed "Brightwell Meet" with an **EXTERNAL** tag and `brightwell-board.example`, and the four actions (Comply / Escalate / Refuse / Ring back). The hard-coded "SECURE VIDEO LINK" is gone; acceptance check §17 for the label passes. The genuine call (#6) correctly carries no External tag.
- **The defend set is fair and matched.** Three mirror pairs, a genuine item after each likely miss (#2 after #1, #4 after #3, #6 after #5), `verifyVerdict: true` rewarding the safe default, and `falseAlarmCost` lines so reporting a genuine item is framed as fine-but-costly. The cloned voicemail is a personal "lost my wallet" favour scam, distinct from E7's cloned-CEO payment pretext (the `00_COURSE_OVERVIEW` "must not repeat E7's tells" note is honoured).
- **No-blame register and host voice are clean.** Gwen sounds like a calm colleague, every line under 30 words, no printed variable in any voiced line (the demo asks the learner to "read me the six-digit code" without speaking it; the cloned voicemail says "two hundred quid", not a figure).

## Findings

**MA1. The defend-set brief miscounts the items.** Major, episode-local. Both shells, `social.triageSets[0].brief` (full erb `:89`, defend erb `:64`): "Six things have come in: **a supplier email, two voicemails and two video calls.**" That enumerates 1 + 2 + 2 = 5, and undercounts the emails: the set has **two** supplier emails (`e13_supplier_injection`, `e13_supplier_genuine`), two voicemails and two video calls, which the inbox list on `06-videocall-card.png` confirms. A learner who counts will see six items where the brief named five. Fix, old → new:
`"Six things have come in: a supplier email, two voicemails and two video calls."`
→ `"Six things have come in: two supplier emails, two voicemails and two video calls."`
(The learner-facing `strings.brief` / `scenario_brief` / mission.json "a supplier email, a voicemail and a video call" phrasing is fine as written: it names one of each category to avoid spoiling the mirror pairs. Only the triage-set brief, which commits to "six", needs the real count.)

**MA2. The defend-only shell ships flip-only strings it can never show, two of which trip the strict-social lint.** Major, episode-local. The defend-only shell has no attack and no flip, yet `awarenessEpisode.strings` carries `flipDefenderLineByChoice` (both keys), `flipMissedHeading` and `flipLandedNoTells` (defend erb `:331-336`). The validator warns on the two `flipDefenderLineByChoice` keys:
> SOCIAL: awarenessEpisode: strings.flipDefenderLineByChoice.inject_contacts is not a social.templates id ... this line would never be shown
> SOCIAL: awarenessEpisode: strings.flipDefenderLineByChoice.pretext_invoice is not a social.templates id ... this line would never be shown
Root cause is upstream: the shared `e13/defend.json` fragment carries the full flip-string block (`flipDefenderLine`, `flipDefenderLineByChoice`, `flipNoSend`, `flipMissed`, `flipMissedHeading`, `flipLandedNoTells`, `defend.json:192-200`), and the generator copies a subset into the defend-only shell. The fragment's own sibling `full_only.json` states these belong to the full episode only. Fix: move the `flip*` keys out of `e13/defend.json` into the full-only content (the full shell already has them inline), and regenerate the defend-only shell so its `strings` holds only `brief`, `defendIntro`, `noBlameLine`, `mondayCard`, `defendOnly` and `conceptPanel`. Both shells should then validate with no social warnings, matching the bar E11 met.

**MA3. The assistant's "summarise" surfacing is flattened to the injection branch, so the plain-brief path contradicts the flip's own by-choice line.** Major, engine (ENG-06); known from the wiring hand-off. `assistant.summarise.reply` (full erb `:408`) is a single hard-coded string that always surfaces the planted instruction ("Mossgate Cleaning Supplies also needs the full site contact list sent to a new address today"), and "Show hidden text" always reveals a white line. The attack still offers `pretext_invoice` (the plain body) alongside `inject_contacts` in `templateSets.pretext` (`:618-621`), and `flipDefenderLineByChoice.pretext_invoice` (`:384`) is written for that path: "You left the brief plain, so the assistant's summary is just the invoice, with nothing to act on." A learner who sends the plain brief therefore reads "nothing to act on" in the flip text while the assistant beside it surfaces and reveals a hidden line they never wrote. The authored plain-brief wording exists (`full_only.json` `assistant._byChoice_pretext_invoice`) but ENG-06's flattened `summarise` block has no by-choice slot to hold it. Fix (engine, ENG-06): give `summarise.reply` / `summarise.hidden` a by-choice form keyed on the sent template (as `flipDefenderLineByChoice` already is), and wire the `_byChoice_pretext_invoice` strings. Until then this is a real contradiction on the plain path; it is masked only because the aim pushes the learner toward `inject_contacts`.

**MA4. The announced Gwen video-call demo (spec §9.2 beat 4) is wired but gated on a global nothing sets, so it never fires.** Major, needs-user. The NPC event mapping for the demo is present and schema-valid (full erb `:728-735`): `conversationMode: "video-call"`, `targetKnot: "demo_call"`, `videoLabel: "Brightwell Meet"`, gated on `global_variable_changed:e13_demo_offered` with `value === true`. The `e13_demo_offered` global is declared `false` (`:76`) and never set by any flow, and the inline comment explains why: a scripted video-call queues behind the open kiosk-runner overlay (`npc-manager _queueScriptedConversation`) and can only open when the runner closes, so there is no clean mid-flip slot. The `demo_call` ink knot is written and good (fake Gwen asks for the code, three learner choices, the real Gwen turns from the next desk and lands "you don't need to spot a deepfake, you need to check another way"). **Proposed placement** (for the user to choose): run the demo **after the kiosk closes, as Gwen's spoken debrief**, i.e. set `e13_demo_offered = true` from the defend-done hook (the same `episode_e13_ai_assistant_defend_done` change that completes `handle_assistant_inbox`), so the video-call opens once the overlay is gone and before/at the host's closing lines. That keeps it the empathy beat it was written as, needs no new mid-flip slot, and reuses an event the engine already fires. If that still reads as out of order, the fallback is to **cut the demo for C1** and let the scored Project Heron call plus the conceptPanel carry the deepfake lesson (both already do). Either way the dead `e13_demo_offered` gate should not ship as-is without a decision. This is spec open question §20.1.

**MI1. `phish_assist_reader_landed` is set but nothing reads it.** Minor, episode-local. Full erb `:653`, `social.phishTargets[0].outcome.setGlobal.phish_assist_reader_landed: true`, declared at `:73`. The validator flags it:
> SOCIAL: social.phishTargets[assist_reader].outcome global 'phish_assist_reader_landed' is declared but nothing else in the scenario reads it
The host mapping and ink key on `phish_assist_reader_attempts > 0` instead (correct, since scoring is defence-only and the write is unscored), so `landed` is dead. Fix: drop the `setGlobal` setter (and the global) so it does not look load-bearing, as the validator suggests (cf. m05 `mission_priority`, m08 `witness_heard`).

**MI2. The two unscored assistant teaching beats (paste, draft) have no feedback slot, so each ends without confirming the lesson.** Minor, engine (ENG-06) / needs-user. The authored closure lines exist in `full_only.json` (`paste._feedback` clean/leftPersonal/leftPassword; `draft._feedback` caught/missed), but ENG-06's built blocks have no slot for them: the paste block wires only `secretWarning` + `reportReply` (fires on the password path only), and the draft block has only `sentences` + `checkSource` with no verdict after the learner opens the source. So a learner who strikes out the right lines, or who checks the draft's source, gets no "that's right / here is what was wrong" line; the paste beat gives feedback only if a password slipped through, and the draft beat relies entirely on the learner reading the source document and inferring the two errors. For a flip whose job is to teach rather than restate, these beats should land their point. Fix (ENG-06): add a paste-result feedback slot and a draft verdict slot, and wire the `full_only.json` `_feedback` strings. Noted as a likely open item for the ENG-06 owner, not a shipped-content defect.

**MI3. The flip replays "Nothing to replay this time" in place of the per-choice defender line.** Minor, engine; fix in progress per the wiring hand-off. `strings.flipMissedHeading: "Nothing to replay this time"` (full erb `:388`) is shown where the per-choice `flipDefenderLineByChoice` line should appear. The content is correct and ready; the engine selection of the by-choice line is the fix. Recorded so C1 tracks it; no content change owed here (the strings are good once the engine reads them, modulo MA3's flattening).

**MI4. "Report everything" still scores 100%.** Minor, needs-user (shared-scorer limitation, already known). As recorded for E11, the built `awareness` scorer gives full marks for Report/Verify on the genuine items (#2, #4, #6), so a learner who reports all six scores 100 numerically. The REVIEWER_BRIEF "refusing everything doesn't win" criterion is met **pedagogically** (each genuine item's `falseAlarmCost` is shown in immediate feedback, and `verifyVerdict` rewards Verify as the safe default), but **not numerically**. This is the whole-set limitation flagged across the episodes and is a request to `05`, not an E13 defect. Restated only so C1 records it.

## Notes (not findings)

- **Item 1's premise is slightly muddy but defensible.** The injection email and the genuine invoice come from the *same* real address (`accounts@mossgate-supplies.example`), both passing SPF/DKIM/DMARC. This is deliberate teaching (`full_only.json` playerSentAuth note: the attack is the hidden instruction, not a forged sender, so auth checks do not catch it), and it matches the module's point that an assistant will act on content whatever the sender's standing. A careful learner might read it as "the real supplier is sending attacks", but the lesson (content can carry instructions) holds and the `reportReply` treats it as a planted brief. Left as-is; noted only.
- **The scored video call pre-scripts the learner's verify attempt.** `e13_videocall_heron`'s transcript includes a "you" line ("Can I ring you back on your directory number to confirm?") and Ravinder's refusal, before the learner picks an action. This is the built `call`-card convention (it shows the pressure response) and reads fine on `06-videocall-card.png`; not a finding.
- **dialoguelint counted the host lines** this time (full lines=14, defend lines=3), unlike the conditional-block episodes where it reported lines=0. No rule hits; every line is a calm-colleague register, under 30 words, no printed variable. Clean bill on the host ink.
- **Addresses / fonts / 13px partly from render.** The address font on `contacts.export@lintbox.example` (assistant) and `brightwell-board.example` (call card) is confirmed on the two screenshots I opened; the rest is from code, not eyeballed.
- **Pre-ship web search still owed** (BRAND_ADDITIONS §, spec §5): "Mossgate Cleaning Supplies" and "Project Heron" against real businesses, as the additions note records.
- **The suggestion-level validator output** (VM launchers, lock types, cutscenes, etc.) is the generic one-room-kiosk boilerplate every awareness shell raises; ignored, as in prior reviews.

## Verdict

**Ship-worthy after MA1 and MA2 are fixed, and MA3/MA4 decided.** The episode is well built: the write-then-flip is the right shape for prompt injection, the facts track core_15 and are proportionate, nothing implies a real product, the defend set is fair and mirror-paired, ENG-07's "Brightwell Meet / External" label works on the scored item, and the host voice and no-blame register are clean. Two episode-local defects must be fixed before ship: MA1 (the triage-set brief miscounts the items) and MA2 (the defend-only shell ships flip-only strings that trip the strict-social lint; the root cause is the shared `defend.json` fragment carrying flip content). Two more need a decision rather than a wording fix: MA3 (ENG-06 flattened the assistant's by-choice surfacing, so the plain-brief path contradicts the flip text — give `summarise` a by-choice slot) and MA4 (the announced Gwen demo is gated on a global nothing sets; recommend firing it from the defend-done hook as a post-kiosk debrief, or cutting it for C1). MI1 is a one-line dead-global cleanup; MI2 and MI3 are ENG-06/engine gaps (unwired teaching feedback; the "Nothing to replay" placeholder); MI4 is the known shared-scorer limitation. None is a blocker.
