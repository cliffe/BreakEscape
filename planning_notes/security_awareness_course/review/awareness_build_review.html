1. What is built

7full episodes, each about 13 minutes (E3 about 17)
7defend-only variants, each about 8 minutes
24pre and post checks: 6 roles × 2 forms × AB/BA order
1810node tests passing at hand-over; all 38 shells validate

The frame. Every scenario takes place in one training room with a kiosk and a host. The kiosk runs the episode in stages:

  1. Brief and scope note.
  2. Attack (unscored). Research public sources, then build one attack from templates whose blanks only take facts you have found.
  3. Flip. Your own message arrives in a defender's inbox with its tells highlighted.
  4. Defend (scored). Triage messages with Trust, Report, Delete or Verify.
  5. Debrief. The habit to keep, an "On Monday" card, the customer's reporting route and a no-blame line.

The frame has no doors, no combat, no VMs and no language model at runtime.

3. Gaps: the 26 planned modules against the build

LargePartNarrow or touchNone

The built product covers payment fraud, phishing and the help desk well. The biggest gaps:

No "what to do next". No episode covers what to do after a click, a typed password or a payment, though every episode leads up to that moment.
Whole core topics missing. Nothing is built for passwords and managers, data protection, handling information, malware, downloads, physical security, shadow IT, insider risk, AI tool use or acceptable use.
Four roles have no check pair. Developers, customer-facing staff, procurement and healthcare have nothing yet.
Accessibility. Only the social overlays meet the accessibility baseline. There is no text route, no accessibility check in CI and no screen-reader audit. Public-sector buyers will ask for all three.
Engine rough edges every new episode will hit.
  • #trigger_minigame is a stub.
  • The title screen and tutorial still say Hacktivity and spy.
  • Pause does not stop the clock.
  • Name entry accepts ASCII only.
  • The call label "SECURE VIDEO LINK" is hard-coded.
