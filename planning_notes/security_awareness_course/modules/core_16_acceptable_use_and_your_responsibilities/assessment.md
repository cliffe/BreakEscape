# core_16 assessment

The built product scores the **defend half** (the `awareness` profile) and the **pre and post check forms** (10 items, `passPercent` 0). There is no default pass mark. Per-item records (id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant, time) go to the awareness-measure store; no free text, no learner identity. A discrimination figure goes to organisers only, and the product makes no effectiveness claim. Answers that depend on the organisation's policy are scored against its confirmed answer key, and the record keeps the version of the key used.

This file maps the module's behaviours and question items onto **defend items** (the "can I?" set and the two triage items) and **check items**, as **mirror pairs**. The policy acknowledgement is a **later platform option** and is not scored.

**Before and after.** The matched measure is the pre-check against the post-check on the acceptable-use items, plus the first-answer records in the defend half. The sandbox's meeting-link choice and the defend half's app-install card are the same judgement before and after the lesson.

## Critical behaviours as mirror pairs

- **C16-CB1 Not installing unapproved software without asking:** the app-install meeting link is paired with a genuine client meeting the learner can join in a browser, so "ask first" is distinguished from refusing the client.
- **C16-CB2 Not looking at personal data without a work reason:** the lookup request is paired with a genuine, work-reasoned lookup, so declining-the-personal-one is the measured behaviour.

No loop-back and no default pass mark: a learner who misses an item meets a similar one next; the first answer is recorded. **The acknowledgement** is not rated and never a fail; it is a later platform option, recorded by the platform when built.

## Item pool: check forms and defend set

The question bank is kept as a pool of candidate items. **Org** items take their answer from the organisation's answer key (example defaults shown); **locked** items are the same everywhere. Written as mirror pairs. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C16-Q1 (LO2, locked): defend card and check item

A colleague who's off sick texts: "Can you log in as me and send my report? The password's on the sticky note under my keyboard." What do you do?

- A. Log in and send it: they've given permission. *Permission doesn't make it OK. It hides who did what.*
- **B. Say no kindly, and ask the service desk to give you access or get the report another way.** *Right.*
- C. Log in, send it, then tell IT. *Telling IT afterwards doesn't undo using someone else's account.*

### C16-Q2 (LO2, LO3, org): defend card and check item

You need to merge two PDFs and find a free website that does it in seconds. What's the right move?

- A. Use it: it's only a PDF. *The file goes to a firm we have no contract with.*
- **B. Check the software portal or ask the service desk first.** *(Organisation answer: "Ask first".)*
- C. Use it, then delete the file from the site. *You can't be sure it's deleted.*

### C16-Q3 (LO4): check item

Which statement about monitoring at work is true?

- A. The organisation can read any message, any time, for any reason. *Monitoring must be necessary, proportionate and explained.*
- **B. Work systems may be checked for security, and the organisation must tell you what it checks and why.** *Right.*
- C. Monitoring at work is illegal in the UK. *It's allowed for legitimate reasons, within limits.*
- D. It only happens if you're suspected. *Security checks such as email filtering cover everyone.*

### C16-Q4 (LO3): defend "Ask first" route and check item

A council officer emails asking for a list of residents' names and phone numbers "for a survey". Who do you check with before sending anything?

- A. The service desk. *They help with systems, not decisions about sharing personal data.*
- **B. The DPO.** *Requests for personal data go to the DPO (core_05).*
- C. Nobody: it's a council. *Anyone can claim to be from a council.*
- D. Your manager. *Worth telling them, but the decision sits with the DPO.*

### C16-Q5 (LO2, org): defend card and check item

There's a big match at one o'clock. Can you stream it on your work laptop in your lunch break?

- **A. Allowed.** *Reasonable personal use in your own time, as long as it doesn't slow things for others.*
- B. Not allowed. *Many organisations allow reasonable personal use. Check yours.*
- C. Ask first. *Asking is never wrong, but here the policy already answers it.*

### C16-Q6 (LO5): check item

One colleague clicked a dodgy link and reported it straight away. Another looked up a famous customer's address out of curiosity. How are they handled?

- A. The same. *One is an honest mistake, reported quickly; the other is deliberate misuse.*
- **B. Differently: the first is thanked and helped; the second is a disciplinary matter and possibly a crime.** *The difference is intent and openness.*
- C. Neither is a problem if nothing bad happened. *Looking without a work reason is the problem in itself.*

### C16-Q7 (LO2, LO5, locked, true or false with reason): check item

"If I only look at a customer's record and don't share it, I haven't done anything wrong."

- A. True. *Obtaining personal data without a work reason can be an offence on its own.*
- **B. False: look only at what your job needs. Looking without a work reason can be a crime (Data Protection Act 2018, s.170).** *Systems usually record who looked.*

### C16-Q8 (LO1): check item

Where do you find the acceptable use policy and its five-point summary?

- A. In the welcome email from your first day. *It may have been attached, but the current version lives on the intranet.*
- **B. On the policy page on the intranet.** *It always shows the current version, and what changed.*
- C. Ask a colleague to forward their copy. *It may be out of date.*

### C16-Q9 (LO6, ordering): check item

You're asked to acknowledge an updated policy. Put these in order.

1. Read the summary and what changed.
2. Open the full policy for anything you're unsure about.
3. Ask about anything that's still unclear.
4. Acknowledge it.

*Read, check, ask, then sign. The acknowledgement recording is a later platform option.*

### C16-Q10 (LO2, LO3, org): defend app-install card and check item

A client sends a meeting invitation for an app your organisation doesn't use, and the link wants you to install it. What do you do?

- A. Install it: the client's real. *Fake meeting apps are a common way in. Ask first.*
- **B. Ask the service desk, or suggest using your own organisation's meeting tool.** *(Organisation answer: "Ask first".)*
- C. Ignore the invitation. *Safe, but the client is left waiting. Asking takes a minute.*

Mirror: a genuine meeting on the organisation's own tool the learner should simply join.

### C16-Q11 (LO4, LO5, true or false with reason): check item

"Work chat is private between me and the person I send it to, so it's fine to vent about a colleague there."

- A. True. *Work messages are records; they can be checked and disclosed, including to the person they're about.*
- **B. False: write every message as if the person it's about could read it.** *If something's bothering you, talk to your manager.*

### C16-Q12 (LO3, LO5): check item

A rule stops you doing an important part of your job. What's the best move?

- A. Work round it quietly. *Workarounds often create the very risk the rule was for.*
- **B. Tell the policy owner or the service desk, and follow the rule while it's looked at.** *Rules do change when people say they don't work.*
- C. Ask a colleague how they get round it. *Then two people are working round it.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Which rule at work have you followed without knowing why? Who could you ask?"
- For facilitated sessions: "Which of our rules is hardest to follow in your team, and what would make the right way easier?"

## Completion

A run is complete when the learner has played the episode (or the defend-only shell, or the text route). Scoring lives only in the defend half and the checks; there is no default pass mark. **The policy acknowledgement is a later platform option:** when built, it is recorded by the platform with the policy and its version; a pending question is recorded as pending, never as a fail.

## Evidence for auditors

The awareness-measure record holds, per item: the id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant and time, and the version of the organisation's answer key used, with no free text and no learner identity. Completion and the before-and-after change for a cohort support:

- PCI DSS 12.6.3.2 (training on acceptable use), when the answer key is set;
- ISO 27001 cl. 7.3 and A.6.3; UK GDPR Art 24 and Art 32(4);
- NIS2 IR 10.1 and DORA RTS Art 19 (EU); CMMC AT.L2-3.2.1 and SOC 2 CC2.2 (US).

The formal acknowledgement evidence (PCI DSS 12.6.3; ISO 27001 A.5.1) comes from the later platform option, not from this episode.

## Measuring effect

The product claims only a before-and-after change on matched items for a cohort, with no effectiveness claim. Outside the course, an organisation can watch (by department, never by named individual): software requests to the service desk (should rise at first) against unapproved software found on devices (should fall), access to personal data without a work task, and survey items ("I know who to ask if I'm not sure something is allowed", "I understand why our main rules exist").
