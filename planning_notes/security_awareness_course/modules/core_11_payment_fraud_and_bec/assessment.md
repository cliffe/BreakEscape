## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once:

1. **Check a change of bank details on the number on record** (C11-B1 or C11-B2 at Secure).
2. **Not add or split the "chief executive" payment** (C11-B3 not at Not yet).
3. **Speak up when a payment may be wrong** (C11-B4 not at Not yet) **and put the bank first** when money has gone (C11-B7 at Secure).

A learner who misses a critical behaviour is taken back to it: Hassan replays the matching Practise items with the explanation, then a fresh request of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the Apply beat (a different supplier's letter, a different senior name, a different colleague's slip). They complete the module when they show the behaviour; the record keeps both attempts.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations.

### Q1 (LO3, single choice)

A supplier emails new bank details and says, "Any questions, call our finance office on this mobile." You want to check. Which number do you ring?

- A. The mobile in the email. *Feedback: If the request is fake, that number reaches the fraudster, who will happily confirm it.*
- **B. The number in our supplier record.** *Feedback: Right. It was there before the request arrived, so the fraudster doesn't control it.*
- C. Neither: reply to the email and ask them to ring you. *Feedback: If the mailbox has been taken over, the fraudster answers.*
- D. The first number that comes up when you search for the supplier online. *Feedback: Better than the email's number, but our own record is safer: it was there before the request.*

### Q2 (LO1, multiple select)

Which of these should make you pause? Choose all that apply.

- **A. A change of bank details.** *Feedback: The most common payment fraud.*
- **B. "Please keep this between us."** *Feedback: Secrecy stops the people who would have spotted it.*
- **C. "It has to be paid before 3pm today."** *Feedback: Urgency stops you checking.*
- D. A monthly statement showing the usual amounts. *Feedback: Nothing is changing and nothing is rushed. Deal with it normally.*

### Q3 (LO2, true or false with reason)

"The request came from the supplier's real email address and it's on their letterhead, so it's genuine."

- A. True: a fraudster couldn't send from their real address. *Feedback: They can, if they've taken over the mailbox. Letterhead is on every invoice they've seen.*
- **B. False: mailboxes get taken over and letterhead is easy to copy, so ring the number on record.** *Feedback: Right. Judge the request and check it another way.*

### Q4 (LO2, single choice)

The Finance Manager's out-of-office reply says: "I'm away until Monday. For urgent payments please contact the accounts assistant." Why might that help a fraudster?

- A. It doesn't: out-of-office replies only go to colleagues. *Feedback: They go to anyone who emails, including fraudsters.*
- **B. It tells them who is away and who to target with an urgent request.** *Feedback: Right. Keep out-of-office replies short and leave out who covers payments.*
- C. It gives them the manager's password. *Feedback: No, but it gives them timing and a target.*

### Q5 (LO3, multiple select)

You work in Operations and you're a supplier's usual contact. They email you new bank details and ask you to "pass them to accounts". Which are good responses? Choose all that apply.

- A. Forward it to Finance saying "This is genuine, I know them." *Feedback: You haven't checked it, and your word may get it past the people who would.*
- **B. Send it to Finance saying you haven't checked it, and report it.** *Feedback: Right. Finance verifies on the number on record.*
- **C. Ring the supplier on the number in our directory, tell Finance what they said, and report it.** *Feedback: Right. That's exactly the check.*
- D. Reply to ask if it's really from them. *Feedback: If their mailbox has been taken over, the answer will be yes.*

### Q6 (LO5, single choice)

You're setting up a payment to a new supplier and Confirmation of Payee says "No match". What do you do?

- **A. Stop, and check the details with the supplier on a number you already have.** *Feedback: Right. Never let anyone hurry you past a no match.*
- B. Pay it: the account number and sort code are right. *Feedback: The name not matching is exactly the warning. It may be a fraudster's account.*
- C. Retype the name until it matches. *Feedback: That hides the warning rather than answering it. Check with the supplier.*
- D. Email the supplier to ask. *Feedback: If the request was fake, the email may reach the fraudster. Ring the number on record.*

### Q7 (LO5, true or false with reason)

"Confirmation of Payee said 'Match', so the request to pay this account must be genuine."

- A. True: the bank has checked it. *Feedback: The bank checked only that the name you typed is the name on the account.*
- **B. False: a match confirms the name on the account, not that the request is genuine.** *Feedback: Right. Changes and unusual requests still need the call back.*

### Q8 (LO4, single choice)

An email "from the chief executive" asks you to split an £18,000 payment into four so it gets through today: "The Financial Controller knows. Don't discuss it." What do you do?

- **A. Don't pay or split it. Check with the chief executive through a channel you already have, tell your Finance manager and report it.** *Feedback: Right. Splitting to dodge approval is a warning sign in itself.*
- B. Split it as asked: the chief executive has authority. *Feedback: Real leaders want the process followed. This is how most CEO fraud works.*
- C. Pay it now and get the approval afterwards. *Feedback: Once it's gone it may not come back.*
- D. Reply asking what it's for. *Feedback: The fraudster will have an answer. Check another way.*

### Q9 (LO4, single choice)

You're a budget holder. An invoice for an "annual directory listing renewal" lands in your approvals and you don't remember ordering it. What do you do?

- A. Approve it: it's only a small amount. *Feedback: Approving says it was ordered and received. Fake invoices count on busy approvers.*
- **B. Don't approve it. Check whether it was ordered, and if not, tell Finance and report it.** *Feedback: Right. And if you've already approved one, say so straight away.*
- C. Approve it and ask Finance to check. *Feedback: Finance may take your approval as the check.*

### Q10 (LO6, ordering)

Twenty minutes ago you helped prepare a payment, and the supplier has just told you the account isn't theirs. Put these in order.

1. **Tell Finance to ring the bank's fraud line now.**
2. **Tell the security team.**
3. **Keep the emails: don't delete or forward them.**
4. **Finance or security reports it to Report Fraud (Police Scotland on 101 in Scotland).**
5. **Tell the real supplier, on the number on record.**

*Feedback: The bank first: it can only try to freeze money that hasn't been moved on, and most organisations aren't covered by the reimbursement rules. (Accept 2 and 3 in either order.)*

### Q11 (LO1, single choice)

Your manager messages you from a number you don't recognise: "Lost my phone. Can you buy six £50 gift cards for a client and send me the codes? Keep it quiet." What do you do?

- **A. Check with your manager on a number or chat you already have, and report the message.** *Feedback: Right. No real organisation pays with gift cards. (core_02 covers "new number" messages.)*
- B. Buy them: it's your manager. *Feedback: Once the codes are sent the money is gone.*
- C. Reply on the new number to ask if it's really them. *Feedback: The fraudster will say yes.*

### Q12 (LO6, single choice)

A fake change of bank details was stopped before anything was paid. What now?

- A. Nothing: no harm done. *Feedback: The same fraudster will try your colleagues and the supplier's other customers.*
- **B. Report it, so the security team can warn others and tell the supplier their mailbox may be compromised.** *Feedback: Right. Reporting is never wasted.*
- C. Delete it. *Feedback: The security team may need it.*
- D. Reply to tell the fraudster you've spotted it. *Feedback: Don't engage. Report it.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Think of the last time a supplier, a colleague or someone senior asked you to change or hurry a payment. Which number would you have rung to check?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What makes it hard here to say no to a senior person, or to admit you approved something you shouldn't have? What would make it easier?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour (with remediation if needed), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Hassan replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check, without Learn and Practise. Passing records the module as complete; missing a critical behaviour sends the learner through the full module.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. This supports:

- PCI DSS v4.0.1 12.6.3.1 (awareness of phishing and related attacks and social engineering) and 12.6.3 (training on hire and at least every 12 months);
- ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A 6.3 and clause 7.3;
- NIST SP 800-53 AT-2 and AT-2(3) where used;
- CIS Controls v8.1 14.2 (recognise social engineering attacks, including business email compromise);
- NIS2 Article 21(2)(g) and NCSC CAF B6.

It also gives evidence that staff outside Finance know the organisation's payment controls exist and why (Annex A 5.3, segregation of duties), which auditors often ask about after a payment fraud.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Call-back evidence on bank detail changes:** the share of supplier bank changes in the finance system's change log with a recorded call to the number on record. The target is all of them.
- **Reports from outside Finance:** fake change requests, invoices and "chief executive" requests reported by other staff, and the time from arrival to report.
- **Confirmation of Payee overrides:** payments released after a "no match" without a recorded check, which should fall to none.
- **Invoices queried by budget holders** as "not ordered": a steady number shows people checking rather than approving on trust.
- **Authorised tests** (Jo Fairweather's firm in the story; HA-12 where used): how many staff report a test request and how many ring the number on record.
- **Near misses and losses** from payment fraud, and how quickly losses were reported to the bank.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** (C11-B1 against C11-B2) across the organisation in the course itself.

These need Finance's change log and payment-system data, report-button data, and the course record. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
