### From behaviours to the year profile

The profile has **one area per core module, core_01 to core_16** (core_00's reporting route is folded into core_04's area). For each area:

1. **Evidence:** every capstone moment and check item tagged with that module's outcomes this year, scored and weighted as in `features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 3 (Secure 2, Developing 1, Not yet 0; Apply weight 2, check items 1, the Hook 0).
2. **Rating:** Secure if the weighted average is at least 1.5 with no Apply moment at Not yet; Not yet below 0.6; Developing otherwise; capped at Developing if a critical behaviour in that area was shown only after practice or not shown.
3. **Too little evidence:** with fewer than two pieces, the area is "not assessed this year" and last year's rating is shown with its date; after two years without evidence it reads "no recent evidence".
4. **"New to you"** is added where the learner hasn't completed that module in any version. The rating still shows, and the recommendation is the full module.
5. **Spotlights** (proposed: Spotlight recommender): up to two areas, chosen in this order: an area whose critical behaviour was practised but not yet shown; an area at Not yet; an area at Developing this year and last; then the organisation's priority order (HA-11). Each points to the full module ("new to you", or Not yet) or to its micro-drills (Developing). They are private recommendations through HA-03's practice trigger: shown only in the learner home, never overdue, never shown to managers.
6. **Organisation spotlights** are separate: the organisation picks one or two modules for a group from aggregate results (HA-09, above the privacy threshold) and assigns them as the "organisation's choice" slots in the annual refresher path (HA-03). Those are visible and can be required.

## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must show, or be taken back through the practice for:

- **C17-CB1 Never giving away their login or a code, or letting a stranger connect:** C17-B5, C17-B12 and C17-B13 not at Not yet.
- **C17-CB2 Never approving or vouching for a change of bank details without a call-back on the number on record:** C17-B8 not at Not yet.
- **C17-CB3 Reporting their own slip before the day ends,** where they slipped: C17-B11 not at Not yet.

A learner who misses one is taken back to it after the debrief, following the course's remediation loop (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4). Ama replays the matching core module's practice items briefly, then a short "replay" brings a fresh variant of the same moment from the bank (BE-13): a different caller, a different supplier, a different slip. If it still isn't shown, a second loop uses another variant and a more direct prompt. After two loops the learner goes on to the check; the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private drill is scheduled two to four weeks later (HA-03's practice trigger, HA-21), the linked outcomes are capped at Developing for this sitting, and the area becomes one of the learner's spotlights. Every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Each sitting draws **ten items**: six from the core modules' own banks (stratified across areas, favouring areas where the day gave this learner little evidence or where they are "new to you") and four from the capstone's twelve below, which test the capstone's own outcomes. Feedback is shown at the end, not after each item, so the check stays an assessment (MG-20's capstone rule). Contacts and procedures shown come from the organisation profile (BE-05); Larkspur defaults are shown here.

### C17-Q1 (LO2, single choice)

Three things happen on one day: at 08:58 you approve a sign-in prompt to make it stop; at 13:40 Finance asks about bank details "you" forwarded; on Tuesday a supplier's payment is missing. Where was the best place to stop it?

- **A. At 08:58: deny the prompt and report it.** *Feedback: Right. The earliest report breaks every link that follows.*
- B. At 13:40, when Finance rang. *Feedback: Reporting then still helps a lot, but the attacker had your mailbox for hours.*
- C. On Tuesday, when the money was missed. *Feedback: By then the money has probably moved on. The sooner, the smaller.*

### C17-Q2 (LO1, multiple select)

It's the afternoon before a long weekend. Which of these should make you pause? Choose all that apply.

- **A. "IT" in chat asking for the code that's just been texted to you.** *Feedback: Yes. IT never asks for codes.*
- **B. A supplier's new bank details "to update before the cut-off".** *Feedback: Yes. Money plus a deadline: check on the number on record.*
- **C. A voice note from the chief executive telling you to approve a payment.** *Feedback: Yes. Voices can be faked. Check another way.*
- D. A colleague's "have a lovely weekend" email. *Feedback: No need to pause on that one. Enjoy it.*

### C17-Q3 (LO3, single choice)

A voice note that sounds exactly like your chief executive says: "Approve the Thornby invoice today. I've agreed it." What do you do?

- A. Approve it: it's definitely her voice. *Feedback: Voices can be faked convincingly (core_15). Don't act on a voice alone.*
- **B. Check with Finance, or with her on a number you already have, before doing anything, and report the voice note.** *Feedback: Right. The procedure protects her too.*
- C. Reply to the voice note asking if it's really her. *Feedback: Whoever sent it will say yes.*

### C17-Q4 (LO4, single choice)

You've just replied all with a handover note that includes customer contacts, and the client was on the thread. What first?

- A. Wait to see if the client mentions it. *Feedback: Waiting loses the best chance to put it right.*
- **B. Report it now through the usual route, so the DPO can ask the client to delete it while it's fresh.** *Feedback: Right. Then try to recall it if your mail client allows.*
- C. Send a second email asking them to ignore the first. *Feedback: Well-meant, but report it first so the DPO can handle it properly.*

### C17-Q5 (LO5, single choice)

A new apprentice asks why you didn't let the delivery driver through the badge gate when his hands were full. What's the best reply?

- A. "Because you'd have got us all in trouble." *Feedback: True or not, blame makes people stop asking questions.*
- **B. "We didn't know who he was there to see. We ask, and ring them down. That's how we keep everyone safe."** *Feedback: Right. The reason, kindly, in one breath.*
- C. "Rules are rules." *Feedback: Correct, but without the reason it won't stick.*

### C17-Q6 (LO3, single choice)

A colleague covering for you next week asks for your password so he can keep an eye on your inbox. What do you say?

- A. "Sure, I'll change it when I'm back." *Feedback: Anything done with your account in the meantime looks like you did it.*
- **B. "I can't share it, but the service desk can give you access to my inbox properly."** *Feedback: Right. Shared access is quick to set up and leaves a proper record.*
- C. "Use the one on the sticky note in my drawer." *Feedback: That's sharing it with anyone who opens the drawer.*

### C17-Q7 (LO4, single choice)

At reception, a visitor asks for "everything you've got about my wife's care". What do you do?

- A. Tell him it has to be in writing. *Feedback: A request for someone's data can be made in person or by phone. Take it seriously.*
- **B. Take his details kindly and pass the request to the DPO the same day, without handing anything over.** *Feedback: Right. The DPO checks who's asking and what they're entitled to.*
- C. Print out what the system shows. *Feedback: Never hand over personal data on the spot. The DPO decides what can be shared.*

### C17-Q8 (LO2, true or false with reason)

"If I denied a sign-in prompt I didn't start, there's no need to report it."

- A. True: I stopped it. *Feedback: You stopped that attempt, but someone has your password and will try again.*
- **B. False: report it, so the password can be changed before they try another way.** *Feedback: Right. A denied prompt is the first link of a chain.*

### C17-Q9 (LO1, single choice)

A courier delivers a parcel to reception: a USB stick labelled "Visitor kiosk update: plug in before 5pm". What do you do?

- A. Plug it into the reception PC: it's for the kiosk. *Feedback: Unknown USB sticks can carry malware. Never plug one in.*
- **B. Give it to IT unplugged, and report it.** *Feedback: Right. IT can check whether anyone really sent it.*
- C. Put it in a drawer and forget it. *Feedback: Safe for you, but nobody learns that someone is sending sticks to reception.*

### C17-Q10 (LO2, LO3, single choice)

A caller from "the visitor kiosk company" says he needs to connect to the reception PC remotely to finish a year-end update. What do you do?

- A. Let him connect: the kiosk does need updates. *Feedback: Remote access gives a stranger your computer. Never because a caller asked.*
- B. Ask for his number and call him back. *Feedback: Better than nothing, but his number proves nothing. Ring IT on a number you already have.*
- **C. Say no, ring IT on the number you already have, and report the call.** *Feedback: Right. If it's genuine, IT will arrange it.*

### C17-Q11 (LO1, single choice)

Your phone asks you to approve a sign-in while your laptop is still starting up. What do you do?

- A. Approve it: it's probably the laptop. *Feedback: The laptop hasn't asked yet. Only approve a prompt you started.*
- **B. Choose "No, it's not me" and report it.** *Feedback: Right. Then change your password through the real site if you're asked to.*
- C. Ignore it until it stops. *Feedback: Not approving is good; reporting is better, because someone has your password.*

### C17-Q12 (LO4, single choice)

You realise something you did earlier might have been a mistake, but you're not sure, and it's nearly time to go home for a long weekend. What's best?

- **A. Report it now, roughly: what happened, when, what you did.** *Feedback: Right. A rough report today beats a perfect one on Tuesday.*
- B. Wait until Tuesday when you can find out more. *Feedback: Four days is a long time for an attacker.*
- C. Say nothing unless someone asks. *Feedback: The sooner it's known, the smaller it stays, and you'll be thanked.*

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played the day through (game, text route, or the team event plus their individual check where the organisation allows it), shown each critical behaviour or been taken back through the practice for it, passed the check, opened the Year End card, and acknowledged any policy the organisation attached to core_17. If the annual policy re-acknowledgement is handled outside the module (HA-07), it doesn't hold up completion.
- **Pass mark:** 8 of 10 (80%, the course default; the organisation can change it within MG-20's limits).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws ten new items. After two fails, Ama's huddle is replayed before the third attempt.
- **Test-out:** not offered. The capstone is the annual assessment, and a test-out would be the same thing without the refresh.
- **Recurrence:** every 12 months through HA-03 (rolling from the last completion by default, or a fixed annual date), always on the edition current when the learner starts. A learner who starts in late August and finishes in September keeps their edition.
- **Sittings:** one or two (the lunchtime checkpoint, BE-12); the record counts both.

## Evidence for auditors

The completion record (HA-05) shows: module id, **edition** and version (HA-19), start and completion dates, number of sittings, time on task, route (game, text route or facilitated), the check score and pass mark in force, each critical behaviour (shown, shown after practice, practised but not yet shown, or not applicable), the before and after pair, linked policy acknowledgements (HA-07) and that the Year End card was opened. Per-area profiles and behaviour ratings appear in aggregate only, unless the organisation chooses to include them (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 11). Each edition's release notes (HA-19) record what was reviewed and changed that year.

This supports:

- **PCI DSS 12.6.3** (training at least every 12 months, by more than one method alongside the drills and reference cards; the annual acknowledgement with HA-07) and **PCI DSS 12.6.3.1**; the release notes support **PCI DSS 12.6.2** (the programme reviewed every year and updated for new threats);
- **ISO 27001 cl. 7.2** (competence evaluated, with documented evidence), **ISO 27001 cl. 7.3** and **ISO 27001 A.6.3**;
- **NIST AT-2**, **NIST AT-2(1)** (practical exercises) and **NIST AT-2(6)** (the current threat environment);
- **CIS 14.1** (at least annually, with content reviewed each year);
- **NCSC CAF B6.b** (each individual's training tracked and refreshed; effect evaluated, not just reach);
- **DSPT 3.2.1** (categories 3 and 4: trained within the last twelve months);
- the **ICO training toolkit** (refresher training with an assessment and a pass mark);
- **NIS2 IR 8.1** and **DORA Art 13(6)**, for EU customers; **NYDFS 500.14(a)(3)** and **CMMC AT.L3-3.2.1e**, for US customers;
- **Cyber Governance Code C4**, through the board scorecard built from the results (HA-09).

The annual sample an auditor usually takes ("show me these ten people were trained in the last twelve months, with their policy acknowledgement") comes straight from the audit pack (HA-05).

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, and from year to year, an organisation can watch:

- **The anchor slots.** The Hook (an unexpected sign-in prompt) and the bank detail change stay in every edition at a calibrated difficulty, so the share of people at Secure on them can be compared year on year, using first sittings only. Other slots change, so they aren't compared across years.
- **Chain breaks:** the share of learners who broke each chain within two game-minutes of its start, by department above the threshold.
- **Spotlight effect:** next year's area ratings for learners who took a recommended spotlight against those who didn't. It isn't a controlled trial (people choose), but it is the closest natural comparison the course offers.
- **Report rate and time to first report** for real suspicious emails and, if used, simulations (HA-12): the course's lead measures, which should hold up through the year rather than peak in the capstone month.
- **The board scorecard** (HA-09): completion on time, the share Secure per area, the anchor slots year on year, report rate, time to first report, and the culture question from the annual pulse (HA-14), "I would feel safe reporting a mistake".
- **The team event:** attendance, and the reflection answers that facilitators send to the security team.

Caveats: editions differ, so compare only anchor slots across years; people expect threats in an assessment, so in-game results flatter real behaviour; and a falling click rate on its own is never evidence that the course works (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 10). The data needs report-button and simulation figures (HA-12) and the annual pulse (HA-14); dashboards show departments, never named individuals below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
