# core_17 assessment

The capstone measures with the built tools: the **pre and post check forms** (the learner's role forms, AB and BA, 10 items, `passPercent` 0) and the **defend items** of the mixed run (the `awareness` profile: 60 for the verdict, 40 for the real flags, reporting never penalised). There is **no default pass mark** (R6). Per-item records (id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant, time) go to the awareness-measure store; no free text, no learner identity. A discrimination figure goes to organisers only, and the product makes no effectiveness claim.

This file maps the capstone's behaviours and question items onto **defend items** (drawn from the year's episodes) and **check items** (the role forms and the capstone's own pool), as **mirror pairs**. The year profile and spotlights are a **proposed, later** extension that reads these same records; they are not built in the first edition.

## Critical behaviours

Each critical behaviour is now a mirror pair (aw5).

- **C17-CB1 Never giving away a login or a code, or letting a stranger connect:** the shared-login, kiosk-call and MFA items are each paired with a genuine counterpart of the same channel, so declining-and-routing is distinguished from refusing everything.
- **C17-CB2 Never approving or vouching for a change of bank details without a call-back on the number on record:** the bank-change item is paired with a genuine invoice.
- **C17-CB3 Reporting their own slip:** reporting is never penalised, so every item that could be a slip credits a report, early or late.

No loop-back and no default pass mark: a learner who misses an item meets a similar one in the mix; the first answer is recorded.

## Knowledge check bank

Item pool: the capstone's own check items.

The role check forms supply most of each edition's check items. The capstone's own pool below adds items that test the capstone's outcomes (chains, busy days, helping a colleague). Written as mirror pairs. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C17-Q1 (LO2): check item

A sign-in prompt at 08:58, a "bank details you forwarded" call at 13:40, and a missing payment on Tuesday. Where was the best place to stop it?

- **A. At 08:58: deny the prompt and report it.** *The earliest report breaks every link that follows.*
- B. At 13:40. *Reporting then still helps, but the attacker had the mailbox for hours.*
- C. On Tuesday. *By then the money has probably moved on.*

### C17-Q2 (LO1, multiple select): check item

It's the afternoon before a long weekend. Which should make you pause? Choose all that apply.

- **A. "IT" asking for the code just texted to you.** *IT never asks for codes.*
- **B. A supplier's new bank details "before the cut-off".** *Money plus a deadline: check on the number on record.*
- **C. A voice note from the chief executive telling you to approve a payment.** *Voices can be faked.*
- D. A colleague's "have a lovely weekend" email. *No need to pause on that one.*

### C17-Q3 (LO3): check item

A voice note that sounds exactly like your chief executive says "approve the invoice today, I've agreed it". What do you do?

- A. Approve it: it's her voice. *Voices can be faked (core_15).*
- **B. Check with Finance, or with her on a number you already have, and report the voice note.** *The procedure protects her too.*
- C. Reply to the voice note. *Whoever sent it will say yes.*

### C17-Q4 (LO4): check item

You've just replied all with a handover note that includes customer contacts, and the client was on the thread. What first?

- A. Wait to see if the client mentions it. *Waiting loses the best chance to put it right.*
- **B. Report it now, so the DPO can ask the client to delete it while it's fresh.** *Then try to recall it.*
- C. Send a second email asking them to ignore the first. *Report it first.*

### C17-Q5 (LO5): check item

A new colleague asks why you didn't let a delivery driver through the badge gate. What's the best reply?

- A. "Because you'd have got us all in trouble." *Blame makes people stop asking.*
- **B. "We didn't know who he was there to see. We ask, and ring them down. That's how we keep everyone safe."** *The reason, kindly.*
- C. "Rules are rules." *Correct, but without the reason it won't stick.*

### C17-Q6 (LO3): check item

A colleague covering next week asks for your password to keep an eye on your inbox. What do you say?

- A. "Sure, I'll change it when I'm back." *Anything done with your account looks like you did it.*
- **B. "I can't share it, but the service desk can give you access properly."** *Shared access leaves a proper record.*
- C. "Use the one on the sticky note." *That's sharing it with anyone who opens the drawer.*

### C17-Q7 (LO4): check item

At reception, a visitor asks for "everything you've got about my wife's care". What do you do?

- A. Tell him it has to be in writing. *A request can be made in person.*
- **B. Take his details kindly and pass the request to the DPO the same day, without handing anything over.** *The DPO checks who's asking.*
- C. Print out what the system shows. *Never hand over personal data on the spot.*

### C17-Q8 (LO2, true or false with reason): check item

"If I denied a sign-in prompt I didn't start, there's no need to report it."

- A. True. *You stopped that attempt, but someone has your password.*
- **B. False: report it, so the password can be changed before they try another way.** *A denied prompt is the first link of a chain.*

### C17-Q9 (LO1): check item

A courier delivers a USB stick labelled "update: plug in before 5pm". What do you do?

- A. Plug it into the reception PC. *Unknown sticks can carry malware.*
- **B. Give it to IT unplugged, and report it.** *IT can check whether anyone really sent it.*
- C. Put it in a drawer. *Safe for you, but nobody learns someone is sending sticks.*

### C17-Q10 (LO2, LO3): check item

A caller from "the kiosk company" needs to connect to the reception PC to finish an update. What do you do?

- A. Let him connect. *Remote access gives a stranger your computer.*
- B. Ask for his number and call back. *His number proves nothing.*
- **C. Say no, ring IT on the number you already have, and report the call.** *If it's genuine, IT will arrange it.*

### C17-Q11 (LO1): check item

Your phone asks you to approve a sign-in while your laptop is still starting up. What do you do?

- A. Approve it: it's probably the laptop. *The laptop hasn't asked yet.*
- **B. Choose "No, it's not me" and report it.** *Then change your password through the real site if asked.*
- C. Ignore it until it stops. *Not approving is good; reporting is better.*

### C17-Q12 (LO4): check item

You realise something you did earlier might have been a mistake, and it's nearly time to go home for a long weekend. What's best?

- **A. Report it now, roughly: what happened, when, what you did.** *A rough report today beats a perfect one on Tuesday.*
- B. Wait until Tuesday. *Four days is a long time for an attacker.*
- C. Say nothing unless asked. *The sooner it's known, the smaller it stays.*

## Completion and pass rules

A run is complete when the learner has played the mixed edition (or the text route, or the team event plus their individual check where the organisation allows it) and taken the pre and post checks. Scoring lives only in the defend items and the checks; **there is no default pass mark**. An organisation may set a pass mark on the post-check percent as a later option, through the platform. Test-out is not offered: the capstone is the annual assessment.

## Evidence for auditors

The awareness-measure record holds, per item: the id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant and time, with no free text and no learner identity. The completion record shows the edition, the role forms used and the before-and-after change. Each edition's release notes record what was reviewed and changed that year. This supports:

- PCI DSS 12.6.3 and 12.6.3.1; the release notes support PCI DSS 12.6.2;
- ISO 27001 cl. 7.2, cl. 7.3 and A.6.3;
- NIST AT-2, AT-2(1) and AT-2(6); CIS 14.1;
- NCSC CAF B6.b; DSPT 3.2.1;
- NIS2 IR 8.1 and DORA Art 13(6) (EU); NYDFS 500.14(a)(3) and CMMC AT.L3-3.2.1e (US);
- the ICO training toolkit (refresher training with an assessment).

## Measuring effect

The product claims only a before-and-after change on matched items for a cohort, with no effectiveness claim. Outside the course, an organisation can watch (by department, never by named individual): the anchor items year on year (an unexpected sign-in prompt and a bank-detail change, kept stable across editions, first sittings only), report rate and time to first report for real suspicious emails, and the culture survey item "I would feel safe reporting a mistake". Editions differ, so only the anchor items are compared across years, and in-game results flatter real behaviour.
