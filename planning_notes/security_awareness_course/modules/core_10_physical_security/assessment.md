## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once:

- **C10-CB1 Not let an unknown person through a secure door or into a secure area** (C10-B1, B2 or B3 not at Not yet).
- **C10-CB2 Keep their badge to themselves** (C10-B5 not at Not yet).
- **C10-CB3 Report at least one concern or their own slip** (C10-B8 not at Not yet).

A learner who misses a critical behaviour is taken back to it: Precious replays the relevant spot-the-risk items with the explanation, then a fresh moment of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the Apply beat. The record keeps both attempts.

## Check and defend items (mirror pairs)

core_10 is delivered as **E12** (new): the visitor and the tailgater, covering core_10 with person-chat in the training room. The attacker view (Red Team Day) is the attack-first half; the defend half is the day of friendly challenges and checks. Only the defend half and the pre and post checks are scored (`passPercent` 0). The assessed behaviours and the question pool below map onto defend items and check items, each critical behaviour a **mirror pair** (aw5) tested by matched items across forms A and B. A physical-security check role is proposed as **New**, each form needing its own matched items.

## Knowledge check bank

A pool of candidate items for the E12 defend set and the check forms, covering every learning outcome. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C10-Q1 (LO1, single choice)

Someone you do not recognise is following you towards the badge door, carrying a laptop and smiling. What is the best thing to do?

- A. Hold the door open; it would be rude not to. *Feedback: At a secure door, holding it lets a stranger tailgate in. Everyone badges in for themselves.*
- **B. Say "Can I help you find someone? I'll walk you to reception," and let the door close.** *Feedback: Right. Friendly, helpful, and it stops a tailgater.*
- C. Say nothing and badge in quickly so they cannot follow. *Feedback: Better to offer help; if you would rather not challenge, report it to reception instead.*
- D. Ask to see their badge and let them through if they have one. *Feedback: A badge can be faked. Take them to reception to sign in.*

### C10-Q2 (LO2, single choice)

A visitor in a hi-vis jacket says he is here for the annual check of the comms room and asks you to let him in so he can get on. What do you check?

- A. Nothing; the jacket and the clipboard are enough. *Feedback: A uniform and a clipboard are easy to get. Verify before granting access.*
- **B. Whether he is on today's expected-visitors list and his work order matches, then escort him to reception.** *Feedback: Right. Expected, signed in, escorted.*
- C. Let him in but stay nearby. *Feedback: Do not grant access to a secure area on a story. Verify first.*
- D. Give him the door code so he can come and go. *Feedback: Never share a door code, especially to a secure area.*

### C10-Q3 (LO3, true or false with reason)

"It's fine to lend my badge to a colleague who has lost theirs, just for the day."

- A. True: they work here, so it is not a problem. *Feedback: The door record would then say you went where they went, and it encourages tailgating.*
- **B. False: everyone badges in for themselves; help them report the loss and get a temporary pass.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C10-Q4 (LO3, single choice)

Why does it matter if you leave your badge on the canteen table while you get a coffee?

- A. It does not matter; a badge is just an ID card. *Feedback: A badge is a key, and many cards can be read at short range.*
- **B. A badge can be copied at close range, and it can be taken, so keep it on you and report a loss at once.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Only if it has your photo on it. *Feedback: The risk is the access it gives, not the photo.*
- D. Only in a public place, not at work. *Feedback: Keep it with you inside too; do not leave it lying around.*

### C10-Q5 (LO4, single choice)

You are stepping away from your desk for two minutes to grab a document. What should you do with your screen?

- **A. Lock it, every time, even for two minutes.** *Feedback: Right. Two minutes is long enough for someone to read your email or act as you.*
- B. Leave it; two minutes is nothing. *Feedback: Lock it every time you step away.*
- C. Turn the monitor off. *Feedback: Turning it off does not lock your session; lock the screen.*
- D. Only lock it if there is a visitor about. *Feedback: Lock it every time, visitor or not.*

### C10-Q6 (LO5, single choice)

It is a warm day and someone has wedged the fire door open with a fire extinguisher. What should you do?

- A. Leave it; it is only for the afternoon. *Feedback: A wedged fire door lets fire and smoke spread, and it undoes the lock.*
- **B. Remove the wedge, close the door, and report it.** *Feedback: Right. If a door must stay open, it should use an alarm-linked hold-open device.*
- C. Prop it more safely with a doorstop. *Feedback: Do not prop a fire door at all; use the proper hold-open device.*
- D. Report it but leave it open meanwhile. *Feedback: Close it as well as reporting it.*

### C10-Q7 (LO4, multiple select)

At the end of the day, which of these should you deal with before you leave? Choose all that apply.

- **A. A customer list printed and left in your tray.** *Feedback: Secure it or put it in confidential waste.*
- **B. A whiteboard with a client's name and a go-live date.** *Feedback: Wipe it.*
- **C. A USB stick and a notebook on your desk.** *Feedback: Lock them away.*
- D. Your (empty) coffee mug. *Feedback: Not a security risk, though the kitchen would thank you.*

### C10-Q8 (LO6, single choice)

You realise you held the side door open this morning for someone who turned out not to work here. What now?

- **A. Report it to security or facilities now; you won't be in trouble, and they can check CCTV.** *Feedback: Right. A fast report lets the team act; the harm comes from delay.*
- B. Say nothing; nothing obviously went wrong. *Feedback: Report it so the team can check. Reporting is never the wrong call.*
- C. Wait and see if anything happens. *Feedback: Waiting gives an intruder time. Report it now.*
- D. Tell only your own manager, quietly. *Feedback: Use the reporting route so security can act, not just your manager.*

### C10-Q9 (LO1, LO2, ordering)

A visitor arrives at reception to see a colleague. Put the steps in order.

1. **Sign them in at reception.**
2. **Give them a visitor badge to wear.**
3. **Let their host know and have the host (or you) escort them.**
4. **Make sure they are signed out and the badge is returned when they leave.**

*Feedback: Visitors are signed in, badged, escorted, and signed out. They are not left to wander.*

### C10-Q10 (LO4, single choice)

You find a printout with staff bank details left on the shared printer. What is the best action?

- A. Leave it; whoever printed it will collect it. *Feedback: Confidential printouts should not be left in the tray.*
- **B. Pick it up, hand it back to the owner or secure it, and use secure release next time.** *Feedback: Right. Collect printing straight away (see core_06).*
- C. Recycle it so nobody sees it. *Feedback: Confidential paper goes in the locked confidential waste, not the shredder or the recycling (see core_06), and the owner may still need it.*
- D. Photograph it and message the owner. *Feedback: Do not photograph confidential data; hand it back or secure it.*

### C10-Q11 (LO2, single choice)

You take card payments at a reception desk. At the start of your shift the card reader has an extra attachment you do not remember. What should you do?

- A. Use it; it is probably an upgrade. *Feedback: An unfamiliar attachment can be a skimming device.*
- **B. Do not use it, and report it so it can be checked.** *Feedback: Right. Staff at card terminals should spot tampering or substitution (PCI DSS).*
- C. Remove the attachment yourself and carry on. *Feedback: Do not tamper with it; report it and let it be checked.*
- D. Only worry if customers complain. *Feedback: Check and report proactively.*

(C10-Q11 applies only where the organisation takes card payments on site; drop it otherwise.)

## Reflection prompt

- "When did you last see a propped door, a badge on a desk or an unlocked screen here? What stopped you, or would stop you, from doing something about it?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What would make it easy and normal to challenge someone we don't recognise, without anyone feeling awkward?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour (with remediation if needed), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **No default pass mark.** The pre and post checks are `passPercent` 0: no pass or fail, and the missions are completion-scored. The post-check shows the change from the newest pre-check. A pass mark or certificate is a later organisation option set on the post-check percent, not a default.
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Precious replays the Learn or Practise beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where allowed): the Apply beat and the check without Red Team Day and the drill. A pass records the module complete; missing a critical behaviour sends the learner through the full module.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. This supports:

- ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A 7.1 to 7.4, 7.6, 7.7, and A 6.3, clause 7.3;
- NIST SP 800-53 Rev 5 AT-2(3) (tailgating) and AT-3(2) (physical security training);
- CIS Controls v8.1 14.2 (tailgating) and 14.4 (clear desk and clear screen);
- PCI DSS v4.0.1 requirement 9 and 9.5.1.3, where card payments are taken on site;
- NCSC CAF B6 and B2.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Challenge and report rates** for unknown people, propped doors and lost badges (security and facilities logs).
- **Results of authorised physical tests** (tailgating success rate, time to first challenge), run kindly and with a clear policy.
- **Clear-desk and clear-screen walk-rounds**: the share of desks and screens found secure.
- **Lost-badge reports**: reported promptly rather than discovered later.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** (C10-B1 against C10-B2) across the organisation.

Authorised physical tests must be framed as finding gaps to fix, never as catching individuals out, and results reported by area, not by named person.
