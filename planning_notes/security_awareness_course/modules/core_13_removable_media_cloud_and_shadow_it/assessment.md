## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once:

- **C13-CB1 Not plug in the posted USB stick** (C13-B4 not at Not yet).
- **C13-CB2 Not send work files to a personal account**, or tell IT before the module ends if they did (C13-B2 not at Not yet).
- **C13-CB3 Ask IT for an approved route** (C13-B2, B3, B5 or B6 at Secure).

A learner who misses a critical behaviour goes through the remediation loop (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4). Owain says what happened without blame, replays the matching Learn chunk with the related Swap Shop cards, and a fresh case of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the Apply beat: a giveaway stick from a trade show, a colleague suggesting personal cloud sync, a new tool request. Shown: recorded as "shown after practice", and the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting. Not shown: a second loop with a different case and a more direct prompt. After two loops the learner goes on to the check; the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private practice drill is scheduled two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21), and the learner is offered help from the organisation's training contact. Nobody else is told by name. Every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Five items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C13-Q1 (LO1, single choice)

You find a USB stick in the car park labelled "Bonus payments". What do you do?

- A. Plug it into your work laptop to find the owner: the security software will protect you. *Feedback: Security software doesn't catch everything, and some devices attack the moment they're plugged in.*
- B. Take it home and look at it on your own laptop. *Feedback: Same risk, with even less protection, and possibly someone's personal data on your home computer.*
- **C. Hand it to IT without plugging it in, and say where you found it.** *Feedback: Right. IT can check it safely and find the owner.*
- D. Leave it: someone will come back for it. *Feedback: The next person may plug it in. Hand it in.*

### C13-Q2 (LO1, single choice)

A padded envelope arrives "from" a client, with a USB stick and a note: "Survey results, please add to your report." There's a phone number on the note. What do you do?

- A. Plug it in: it's from a client. *Feedback: Anyone can put a client's name on an envelope.*
- B. Ring the number on the note to check. *Feedback: If the envelope is fake, so is the number. Use one you already have.*
- **C. Call the client on the number you already have, and give the stick to IT either way.** *Feedback: Right. Check another way, and let IT handle the stick.*
- D. Plug it into an old spare PC instead. *Feedback: The spare PC is still on the network. Give it to IT.*

### C13-Q3 (LO1, single choice)

Your manager hands you a free USB stick from a trade show to carry tomorrow's presentation. What do you do?

- A. Use it: it's brand new. *Feedback: Giveaway sticks have carried malware before. New doesn't mean clean.*
- **B. Don't plug it in. Open the presentation from Larkspur Files on the meeting room screen, or ask IT for an approved stick if the policy allows one.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Wipe it first, then use it. *Feedback: You'd have to plug it in to wipe it.*
- D. Use it on your personal laptop instead. *Feedback: That moves the risk, and the work file, onto a device the organisation doesn't protect.*

### C13-Q4 (LO2, true or false with reason)

"Emailing work files to your own personal address is fine, because they're only going to you."

- A. True: nobody else can see your personal email. *Feedback: Personal accounts get broken into, and the organisation can't protect or recover what's there.*
- **B. False: it moves the files outside the organisation's protection, and with personal data in them, it can be a breach. Use the approved way to work from elsewhere.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C13-Q5 (LO2, single choice)

Your team wants a group chat on a personal messaging app because it's "encrypted". What's the problem?

- A. None: encryption makes it secure. *Feedback: Encryption protects messages on the way. It doesn't decide who's in the group.*
- **B. The organisation can't control who's in the group, back it up or remove people who leave. Use the company chat tool.** *Feedback: Right. In a real case, a stranger was added to a health team's group by mistake.*
- C. Only that it might use mobile data. *Feedback: That's the least of it.*
- D. Only that managers can't read it. *Feedback: The issue is control and protection, not managers.*

### C13-Q6 (LO3, multiple select)

Which are real risks of keeping work in a free online app on someone's personal account? Choose all that apply.

- **A. There's no contract about where the data goes or what it's used for.** *Feedback: Right: no contract.*
- **B. Nobody can remove access when someone leaves.** *Feedback: Right: no control.*
- **C. If the account is closed, the data goes with it.** *Feedback: Right: no backup.*
- D. It will slow your computer down. *Feedback: That isn't the problem. The problems are contract, control and backup.*

### C13-Q7 (LO3, LO4, single choice)

You need to send a 2GB video to a client by tonight, and email won't take it. What do you do?

- A. Use a free transfer site: it's quick. *Feedback: The link may never expire, and the site has no agreement with your organisation.*
- B. Send it to your personal account in pieces and forward them from there. *Feedback: That's two workarounds in one.*
- **C. Share it from the organisation's file-sharing service with the named person and an end date, or ask IT.** *Feedback: Right. A named recipient, an end date, and you know when it's been opened.*
- D. Put it on your own USB stick and post it. *Feedback: Sticks get lost in the post, and yours isn't approved storage.*

### C13-Q8 (LO4, single choice)

The official file share is so slow that colleagues are emailing files to themselves to work on. What's the most useful thing you can do?

- A. Do the same: everyone else is. *Feedback: That's how a workaround becomes normal.*
- **B. Tell IT it's slow, and ask what to use in the meantime.** *Feedback: Right. They can't fix what they don't know about, and they'll usually have a stopgap.*
- C. Set up a free cloud folder for the team. *Feedback: Kind, but it moves the team's work outside the organisation's protection.*
- D. Nothing: it isn't your job. *Feedback: Reporting friction helps everyone, including you.*

### C13-Q9 (LO5, single choice)

You realise your team's rota has lived for months in a free app on a former temp's personal account. What do you do?

- A. Keep using it: it works. *Feedback: It works until the account is closed, and the temp can still see it.*
- B. Copy it to your own personal account so it's safe. *Feedback: That makes another copy nobody controls.*
- **C. Tell IT, so they can move it into an approved space and remove the old access. Nobody's in trouble.** *Feedback: Right.*
- D. Delete it so nobody can see it. *Feedback: The team still needs the rota, and copies may remain. Let IT move it.*

### C13-Q10 (LO5, single choice)

This morning you forwarded a customer complaint to your personal email to read on the bus. Now you're worried. What do you do?

- A. Delete it from your personal account and say nothing. *Feedback: Copies can remain, and the organisation may need to assess it.*
- **B. Report it to the security team. With personal data in it, they'll bring in the DPO and make sure it's dealt with properly, and you won't be in trouble for telling them.** *Feedback: Right. Telling them early is what makes it small.*
- C. Forward it back to your work account so it's in both places. *Feedback: That doesn't remove the personal copy.*
- D. Wait and see if anything happens. *Feedback: If anything happens, you'd rather it had been reported. Tell them now.*

### C13-Q11 (LO2, LO4, single choice)

You'd like to sync the shared drive to your personal cloud storage so you can read files on your tablet at home. What's the right move?

- A. Go ahead: it's only for reading. *Feedback: The files would still be copied into an account the organisation doesn't control.*
- **B. Ask IT about the approved app for reading work files on another device.** *Feedback: Right. There's usually an approved way.*
- C. Go ahead, and delete the files afterwards. *Feedback: Synced copies spread to every device on that account.*
- D. Email the files to yourself instead. *Feedback: That's the same workaround by another route.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Which tool at work is slow or awkward enough that you've worked round it? Who have you told?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What's living in a personal account or a free app in our team right now, and how would we move it?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken through the remediation loop for it (at most two loops), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 4 of 5 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws five new items. After two fails, Owain replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. Passing records the module as complete; missing a critical behaviour sends the learner through the full module.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. This supports:

- CIS 14.4 (storing and transferring data: C13-B2, B3 and B5) and CIS 14.5 (unintentional data exposure: C13-B3 and B5);
- ISO 27001 A.6.3;
- PCI DSS 12.6.3.2 (acceptable use of end-user technologies), with core_16, where the organisation's acceptable use policy covers storage, removable media and tools;
- UK GDPR Art 5(1)(f) and Art 32(4), as evidence that staff were trained to handle personal data securely;
- HIPAA 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(B) (the removable media decisions) for US customers;
- NIS2 Art 21(2)(g) and NCSC CAF B6.b.

See `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` for the full references.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Authorised USB drop tests:** sticks handed in, plugged in and never returned.
- **Work email forwarded to personal addresses**, and uploads to unapproved cloud services, from the organisation's data loss prevention and cloud discovery tools.
- **Tool requests through the official route**, and how quickly IT answers them: the quick yes only works if it is quick.
- **Reports of slow or broken official tools.** A rise is good news.
- **Shadow IT found and moved** into approved services, and old access removed from it.
- **Removable media use** on work devices, from device control logs.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** across the organisation in the course itself.

These need data from the organisation's mail system, cloud discovery and device management, and the service desk. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
