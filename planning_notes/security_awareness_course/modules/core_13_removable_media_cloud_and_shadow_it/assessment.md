# core_13 assessment

The built product scores the **defend half** (the `awareness` profile: 60 for the verdict, 40 for naming the real flags, reporting never penalised) and the **pre and post check forms** (10 items, `passPercent` 0). There is no default pass mark. Per-item records (id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant, time) go to the awareness-measure store; no free text, no learner identity. A discrimination figure goes to organisers only, and the product makes no effectiveness claim.

This file maps the module's behaviours and question items onto **defend items** (Swap Shop and the found-media cards) and **check items** (in the general check forms), as **mirror pairs**.

**Before and after.** The matched measure is the pre-check against the post-check on the removable-media and shadow-IT items, plus the first-answer records in the defend half. Within the episode, the consequence montage shows the contrast between an unknown stick before the lesson (in the sandbox) and after it (the posted-stick card).

## Critical behaviours as mirror pairs

- **C13-CB1 Not plug in an unexpected USB stick:** the posted-stick card is paired with a genuine delivery the learner can accept, so "never plug in" is distinguished from refusing everything.
- **C13-CB2 Not send work files to a personal account:** the personal-email item is paired with a genuine "work from home" need met by the approved route.
- **C13-CB3 Ask IT for an approved route:** the Swap Shop pairs each shortcut with its approved swap, so choosing the swap is the measured behaviour.

No loop-back and no default pass mark: a learner who misses an item meets a similar one next, and the first answer is recorded.

## Item pool: check forms and defend set

The question bank is kept as a pool of candidate items, written as mirror pairs. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C13-Q1 (LO1): defend card and check item

You find a USB stick labelled "Bonus payments". What do you do?

- A. Plug it into your work laptop to find the owner. *Security software doesn't catch everything, and some devices attack the moment they're plugged in.*
- B. Take it home and look on your own laptop. *Same risk, less protection.*
- **C. Hand it to IT without plugging it in, and say where you found it.** *IT can check it safely and find the owner.*
- D. Leave it: someone will come back. *The next person may plug it in.*

Mirror: the B-form item is a stick left in a meeting room, same shape.

### C13-Q2 (LO1): defend card and check item

A padded envelope arrives "from" a client with a USB stick and a note giving a phone number. What do you do?

- A. Plug it in: it's from a client. *Anyone can put a client's name on an envelope.*
- B. Ring the number on the note. *If the envelope is fake, so is the number.*
- **C. Call the client on the number you already have, and give the stick to IT either way.** *Check another way, and let IT handle the stick.*
- D. Plug it into an old spare PC. *The spare PC is still on the network.*

### C13-Q3 (LO1): check item

Your manager hands you a free USB stick from a trade show to carry a presentation. What do you do?

- A. Use it: it's new. *Giveaway sticks have carried malware. New doesn't mean clean.*
- **B. Don't plug it in. Open the file from the file-sharing service, or ask IT for an approved stick if the policy allows one.** *Right.*
- C. Wipe it first. *You'd have to plug it in to wipe it.*
- D. Use it on your personal laptop. *That moves the risk, and the file, onto an unprotected device.*

### C13-Q4 (LO2): check item (true or false with reason)

"Emailing work files to your own personal address is fine, because they're only going to you."

- A. True. *Personal accounts get broken into, and the organisation can't protect or recover what's there.*
- **B. False: it moves the files outside the organisation's protection, and with personal data in them it can be a breach.** *Right.*

### C13-Q5 (LO2): check item

Your team wants a group chat on a personal messaging app because it's "encrypted". What's the problem?

- A. None: encryption makes it secure. *Encryption protects messages on the way; it doesn't decide who's in the group.*
- **B. The organisation can't control who's in the group, back it up or remove people who leave. Use the company chat tool.** *In a real case, a stranger was added to a health team's group.*
- C. Only mobile data. *That's the least of it.*
- D. Only that managers can't read it. *The issue is control and protection.*

### C13-Q6 (LO3): defend Swap Shop reasoning and check item (multiple select)

Which are real risks of keeping work in a free online app on someone's personal account? Choose all that apply.

- **A. No contract about where the data goes.** *Right: no contract.*
- **B. Nobody can remove access when someone leaves.** *Right: no control.*
- **C. If the account is closed, the data goes with it.** *Right: no backup.*
- D. It slows your computer down. *Not the problem.*

### C13-Q7 (LO3, LO4): defend Swap Shop item and check item

You need to send a 2GB video to a client by tonight, and email won't take it. What do you do?

- A. Use a free transfer site. *The link may never expire, and the site has no agreement with your organisation.*
- B. Send it to yourself in pieces. *Two workarounds in one.*
- **C. Share it from the file-sharing service with the named person and an end date, or ask IT.** *A named recipient, an end date, and you know when it's opened.*
- D. Put it on your own stick and post it. *Sticks get lost; yours isn't approved storage.*

### C13-Q8 (LO4): check item

The official share is so slow that colleagues email files to themselves. What's the most useful thing you can do?

- A. Do the same. *That's how a workaround becomes normal.*
- **B. Tell IT it's slow, and ask what to use in the meantime.** *They can't fix what they don't know about.*
- C. Set up a free cloud folder for the team. *Kind, but it moves the work outside the organisation's protection.*
- D. Nothing. *Reporting friction helps everyone.*

### C13-Q9 (LO5): defend Swap Shop item and check item

Your team's rota has lived for months in a free app on a former temp's personal account. What do you do?

- A. Keep using it. *It works until the account is closed, and the temp can still see it.*
- B. Copy it to your own personal account. *That makes another copy nobody controls.*
- **C. Tell IT, so they can move it into an approved space and remove the old access.** *Right. Nobody's in trouble.*
- D. Delete it. *The team still needs it, and copies may remain.*

### C13-Q10 (LO5): check item

This morning you forwarded a customer complaint to your personal email to read on the bus. Now you're worried. What do you do?

- A. Delete it and say nothing. *Copies can remain, and the organisation may need to assess it.*
- **B. Report it to the security team. With personal data in it, they'll bring in the DPO.** *Telling them early makes it small.*
- C. Forward it back to your work account. *That doesn't remove the personal copy.*
- D. Wait and see. *Report it now.*

### C13-Q11 (LO2, LO4): check item

You'd like to sync the shared drive to your personal cloud so you can read files on your tablet at home. What's the right move?

- A. Go ahead: it's only reading. *The files would still be copied into an account the organisation doesn't control.*
- **B. Ask IT about the approved app for reading work files on another device.** *There's usually an approved way.*
- C. Go ahead, and delete afterwards. *Synced copies spread to every device on that account.*
- D. Email them to yourself. *The same workaround by another route.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Which tool at work is slow or awkward enough that you've worked round it? Who have you told?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What's living in a personal account or a free app in our team right now, and how would we move it?"

## Completion

A run is complete when the learner has played the episode (or the defend-only variant, or the text route). Scoring lives only in the defend half and the checks; there is no default pass mark. An organisation may set a pass mark on the post-check percent later, through the platform.

## Evidence for auditors

The awareness-measure record holds, per item: the id, form, verdict, chips, score, stage, variant and time, with no free text and no learner identity. Completion, and the before-and-after change for a cohort on matched items, support:

- CIS 14.4 (C13-B2, B3, B5) and CIS 14.5 (C13-B3, B5);
- ISO 27001 A.6.3;
- PCI DSS 12.6.3.2, with core_16;
- UK GDPR Art 5(1)(f) and Art 32(4);
- HIPAA 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(B) for US customers;
- NIS2 Art 21(2)(g) and NCSC CAF B6.b.

## Measuring effect

The product claims only a before-and-after change on matched items for a cohort, with no effectiveness claim. Outside the course, an organisation can watch (by department, never by named individual): authorised USB-drop tests (sticks handed in, plugged in, never returned), work email forwarded to personal addresses and uploads to unapproved cloud services, tool requests and how quickly IT answers them, reports of slow or broken tools (a rise is good news), and shadow IT found and moved into approved services.
