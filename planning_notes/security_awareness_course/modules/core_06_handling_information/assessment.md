## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once, show each of these or be taken back through the practice for it:

- **C06-CB1 Dispose of Confidential paper in the locked confidential waste** in the clear-out (C06-B10 at Secure), after Jo's audit has shown what happens to it anywhere else.
- **C06-CB2 Share a file only with specific people** (C06-B2 not at Not yet).
- **C06-CB3 Keep Confidential information out of personal accounts** (C06-B5 and C06-B6 not at Not yet).

The remediation loop follows `features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4. If a critical behaviour wasn't shown, the debrief says so without blame and Ama offers to go over it. She replays the matching "Label It" or sharing items with the explanation, then a fresh instance of the same step arrives from the bank in a short replay of the Apply beat: different drafts and bins, a different folder to share, a different colleague asking to use a personal account (BE-13). Shown after practice, the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting. Not shown, a second loop follows with a more direct prompt. After two loops the learner goes on to the check anyway: the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private "Label It" drill is scheduled two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21), and the organisation's training contact offers help. Nobody else is told by name, and every attempt is kept.

C06-B4 (the rival's price list) and C06-B7 (hidden content) are assessed and shown in the debrief but are not critical. A learner rated Not yet on either sees the matching consequence scene and the fix, and the organisation sees the result on its dashboard.

## Check and defend items (mirror pairs)

core_06 is delivered with core_05 as **E11** (new), a **consequence-flip** episode (R3): the learner mishandles information in a safe sandbox (the bin, the share dialog, the hidden content) and the flip shows what it exposed, then a scored **defend half** of label-and-route and send-or-share decisions. Only the defend half and the pre and post checks are scored (`passPercent` 0). The assessed behaviours and the question pool below map onto defend items and check items, each critical behaviour a **mirror pair** (aw5) tested by matched items across forms A and B. The checks use the **general** role, or the proposed all-staff data-handling check pair (**New**).

## Knowledge check bank

A pool of candidate items for the E11 defend set and the check forms, at least two per learning outcome so a retake draws a fresh set. Stems are short workplace situations. Labels are the company's defaults; they change with the organisation's profile (BE-05).

### C06-Q1 (C06-LO1, single choice)

A council sends Carrholme a site plan marked OFFICIAL-SENSITIVE. How do you handle it?

- A. As Public: it's the council's document, not ours. *Feedback: The marking means the council wants it handled with extra care.*
- **B. As at least Confidential, following any handling instructions the council gave.** *Feedback: Right. Follow the owner's marking.*
- C. As Internal: anyone at Carrholme can see it. *Feedback: Only the people working on that site need it.*
- D. Ask the council to remove the marking so it's easier to share. *Feedback: The marking is there for a reason. Handle it as marked.*

### C06-Q2 (C06-LO1, single choice)

You've put together a spreadsheet of your team's names and their sickness absence for your manager. Which label does it get?

- A. Internal: it's only going to your manager. *Feedback: It holds health data about named people. That needs more than Internal.*
- B. Confidential. *Feedback: Personal data is at least Confidential, but health data is special category, which Carrholme labels Restricted.*
- **C. Restricted.** *Feedback: Right. Special category data is Restricted at Carrholme.*
- D. No label: it's a working file. *Feedback: Label it when you create it, working file or not.*

(C06-Q2 depends on the organisation's label definitions. It is rewritten from the organisation profile (BE-05), or dropped where special category data has no label of its own.)

### C06-Q3 (C06-LO2, single choice)

You need to tell 200 care home families about a change of collection day. How do you send it?

- A. One email with everyone in the To field. *Feedback: Every family would see every other family's address, and that they have a relative in care.*
- B. One email with everyone in CC. *Feedback: CC shows the addresses just like To.*
- C. One email with everyone in Bcc. *Feedback: Bcc hides the addresses but is easy to get wrong, and the ICO says it's not enough on its own for mailings like this.*
- **D. The bulk email tool or mail merge, so each family gets its own email.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C06-Q4 (C06-LO2, single choice)

You've password-protected a file for a partner firm. How do you give them the password?

- A. In the same email as the file. *Feedback: Anyone who gets the email gets the key as well.*
- B. In a second email straight after. *Feedback: Anyone who can read the first email can read the second.*
- **C. By phone or text, a different way from the file.** *Feedback: Right. Keep the key apart from the lock.*
- D. In the file's name. *Feedback: That's the same as sending no password at all.*

### C06-Q5 (C06-LO2, single choice)

Your manager asks you to send the bid pack to his personal email so he can work on it on the train. What do you do?

- A. Send it: he's the bid lead. *Feedback: Seniority doesn't change where Confidential information may go.*
- B. Send just the pricing, which is all he needs. *Feedback: The pricing is the most sensitive part.*
- **C. Say no, kindly, and offer the approved way: his work laptop and the VPN.** *Feedback: Right. Saying no is easier with an alternative ready.*
- D. Send it password-protected. *Feedback: It still ends up in a personal account the organisation can't control.*

### C06-Q6 (C06-LO3, single choice)

You're sharing a folder with a partner firm for one week of work. Which settings?

- A. Anyone with the link, can edit. *Feedback: Anyone the link is forwarded to could open and change it.*
- B. Everyone at Carrholme. *Feedback: That's the wrong group, and it doesn't include the partner.*
- **C. Specific people, can comment, ending in a week.** *Feedback: Right: named people, the least access that works, and an end date.*
- D. Specific people, can edit, no end date. *Feedback: Closer, but more access, for longer, than they need.*

### C06-Q7 (C06-LO3, single choice)

You notice a folder of staff CVs from an old bid is still shared with "anyone with the link". What do you do?

- A. Leave it: nobody has the link any more. *Feedback: You can't know who has it.*
- **B. Close the link and report it.** *Feedback: Right. Closing it stops further access; reporting lets the security team and the DPO check whether anyone opened it.*
- C. Delete the whole folder. *Feedback: The owner may still need it, and the access history matters. Close the link and report it.*
- D. Email the folder's owner and wait for a reply. *Feedback: Close the link now; the owner can reopen access properly if it's needed.*

### C06-Q8 (C06-LO4, multiple select)

Before a spreadsheet leaves Carrholme, what do you check for? Choose all that apply.

- **A. Hidden worksheets, rows and columns.** *Feedback: Right. Hidden isn't removed.*
- **B. Comments and tracked changes.** *Feedback: Right. They travel with the file.*
- **C. Text that's only covered up or coloured to match the background.** *Feedback: Right. Covered isn't removed.*
- D. The font. *Feedback: The font can't leak anything.*

### C06-Q9 (C06-LO4, single choice)

A colleague has "redacted" a phone number in a PDF by drawing a black box over it. Is it safe to send?

- A. Yes: you can't see the number. *Feedback: The number may still be there under the box, ready to copy.*
- **B. Not yet: redact it with the redaction tool on a copy, then check the result.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Yes, once it's saved as a new PDF. *Feedback: Converting to PDF isn't a secure way to redact on its own.*
- D. Yes, once the text under the box is changed to white. *Feedback: White text can still be selected and copied.*

### C06-Q10 (C06-LO5, single choice)

You're clearing old drafts of the bid pricing off your desk. Where do they go?

- A. The paper recycling. *Feedback: Anyone handling the recycling can read them.*
- B. The strip-cut shredder by the printer. *Feedback: Strips can be pieced back together. At Carrholme that shredder is for Internal paper only.*
- **C. The locked confidential waste console.** *Feedback: Right.*
- D. Take them home to recycle. *Feedback: Confidential paper doesn't leave the building to be thrown away.*

### C06-Q11 (C06-LO5, single choice)

A partner firm accidentally emails you a rival bidder's price list. Your manager says, "Open it." What do you do?

- A. Open it: it landed in our lap. *Feedback: Using it would be wrong, and could get the bid excluded.*
- B. Forward it to your manager and let him decide. *Feedback: That spreads it further.*
- C. Delete it and say nothing. *Feedback: Better than opening it, but nobody can then show it wasn't used, and the sender doesn't know it went astray.*
- **D. Leave it unopened, tell your manager it can't be used, tell the sender and report it.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C06-Q12 (C06-LO5, single choice)

You find someone else's printout in the printer tray. The cover sheet says "HR: Confidential". What do you do?

- A. Read it to work out whose it is. *Feedback: The cover sheet tells you whose it is. Don't read the rest.*
- B. Leave it for them to find. *Feedback: Anyone else could find it first.*
- C. Put it in the recycling. *Feedback: Then anyone handling the recycling could read it.*
- **D. Take it to the owner unread, and make sure it's reported, because it was left in the open.** *Feedback: Right. If it holds personal data, the DPO needs to know (core_05).*

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken back through the practice for it (at most two loops), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card. If the organisation attaches its information handling or classification policy (HA-07), acknowledging it is part of completion.
- **No default pass mark.** The pre and post checks are `passPercent` 0: no pass or fail, and the missions are completion-scored. The post-check shows the change from the newest pre-check. A pass mark or certificate is a later organisation option set on the post-check percent, not a default.
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Ama replays the Learn beat (her part, not the bin audit) before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. Passing (the check passed and every critical behaviour shown first time) records the module as complete; otherwise the learner goes through the full module, with no "failed" label.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, whether the reference card was opened, and the policy acknowledgement where one was attached. This supports:

- CIS 14.4 and 14.5; ISO 27001 A.6.3; NIST AT-2; NCSC CAF B6.b; DSPT 3.2.1;
- UK GDPR Art 5(1)(f), Art 32 and Art 32(4), and the ICO training toolkit's data sharing and records management topics;
- NIS2 Art 21(2)(g) and IR 8.1, and DORA Art 13(6), for EU customers; FTC 314.4(e) for US customers;
- the organisation's own handling controls (classification, labelling, transfer, disposal), proposed for `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` in `README.md`.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **What's in the bins** (a periodic waste audit like Jo's): Confidential paper in general recycling should fall.
- **Over-shared links** (the file-sharing service's admin reports): links open to "anyone", and external shares with no end date, should fall.
- **Work information sent to personal accounts** (mail or data loss prevention reports, where they exist): should fall.
- **Misdirected emails reported:** may rise at first as reporting improves, then fall. **Uncollected secure print jobs:** should fall.
- **Devices returned to IT** against the asset register: unreturned devices should fall.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** (C06-B1 against C06-B10) across the organisation, in the course itself.

These need admin reports from the file-sharing service and the mail system, facilities' waste audits, and IT's asset register. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
