## Critical behaviours

Checked at the end of the Apply beat (BE-01). The hook is a baseline and never counts towards these.

- **R02-CB1 Verify a changed account before its first payment, on a number from before the change:** R02-B3 at Secure.
- **R02-CB2 Never bypass dual control:** R02-B6 not at Not yet.
- **R02-CB3 Ring the bank first when money has gone:** R02-B7 at Secure.

An organisation that takes card payments can make R02-B8 (not at Not yet) a fourth critical behaviour through its organisation profile (HA-11).

The remediation loop follows `features/assessment_and_evidence.md` section 4. If a critical behaviour wasn't shown, the Monday debrief says what happened without blame, and Eleni offers to go over it: *"That one catches a lot of good people. Let's try it once more."* She replays the matching Desk Check items with the explanation, then a fresh case of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the run (another supplier with a changed account, another cut-off squeeze, another colleague's diverted payment), drawn from the bank. A second loop, if needed, uses a different case and a more direct prompt. After two loops the learner goes on to the check: the record says "practised, not yet shown", the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting, and a private "Run Review" drill on the same behaviour is scheduled for two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21). Nobody else is told by name, and every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, one per learning outcome, from a bank of twelve with two items for every outcome. Suppliers, names and amounts are filled per play (BE-13).

### R02-Q1 (R02-LO1, single choice)

A supplier's bank details were changed through the supplier portal at 23:12 on Sunday. Today's run includes the first payment to the new account, and the name check says "No match". What do you do?

- A. Pay it: the change came through the supplier's own login. *Feedback: Logins get phished. A portal change is a claim like any other.*
- **B. Hold it, and ring the supplier on a number from before the change.** *Feedback: Right. A changed account, a first payment and a no match together are the classic pattern.*
- C. Email the supplier's accounts address to ask. *Feedback: If their email or portal account is compromised, the fraudster answers.*
- D. Retype the payee name until it matches. *Feedback: That hides the warning rather than answering it.*

### R02-Q2 (R02-LO1, single choice)

You're the second approver on a bank change. Your colleague's note says "Rang the number on our record, all fine." The change history shows the record's phone number was changed nine days ago "at the supplier's request by email". What do you do?

- A. Approve it: your colleague rang the record number. *Feedback: The record number may be the fraudster's, set up by that earlier request.*
- **B. Challenge it: ring the number from the original set-up file or contract, and look at both changes.** *Feedback: Right. A second approver checks the evidence, not just that there is some.*
- C. Ask your colleague to ring again. *Feedback: The same number would give the same answer.*

### R02-Q3 (R02-LO2, multiple select)

Which of these in a payment run should make you hold a payment? Choose all that apply.

- **A. The first payment to bank details changed last week.** *Feedback: The moment a changed account is paid is the moment the fraud pays out.*
- **B. A new payee set up at 19:42 by someone who is on annual leave.** *Feedback: Their account may have been used by someone else. Tell security too.*
- **C. Two suppliers paid into the same bank account.** *Feedback: One of them may be fake.*
- D. A supplier paid the same amount every month for six years, with no changes. *Feedback: Nothing has changed. Pay it as usual.*

### R02-Q4 (R02-LO2, hotspot)

Shown: a run row for "Ravensworth Advisory Ltd", £9,800, purchase order "none", set up "yesterday 19:42 by s.kaur (on leave)", name check "Match". Select the parts that should make you hold it. (List alternative: tick the fields.)

- **Correct: "none" (no purchase order), "19:42 by s.kaur (on leave)", and £9,800 (just under the £10,000 limit).** *Feedback: Nobody ordered it, it was set up by an account whose owner is away, and the amount is shaped to dodge the higher approval. The name check matching doesn't change any of that.*

### R02-Q5 (R02-LO3, single choice)

It's 15:50, the cut-off is 16:00 and the second approver is stuck in a meeting. A colleague says, "I've still got approver access from when I covered last year. I'll press it." What do you do?

- A. Let them: they've done it before. *Feedback: They're not an authorised approver now. Leftover access removes the real second check.*
- **B. Thank them, but ask the approver to step out for two minutes, or hold the run and tell suppliers it'll be late; and ask IT to remove the leftover access.** *Feedback: Right. Late is better than lost.*
- C. Approve it twice yourself from two screens. *Feedback: That's one person, twice.*

### R02-Q6 (R02-LO3, single choice)

A voicemail in the chief executive's voice says: "Release the payment today. I've approved it. Don't wait for the second approver." What do you do?

- **A. Check with the chief executive through a channel you already have, and keep to the two-approver process either way.** *Feedback: Right. Voices can be cloned (core_15), and the process applies even when it really is them.*
- B. Release it: you know her voice. *Feedback: A familiar voice isn't a check.*
- C. Ring back the number the voicemail came from. *Feedback: That may reach whoever left it.*

### R02-Q7 (R02-LO4, single choice)

An email from a colleague's personal address says: "New bank, please update my salary details before payday." What do you do?

- A. Update it: it has their name and staff number. *Feedback: Those are easy to find. This is how payroll diversion works.*
- **B. Don't change anything from an email. Ask them to change it in the HR system, confirm it with them in person or on the directory number, and report the email.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Reply asking them to confirm. *Feedback: The reply goes to whoever sent it.*

### R02-Q8 (R02-LO4, single choice)

A customer says it paid an invoice twice and asks for the refund to "our new account". What do you do?

- A. Refund to the new account as asked. *Feedback: A change of details plus urgency is the pattern, and refunds are a route for it.*
- **B. Check the ledger, then refund only to the account the money came from, confirming with the customer on the number on record.** *Feedback: Right. Refunds go back the way they came.*
- C. Refund half to each account. *Feedback: That still sends money to an unchecked account.*

### R02-Q9 (R02-LO5, ordering)

A colleague's pay went to a fraudster's account this morning. Put the first steps in order.

1. **Ring the bank's fraud line now, using the number from the bank's own details.**
2. **Stop any other payments to that account, and keep the email.**
3. **Tell the security team and the DPO.**
4. **Make sure the colleague is paid today.**
5. **Report it to Report Fraud (Police Scotland on 101 in Scotland) and note the reference.**

*Feedback: The bank first: it can only freeze money that hasn't been moved on. (Accept 2, 3 and 4 in any order.)*

### R02-Q10 (R02-LO5, single choice)

Kev's wages went to the wrong account. Who needs to ring their bank to try to recall the money?

- A. Kev, because it's his pay. *Feedback: Kev's bank never handled the payment. Make sure Kev is paid, though.*
- **B. Larkspur, through Finance, straight away: the paying organisation's bank starts the recall.** *Feedback: Right. Ring our bank's fraud line now.*
- C. Nobody: once it has gone, it has gone. *Feedback: Banks can sometimes freeze money that hasn't been moved on. Speed matters.*

### R02-Q11 (R02-LO6, single choice)

A care home manager emails a photo of the front and back of the home's card, "security code's on the back", to pay an overdue invoice. What do you do?

- A. Take the payment from the photo, then delete the email. *Feedback: Don't use card details that arrive by email. Follow the procedure instead.*
- **B. Don't use it; delete it by the card data procedure; reply without the card details, with the secure payment link; tell security as the procedure says.** *Feedback: Right. Card numbers never travel by email.*
- C. Save the photo in the customer's file for next time. *Feedback: Card security codes must never be kept after a payment, even with the customer's permission.*

### R02-Q12 (R02-LO6, multiple select)

Which of these are fine? Choose all that apply.

- **A. Card numbers shown masked on screen, with only the last four digits visible.** *Feedback: Masking is exactly what should happen.*
- **B. Taking a customer's card payment through the organisation's secure payment link.** *Feedback: The approved route.*
- C. Keeping a regular customer's security code so they don't have to read it out each time. *Feedback: Never, even if they agree.*
- D. Writing a card number on a sticky note to key in later. *Feedback: Card numbers never go on paper or in spreadsheets.*

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been through the remediation loop for it (at most two loops), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Outcome ratings** follow the default rule in `features/assessment_and_evidence.md` section 3: Apply evidence counts double, the hook counts nothing, and an outcome whose critical behaviour was shown only after remediation is at most Developing.
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Eleni replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it; recommended for annual renewals): the Apply beat and the check. It passes only if the check is passed and every critical behaviour is shown first time; otherwise the learner goes through the full module, with no "failed" label.
- **Prerequisite:** core_11 is recommended, not required. A learner who hasn't taken it hears Eleni's one-minute version of core_11's rule ("ring the number you already have") at the start of Learn.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. This supports (short references from `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`):

- ISO 27001 cl. 7.2 (documented evidence of competence for finance roles) and ISO 27001 A.6.3;
- NIST AT-3 and AT-2(3), with individual records for NIST AT-4;
- CIS 14.9 (role-specific training);
- PCI DSS 12.6.1 and 12.6.3.1, the training records PCI DSS 12.6.3 expects, and PCI DSS 9.5.1.3 in part where staff use card terminals;
- NCSC CAF B6.b (each person's training tracked);
- NIS2 IR 8.2 and DORA Art 13(6) where they apply; NYDFS 500.14(a)(3) and SOC 2 CC1.4 for US customers.

It also shows finance staff were trained in the organisation's own payment controls (segregation of duties, ISO 27001 A.5.3) and card data rules, which assessors, insurers and banks ask about after a payment fraud.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Call-back evidence:** the share of bank detail changes in the finance system's change log with a recorded call, and the share made to a number that predates the request. The target is all of them.
- **Holds per run** and what they turned out to be (frauds stopped, genuine payments delayed). A steady trickle of holds is healthy.
- **Name check overrides:** payments released after a "no match" without a recorded check, which should fall to none.
- **Approvals by the right people:** sign-offs by delegates rather than shared or leftover access; approver access removed in access reviews (with role_05).
- **Time from discovery to the bank's fraud line** in real incidents, from the incident log.
- **Payroll changes made outside the HR system,** which should be none.
- **Card data found where it shouldn't be:** card details received by email and deleted under the procedure, and any found in mailboxes, spreadsheets or on paper during audits.
- **Authorised tests** (Jo Fairweather's firm in the story): how many test change requests were verified on the number on record, and how many test payments were held.
- **Losses and recoveries** from payment fraud each year.

These need the finance system's change log and payment data, the incident log and access reviews, and the course record. The dashboard shows them by team, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
