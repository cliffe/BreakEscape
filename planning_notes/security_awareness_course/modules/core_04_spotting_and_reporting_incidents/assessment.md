# core_04 assessment

## Assessed behaviours

Each row is recorded through BE-01 and shown in the debrief and the learner's record. "How soon" is always measured in story events, never in minutes: whether the report came before the next story event after the player noticed the sign (that incident's next stage, or the afternoon's next interruption). BE-04's time scale and "timers off" don't change anyone's rating, and meeting a sign late, because of the order the player did the task in, costs nothing.

The **before and after** for each learner is C04-B1 against C04-B5: a colleague's incident each time, unannounced, measured by whether and how soon it got reported, and, in Apply, how kindly.

LO6 (why speed matters and what happens next) is evidenced by the stage at which each incident was reported (C04-B2, B3 and B5) and by check items C04-Q8 and C04-Q10.

## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must show:

- **C04-CB1** Reports the incidents they meet: at least three of the four Apply incidents (C04-B2 to C04-B5) reported before the Apply beat ends.
- **C04-CB2** Gets a colleague's mistake reported: C04-B5 not at Not yet.
- **C04-CB3** Keeps the evidence and says what they did: C04-B8 not at Not yet.

A learner who misses one goes through the course's remediation loop (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4): Ama says what happened without blame (*"That one slips past lots of people. Let's try it once more."*), replays the matching Learn chunk and Practise item, and a fresh incident of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the afternoon, drawn from the bank (BE-13): new signs in the shared systems or a device gone missing (CB1), a different colleague's mistake (CB2), a different sign that tempts a restart or a deletion (CB3). Shown after practice, the outcome is capped at Developing. After two loops without it, the learner goes on to the check; the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private "Report it or not?" drill is scheduled two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21), and the learner is offered help from the organisation's training contact. Nobody else is told by name.

Every attempt stays in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C04-Q1 (LO1, multiple select)

Which of these should you report to the security team? Choose all that apply.

- **A. A password reset email you didn't ask for.** *Feedback: Yes. Someone may be trying to get into your account.*
- **B. Your work badge, lost on the way home.** *Feedback: Yes. It can be cancelled before anyone uses it.*
- **C. A spreadsheet of customer details you sent to the wrong person.** *Feedback: Yes, straight away. The DPO will take it from there.*
- **D. Files on the shared drive that suddenly won't open.** *Feedback: Yes. That can be the first sign of something spreading.*
- E. The printer saying "paper jam". *Feedback: That's a normal IT ticket. If you aren't sure whether something is a security problem, report it anyway.*

### C04-Q2 (LO2, single choice)

It's 11pm and you realise you left your work phone on the train. What do you do?

- A. Wait until the morning and tell your manager. *Feedback: The phone may still be signed in to email. The 24/7 route exists for this.*
- **B. Report it now to the 24/7 service desk, so it can be locked and wiped.** *Feedback: Right. Minutes matter, even at night.*
- C. Ring the train company first and report it only if they don't have it. *Feedback: Ring them too, but report it first. It can be unlocked again if it turns up.*
- D. Change your email password from your laptop and leave it at that. *Feedback: Helpful, but the phone may hold other data and sessions. Report it.*

### C04-Q3 (LO3, single choice)

Which report helps the security team most?

- A. "Something's wrong with Leo's email." *Feedback: They'd have to ring you back to find out what, when and who.*
- B. "URGENT!!! We've been hacked!!" *Feedback: It gets attention, but tells them nothing they can act on.*
- **C. "At 13:50 Leo Brennan's mailbox sent invoice emails he didn't write, to customers including Brookfield Primary. He's at his desk. We haven't changed anything."** *Feedback: Right. What, when, where, who, and what's been done.*
- D. "I'll write it up properly at the end of the day." *Feedback: A rough report now beats a perfect one later.*

### C04-Q4 (LO4, ordering)

Your screen fills with files you can't open and a note asking for payment. Put these in order.

1. **Leave everything as it is: don't close, delete or restart anything.**
2. **Ring the security team (or the 24/7 service desk).**
3. **Tell them what you see and what you've done.**
4. **Follow their instructions, such as unplugging the network cable.**

*Feedback: Leaving it as it is keeps the evidence and stops you making it worse; ringing straight away means they can stop it spreading. (Accept 1 and 2 in either order.)*

### C04-Q5 (LO4, single choice)

You opened an attachment and now your laptop is acting strangely. Should you switch it off?

- A. Yes, straight away, to stop whatever it is. *Feedback: Switching off can destroy evidence and isn't always what's needed. Ask first.*
- **B. Not unless you're told to. Report it now and follow the service desk's instructions; they may ask you to unplug the network cable and leave it on.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Run a virus scan and see what it finds. *Feedback: Don't investigate it yourself. Report it.*

### C04-Q6 (LO5, single choice)

You've just sent a spreadsheet with customer contact details to the wrong person. What do you do?

- A. Email them asking them to delete it, and leave it there if they agree. *Feedback: Asking is reasonable, but the organisation still needs to know: it may have to tell the customer or the regulator.*
- **B. Report it now through the usual route, even though it was your mistake.** *Feedback: Right. Reporting fast is what's thanked, and it lets the DPO put things right.*
- C. Wait to see whether the recipient says anything. *Feedback: Every hour counts, for the people in the spreadsheet and for the organisation's legal deadlines.*
- D. Tell the customer yourself. *Feedback: Leave that to the DPO, who will do it properly.*

### C04-Q7 (LO5, single choice)

A colleague tells you they did something similar and asks you not to tell anyone. What do you say?

- **A. "Let's report it together now. It'll take two minutes, and you'll be thanked, not told off."** *Feedback: Right. If they still won't, report it yourself and tell them you have.*
- B. "Your secret's safe with me." *Feedback: Kind, but it leaves the problem growing and them on their own with it.*
- C. "That's on you." *Feedback: It's true, but it makes them less likely to report, now or next time.*

### C04-Q8 (LO6, single choice)

Why does reporting quickly matter so much?

- A. So the person responsible can be found. *Feedback: Reports are about stopping harm, not finding someone to blame.*
- **B. Because most damage happens between noticing and reporting, and the team can only stop it once they know.** *Feedback: Right. The organisation also has legal deadlines that start when it becomes aware.*
- C. Because reports are counted for league tables. *Feedback: No. Speed is about limiting harm.*

### C04-Q9 (LO1, true or false with reason)

"If I'm not sure it's a security incident, I shouldn't waste the security team's time."

- A. True: only report things you're sure about. *Feedback: Most incidents start as something that might be nothing. The team decides.*
- **B. False: report it anyway. A false alarm costs minutes; a missed incident can cost months.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C04-Q10 (LO6, single choice)

What usually happens after you report something?

- A. You're asked to fix it yourself. *Feedback: No. The team handles it and tells you what, if anything, to do.*
- **B. The team works out how serious it is, stops it spreading, investigates and puts things right, and may ask you a few questions.** *Feedback: Right. They'll also decide whether anyone outside needs to be told.*
- C. You're disciplined if it turns out to be your fault. *Feedback: No. Innocent mistakes aren't punished: reporting is what's wanted.*
- D. Nothing, unless it's serious. *Feedback: Every report is looked at, and many small ones turn out to matter.*

### C04-Q11 (LO2, single choice)

You see someone you don't recognise in the corridor by the comms room, and its door is propped open. What do you do?

- A. Nothing: they're probably meant to be there. *Feedback: They may be, and a quick check costs nothing. Report it.*
- **B. Report it to security or facilities now; if you're comfortable, ask politely if you can help them find someone.** *Feedback: Right. You don't have to confront anyone.*
- C. Close the door and walk on. *Feedback: Good instinct, but someone may be inside. Report it.*

### C04-Q12 (LO1, LO2, single choice)

You get a sign-in prompt on your phone that you didn't start, and you press Deny. Anything else?

- A. No: denying it was enough. *Feedback: Denying is right, but it means someone has your password.*
- **B. Yes: report it, so your password can be changed and the attempt checked.** *Feedback: Right. See core_03.*
- C. Turn your phone off for the day. *Feedback: That stops the prompts, not the person with your password.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Think of something odd you noticed at work and didn't mention. Who would you tell now, and what would you say?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What makes people here hesitate before reporting, especially their own mistakes, and what would change that?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken through the remediation loop for it (at most two loops), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Ama replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check, without Learn and Practise. Passing records the module as complete; missing a critical behaviour sends the learner through the full module, with no "failed" label.
- **Fair timing:** with BE-04's time scale or with timers off, the spread follows the clock or the task's steps, and every "how soon" rating counts story events after the player noticed the sign, so the text route and every setting give the same evidence.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown, shown after practice, or practised and not yet shown, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. For this module it also lists **each report the learner made in play**: the incident, the stage it had reached and the completeness score (no free text). This supports:

- ISO 27001 A.6.8 (the reporting route used, with its records), ISO 27001 cl. 7.3 and ISO 27001 A.6.3; ISO 27002 6.3 (reporting security events as a basic procedure);
- NIST AT-2 and NIST AT-2(4);
- CIS 14.6;
- PCI DSS 12.6.1;
- NCSC CAF B6.a (people know how to raise a concern; reporting treated positively) and NCSC CAF B6.b (individual training tracked);
- NIS2 Art 21(2)(g) and NIS2 IR 8.1;
- UK GDPR Art 33 (staff report possible breaches internally at once) and the ICO training toolkit (personal data breaches in the all-staff programme);
- DSPT 2.1.1 and 3.2.1 (categories 3 and 4), for NHS customers;
- SOC 2 CC2.2 and HIPAA 164.308(a)(5)(ii)(B) and (C), for US customers.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch (the measures `features/assessment_and_evidence.md` puts first are report rate and time to first report):

- **Time to report:** the median time from the first sign of an incident to the first report, from the incident log. It should fall.
- **Reports per 100 staff,** including near misses and **self-reported mistakes**. A rise is good news: it means people feel safe to tell.
- **Who found it first:** the share of incidents first reported by staff, compared with those found by tools, customers or third parties.
- **Report quality:** how often the security team has to call back for what, when, where, who or what was done.
- **Lost and stolen devices:** the delay between losing a device and reporting it, including out of hours.
- **Report-button data** for suspicious messages (HA-12): report rate and time to first report for each campaign.
- **Confidence to report:** a short survey item, "I would feel comfortable reporting my own mistake" (HA-14), which tracks the NCSC's culture principle on openness.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** across the organisation in the course itself.

These need the organisation's incident or ticket log with timestamps, report-button data and a short survey. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09). Reports must never be used to rank or single out the people who made the mistakes they describe.
