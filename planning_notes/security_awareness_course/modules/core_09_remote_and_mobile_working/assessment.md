## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must show each of these, or be taken back through the practice for it:

- **C09-CB1 Keep client details out of other people's sight and hearing** in the Apply beat (C09-B3 or C09-B9 at Secure).
- **C09-CB2 Keep devices with them or locked away** (C09-B4 and C09-B6 not at Not yet).
- **C09-CB3 Report a lost device the same evening** (C09-B7 not at Not yet).

Signing in on the fake Wi-Fi page (C09-B2 at Not yet) is not a critical behaviour here, because core_01 and core_03 build the habit of never signing in through an unexpected page. It still rules out Secure for C09-LO3, and Kai's follow-up message gives the player the chance to report it.

**The remediation loop** (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4):

1. Zainab says what happened without blame and replays the relevant Practise round with her explanation (one to two minutes).
2. A fresh situation of the same kind arrives in a short replay drawn from the micro-drill bank: an airport gate instead of the café, a phone left on a bus instead of the tablet, a client call at a station.
3. Shown: recorded as "shown after practice", and the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting.
4. Not shown: a second loop, with a different situation and a more direct prompt from Zainab.
5. After two loops the learner goes on to the check anyway. The record says "practised, not yet shown", a private practice drill is scheduled for two to four weeks later (HA-21), and the learner is offered help from the organisation's training contact. Nobody else is told by name.

Every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C09-Q1 (C09-LO1, hotspot, easy)

Shown: a train carriage from above, with passengers in their seats. You need to read a Confidential spreadsheet. Click the seat where nobody can read your screen.

- **Correct hotspot:** the single seat with its back to the bulkhead. *Feedback: Right. With nobody behind you, only the person beside you could see, and a privacy screen deals with that.*
- **Aisle seat with passengers behind:** *Feedback: The people in the row behind can see straight over your shoulder.*
- **Table seat facing strangers:** *Feedback: The people opposite can't see your screen, but the people behind them and across the aisle can.*
- **List alternative** (keyboard and screen reader users): the seats as buttons with descriptions ("aisle seat, two passengers behind you").

### C09-Q2 (C09-LO1, single choice, easy)

You're on a busy train and want to check a list of customers' names and addresses before a meeting. What's best?

- A. Open it quickly and close it again. *Feedback: A few seconds is enough for someone to read a name or take a photo.*
- **B. Leave it for somewhere private, or sit with your back to a wall with your privacy screen on.** *Feedback: Right. Work on something less sensitive while you travel.*
- C. Turn the screen brightness right down. *Feedback: It makes it harder for you to read, not much harder for others.*
- D. Ask the person next to you not to look. *Feedback: Polite, but it draws attention to it, and they're not the only one who can see.*

### C09-Q3 (C09-LO2, single choice, easy)

You're in a café queue when a client rings and asks you to read out the alarm code for their building. What do you say?

- A. Read it out quietly. *Feedback: Everyone in the queue can still hear it.*
- **B. "I'll call you back in five minutes from somewhere private," or send it through the approved secure route.** *Feedback: Right. "I'll call you back" is a complete answer.*
- C. Text it to them now. *Feedback: Ordinary texts aren't the place for codes. Use the approved route.*
- D. Spell it out using the phonetic alphabet. *Feedback: It's still the code, said aloud in a queue.*

### C09-Q4 (C09-LO3, single choice, medium)

At a hotel you join the guest Wi-Fi. A page appears asking for your work email address and password "to continue". What do you do?

- A. Type them in: hotels often ask you to sign in. *Feedback: A hotel's page may ask for your room number and surname. It has no reason to want your work password.*
- **B. Close the page, disconnect, use your phone's hotspot, and report it.** *Feedback: Right. A Wi-Fi page asking for your work password is a fake.*
- C. Type a different password to see what happens. *Feedback: Don't test it. Close it and report it.*
- D. Use the network, but don't open anything important. *Feedback: You're still on a network that someone set up to steal passwords. Use the hotspot.*

### C09-Q5 (C09-LO3, multiple select, medium)

You're about to work from a café. Which of these are good habits? Choose all that apply.

- **A. Ask the staff for the Wi-Fi network's name.** *Feedback: Lookalike networks often have nearly the same name.*
- **B. Wait for the VPN to show it's connected before opening work.** *Feedback: The VPN protects your data on its way to the company.*
- C. Pick whichever network has the strongest signal. *Feedback: A fake network can easily have the strongest signal. Check the name.*
- **D. Use your phone's hotspot if you're not sure.** *Feedback: Your phone's own mobile connection is the simple safe choice.*
- E. Accept the café's terms and type your work password into its page. *Feedback: Accept the terms and nothing more. Never your work password.*

(The overall feedback adds: public Wi-Fi isn't "never safe" for work; with the venue's real network and the VPN connected, most work is fine. Where the organisation sets "phone hotspot only", options A and B are reworded to that rule and D becomes the only safe choice.)

### C09-Q6 (C09-LO2, true or false with reason, easy)

"If I'm wearing headphones on the train, nobody can hear my work call."

- A. True: the other person's voice only goes into my ears. *Feedback: That's half of it. Everyone nearby still hears your side: the names, the codes, the client.*
- **B. False: people can still hear everything I say. If it's confidential, I'll call back from somewhere private.** *Feedback: Right. "I'll call you back" is a complete answer.*

### C09-Q7 (C09-LO4, single choice, easy)

You're working in a café and need the toilet. The friendly person at the next table offers to watch your laptop. What do you do?

- A. Thank them and go: you'll only be a minute. *Feedback: They're probably lovely. They're also a stranger, and anyone can walk past.*
- B. Lock the screen and leave it with them. *Feedback: Better, but the laptop itself can still walk off.*
- **C. Thank them, and take the laptop with you.** *Feedback: Right. Your devices stay with you, or locked away.*

### C09-Q8 (C09-LO5, ordering, hard)

At 21:30 in your hotel room you realise your work phone isn't in your bag. Put these in order.

1. **Ring the service desk now.**
2. **Tell them what it is, when and where you last had it, and what was on it.**
3. **Ring the place you last had it (the venue's lost property).**
4. **Follow the service desk's instructions, such as moving your sign-in app to another device.**

*Feedback: Reporting first matters most: the service desk can lock it within minutes, and the venue may not answer until morning. (Accept 3 before 4 in either position after 2.)*

### C09-Q9 (C09-LO5, single choice, medium)

Your work tablet has a PIN, and you think you left it on the train. Should you report it tonight?

- A. No: it's locked, so nobody can use it. *Feedback: The lock buys time. Reporting lets the service desk sign it out of company services and wipe it.*
- **B. Yes, straight away. If it turns up, they'll switch it back on.** *Feedback: Right. The hours between losing it and reporting it are what matter.*
- C. Only if it hasn't turned up by tomorrow. *Feedback: By tomorrow it could have been in someone else's hands all night.*
- D. Only if it had personal data on it. *Feedback: You may not know everything that's on it or signed in. Report it either way.*

### C09-Q10 (C09-LO4, single choice, easy)

You're working from home. Your partner's laptop has broken and they ask to borrow your work laptop to pay a bill. What do you say?

- A. "Go ahead, I'm logged in." *Feedback: Anything they do happens in your name, on a device with work on it.*
- B. "OK, but I'll stay with you." *Feedback: Kind, but a work device is still for work only.*
- **C. "Not this one, it's for work. Use my phone, or the family tablet."** *Feedback: Right. Offer another device.*

### C09-Q11 (C09-LO6, hotspot, multiple, hard)

Shown: a camera preview of your kitchen before a client video call. Click everything you'd sort out before joining.

- **Correct hotspots:** the whiteboard with a client's door code; the printout of names pinned to the wall; the smart speaker on the shelf (for a confidential call). *Feedback: Right. Wipe or turn the whiteboard, take the printout down, mute the speaker, or use background blur.*
- **Not needed:** the plant; the family photo. *Feedback: Personal items are your choice. Client details and listening devices are what matter here.*
- **List alternative:** the items in the picture as checkboxes.

### C09-Q12 (C09-LO6, single choice, easy)

You need to show a client one document on a video call. You have your inbox and another client's price list open too. What do you do?

- A. Share your whole screen: it's easier. *Feedback: They'll see your inbox, pop-ups and the other client's prices.*
- **B. Share just that document's window.** *Feedback: Right. Share the window, not the screen.*
- C. Turn the camera off and share the screen. *Feedback: The camera isn't the problem. The shared screen shows everything.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Picture the last place you worked outside the office. Who could have seen your screen, and who could have heard you?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What would make it easier for someone here to ring the service desk at nine at night about a lost phone?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken through the remediation loop for it, passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Zainab replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it, off by default): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. It passes when the check is passed and every critical behaviour is shown first time; otherwise the learner goes through the full module, with no "failed" label. Recommended for annual renewals rather than a first sitting.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown, shown after practice or practised and not yet shown, the check score, and whether the reference card was opened. Using the short references in `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`, section 2, this supports:

- CIS 14.5 (including losing a portable device) and CIS 14.8 (insecure networks, including home networks);
- DSPT 6.3.2 (categories 3 and 4), where the organisation sets the "phone hotspot only" rule, and DSPT 3.2.1 (categories 3 and 4) as part of the annual figure;
- ISO 27001 A.6.3 and NIST AT-2;
- NCSC CAF B6.b (individual training records);
- NIS2 Art 21(2)(g), NIS2 IR 8.1 and DORA Art 13(6);
- UK GDPR Art 5(1)(f) and Art 32, as part of showing that staff are trained to protect personal data outside the office;
- FTC 314.4(e) for US customers;
- PCI DSS 12.6.3.2 (acceptable use of end-user technologies), once the mapping's row is updated.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Time from loss to report** for lost and stolen devices (service desk tickets): the median, the share reported within an hour, and the share reported out of hours, which should rise.
- **Devices wiped or locked before any use elsewhere**, from device management.
- **Sign-ins from unfamiliar networks** and password resets after fake Wi-Fi pages, from the identity provider; reports of fake networks by staff.
- **Privacy screen requests** and the number of laptops with one issued.
- **Video call incidents** reported (client details visible, wrong screen shared).
- **The hook-to-Apply change** across the organisation (C09-B1 against C09-B9).

These need service desk, device management and sign-in data. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
