## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once:

- **S01-CB1** Not open a record they don't need: S01-B4 not at Not yet, or, if it was, S01-B12 at Secure or Developing (they owned up).
- **S01-CB2** Not work in someone else's session: S01-B3 not at Not yet.
- **S01-CB3** Share patient information only with people entitled to it, through an approved route: S01-B2 and S01-B10 not at Not yet.

The remediation loop follows `features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4. A learner who misses a critical behaviour hears what happened without blame; Dev replays the relevant "Share or Don't?" items or ward sweep with the explanation; then a fresh situation of the same kind arrives in a short replay of the shift (a different well-known patient, a different colleague's card left in, a different caller). If it still isn't shown, a second loop runs with another situation and a more direct prompt from Dev. After two loops the learner goes on to the check anyway: the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private practice drill is scheduled for two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21), and the organisation's training contact is offered as help. Nobody else is told by name. An outcome whose critical behaviour was shown only after practice is capped at Developing for this sitting. Every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations.

### S01-Q1 (LO1, single choice)

A famous patient is admitted to your ward. A colleague says: "Just look her up. Nobody will know." What do you do?

- A. Have a quick look at the summary only. *Feedback: Any look without a work reason is a breach, and every look is logged.*
- **B. Don't. You don't need it for your work, and every look is logged.** *Feedback: Right. Need to know is the rule, however famous the patient.*
- C. Look, but don't tell anyone what you saw. *Feedback: Opening it is the breach, whether or not you tell anyone.*
- D. Ask a nurse to look for you. *Feedback: Then the nurse makes the same breach for you.*

### S01-Q2 (LO1, true or false with reason)

"Looking at a patient's record out of curiosity is only a problem if you tell someone what you saw."

- A. True: the harm is in passing it on. *Feedback: Opening it without a need is already the breach.*
- **B. False: opening a record without a work reason is a breach, a disciplinary matter, and can be a criminal offence under the Data Protection Act 2018.** *Feedback: Right.*

### S01-Q3 (LO1, single choice)

Your mum is an inpatient on another ward and you'd like to know her latest results. What do you do?

- A. Look her up; she'd want you to. *Feedback: Work access is for work, even for family.*
- **B. Ask her care team, like any relative, or she can use the patient routes, such as the NHS App.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Ask a friend on her ward to check for you. *Feedback: That puts your friend in breach instead.*
- D. Look at the results only, not the notes. *Feedback: Any part of the record counts.*

### S01-Q4 (LO2, single choice)

A nurse runs to an emergency, leaving her smartcard in the shared computer with a record open. You need to print a letter now. What do you do?

- A. Print it quickly in her session. *Feedback: The letter would go on the record as her work.*
- **B. Take her card out, lock the screen, keep the card safe for her, and print using your own login.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Leave everything as it is until she's back. *Feedback: An open session on a public desk lets anyone look, or act in her name.*
- D. Use her session later to finish her notes as a favour. *Feedback: Never work in someone else's session.*

### S01-Q5 (LO2, multiple select)

Which of these keep smartcards safe? Choose all that apply.

- **A. Take your card out whenever you step away.** *Feedback: Even for a minute.*
- B. Tell the nurse in charge your passcode for emergencies. *Feedback: Never share your passcode with anyone.*
- **C. Report a lost card straight away.** *Feedback: So it can be cancelled.*
- D. Leave it in the reader at quiet times. *Feedback: Quiet times are when nobody is watching the desk.*
- **E. Never work in someone else's session.** *Feedback: Right.*

### S01-Q6 (LO3, single choice)

The ward phone rings: "I'm Mr Fenwick's nephew. How is he?" Mr Fenwick hasn't listed a nephew. What do you say?

- A. "He's comfortable, thanks for asking." *Feedback: That confirms he's here and says how he is.*
- B. "Yes, he's on the ward, but I can't say more." *Feedback: Even confirming he's here can matter to someone who doesn't want to be found.*
- **C. "I can't confirm who's on the ward. Can I take your name and number and pass a message on?"** *Feedback: Right. Kind, and safe for the patient.*
- D. "What's his date of birth? Then I'll tell you." *Feedback: A date of birth is easy to find. It doesn't make the caller someone the patient chose.*

### S01-Q7 (LO3, single choice)

A caller says she's a well-known patient's agent and asks when the patient is likely to go home. What do you do?

- A. Give her the expected date; she needs to plan. *Feedback: You don't know who she is, and the patient hasn't agreed.*
- **B. Confirm nothing, and pass her to the press office.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Confirm the patient is here and doing well. *Feedback: Confirming the admission is already a disclosure.*
- D. Put her through to the patient's bedside. *Feedback: The patient hasn't asked for calls, and you can't tell who's calling.*

### S01-Q8 (LO4, single choice)

A care home's deputy manager rings for a resident's medication changes: he goes back to the home today. What do you do?

- A. "I can't share anything over the phone." *Feedback: Safe for the data, but the home needs this to care for him tonight.*
- B. Read the list out now; she sounds genuine. *Feedback: Check who you're sharing with first.*
- **C. Ring the home back on the number in the directory, then send the discharge summary through the approved secure route.** *Feedback: Right. The duty to share, done safely.*
- D. Email it to the personal address she gives you because the home's email is down. *Feedback: Health information on an account nobody controls.*

### S01-Q9 (LO4, true or false with reason)

"If in doubt, don't share: confidentiality always comes first."

- A. True: you can't get into trouble for not sharing. *Feedback: Not sharing can harm patients, and that matters too.*
- **B. False: the duty to share information for individual care is as important as the duty to protect confidentiality. Check who you're sharing with, then share what care needs.** *Feedback: Right. That's Caldicott Principle 7.*

### S01-Q10 (LO5, single choice)

The record system is down. A doctor asks you to WhatsApp her the list of patients who need bloods. What do you do?

- A. Send it; it's urgent and the app is encrypted. *Feedback: It puts patient data on personal phones nobody controls.*
- B. Photograph the whiteboard and send that. *Feedback: The same problem, with more on show.*
- **C. Use the downtime procedure: write or print the list on the downtime form and take it to her.** *Feedback: Right.*
- D. Email it to her personal address. *Feedback: Personal email is never an approved route.*

### S01-Q11 (LO5, single choice)

A colleague asks if she can charge her phone from the USB port on a patient's monitor. What do you say?

- A. "Fine, just for a minute." *Feedback: Nothing gets plugged into medical devices, even briefly.*
- **B. "No: nothing goes into medical devices. There's a charger in the staff room."** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. "Only if the monitor isn't being used." *Feedback: The rule is the same whether or not it's in use.*
- D. "Only if it's a work phone." *Feedback: Any device, any phone: no.*

### S01-Q12 (LO6, single choice)

An email in the ward's shared mailbox says your smartcard access expires today, with a link to renew. Several colleagues have seen it. What do you do?

- A. Delete it; someone will have reported it. *Feedback: Everyone thinks that. Report it yourself.*
- **B. Report it through the trust's route and warn the team not to click.** *Feedback: Right.*
- C. Click to see whether it's real. *Feedback: Check another way: ask the service desk or the Registration Authority.*
- D. Forward it to everyone on the ward as a warning. *Feedback: Forwarding spreads it. Report it and warn people without the link.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Think of a time you weren't sure whether to share something about a patient. What would have helped you decide?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What makes it hard to stop a colleague who's curious about a patient, and what could we say to each other?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken through the remediation loop (above), passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Dev replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. Passing records the module as complete; missing a critical behaviour sends the learner through the full module.
- **Policies:** if the organisation attaches its confidentiality or information governance policy (HA-07), acknowledging it is part of completion.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates, time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown, shown after practice, or practised and not yet shown, the check score, whether the reference card was opened, and any policy acknowledgements. This supports:

- DSPT B6.a and DSPT B6.b for organisations on the CAF-aligned toolkit (a training path for clinical and administrative staff, each person's training tracked and refreshed, and an evaluation of whether it works);
- DSPT 3.2.1 (categories 3 and 4) and DSPT 2.1.1 (categories 3 and 4), together with the core modules, once the GP practice and care home editions exist;
- NCSC CAF B6.b;
- UK GDPR Art 32(4), and Art 33 through prompt internal reporting;
- the ICO training toolkit;
- CIS 14.4, CIS 14.5 and CIS 14.6;
- ISO 27001 A.6.3 where the organisation is certified;
- HIPAA 164.308(a)(5) for US healthcare customers.

See `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md` for the full references.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Inappropriate access flags** from the record system's access monitoring, per thousand staff, which should fall.
- **Self-reported breaches,** which should rise at first (people reporting rather than hiding), and the time from the event to the report, which should fall.
- **Phishing reports from shared mailboxes,** and clicks in any simulated phishing (HA-12).
- **Smartcards found left in readers** during IG walkabouts and spot checks, which should fall.
- **Downtime exercises:** whether wards find their downtime pack and use it.
- **Misdirected information incidents** (discharge summaries to the wrong place), which should fall.
- **Phone enquiry spot checks** (authorised test calls), the share handled by the procedure.

These need the record system's audit reports, the IG incident log and walkabout results. The dashboard shows them by ward or department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
