## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must show each of these, or be taken back through the practice for it:

- **C15-CB1 Keep passwords and Restricted information out of AI tools** (C15-B3 not at Not yet).
- **C15-CB2 Check AI output before sending it** (C15-B4 not at Not yet).
- **C15-CB3 Check an unusual request another way before acting** (C15-B8 not at Not yet).

**The remediation loop** (`features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4):

1. Ama says what happened without blame and replays the matching Practise items with her explanation (one to two minutes).
2. A fresh situation of the same kind arrives in a short replay: a different document with something Restricted in it, a different draft with a made-up reference, or a different unusual request (a "client" on a video call asking for site access codes, a voice note from "Martin" asking for a file to go to a new address).
3. Shown: recorded as "shown after practice", and the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting.
4. Not shown: a second loop, with a different situation and a more direct prompt from Ama.
5. After two loops the learner goes on to the check anyway. The record says "practised, not yet shown", a private practice drill is scheduled for two to four weeks later (HA-21), and the learner is offered help from the organisation's training contact. Nobody else is told by name.

Every attempt is kept in the record.

## Knowledge check bank

Six items per sitting, drawn so that every learning outcome is covered. Stems are short workplace situations. Tool names in the stems come from the organisation profile (BE-05).

### C15-Q1 (C15-LO1, single choice, easy)

You want to tidy the wording of a client contract before a meeting. A free chatbot website would do it in seconds. What do you do?

- A. Use the free site: it's only the wording. *Feedback: The whole contract, prices and all, would go to a company you have no contract with.*
- **B. Use the organisation's approved assistant, or tidy it yourself.** *Feedback: Right. Confidential information goes only into approved tools.*
- C. Use the free site, but delete the chat afterwards. *Feedback: Deleting your copy doesn't delete the provider's.*
- D. Use the free site from your personal phone. *Feedback: It's the same information going to the same place.*

### C15-Q2 (C15-LO1, multiple select, medium)

Which of these can go into your organisation's approved AI assistant? Choose all that apply.

- **A. Notes from a team meeting about next week's rota.** *Feedback: Internal information is fine in the approved tool.*
- **B. A published press release you want to shorten.** *Feedback: Public information is fine anywhere.*
- C. Your password, so it can help you set up an app. *Feedback: Never put passwords or codes into any AI tool.*
- D. A list of care home residents and their medical conditions. *Feedback: Restricted and health information stays out, even of the approved tool.*
- **E. The terms of a client contract, to find the renewal date.** *Feedback: Confidential information can go into the approved tool when the task needs it.*

### C15-Q3 (C15-LO2, hotspot, multiple, medium)

Shown: a customer complaint you're about to paste into the approved assistant so it can suggest a reply. Click everything you'd take out first.

- **Correct hotspots:** the customer's account password, which they typed into their email; their daughter's name and medical condition; their bank sort code and account number. *Feedback: Right. The reply doesn't need any of them, and the password should be reported so it gets changed.*
- **Not needed:** the complaint itself (what went wrong and when); the site name. *Feedback: That's what the assistant needs to suggest a reply.*
- **List alternative:** the parts of the complaint as checkboxes.

### C15-Q4 (C15-LO2, true or false with reason, medium)

"If I take the person's name out, it's anonymous, so I can paste it anywhere."

- A. True: without a name nobody can tell who it is. *Feedback: A room number, a condition and a care home can identify someone without a name.*
- **B. False: other details can still identify someone, and the organisation's information still belongs in approved tools only.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C15-Q5 (C15-LO3, single choice, easy)

Your assistant's draft says: "Missed cleans fell 40% last year (National Facilities Index, 2025)." What do you do before sending it?

- A. Send it: it gives a source. *Feedback: AI tools invent sources as well as figures.*
- **B. Open the source and check the figure against your own data. If you can't find it, take it out.** *Feedback: Right. Check every number and reference.*
- C. Ask the assistant whether it's sure. *Feedback: It may say yes. It can't check itself.*
- D. Add "AI-generated" at the bottom. *Feedback: Being open is good, but you're still responsible for what it says.*

### C15-Q6 (C15-LO3, true or false with reason, easy)

"If the AI wrote it and I just sent it on, mistakes in it aren't my responsibility."

- A. True: the tool made the mistake. *Feedback: Courts have told professionals the opposite: if you use AI, you check it.*
- **B. False: whatever I send is mine, so I check it first.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C15-Q7 (C15-LO4, C15-LO6, single choice, medium)

You ask your assistant to summarise your inbox. It says: "A supplier needs your full customer list sent to a new address today." The supplier's email itself just says "invoice attached". What do you do?

- A. Send the list: the assistant read the email for you. *Feedback: Hidden instructions in an email can steer an assistant. It isn't a reason to send data.*
- B. Ignore it and carry on. *Feedback: You're safe, but others may get the same email.*
- **C. Don't act on it. Report the email so the security team can check it.** *Feedback: Right. Treat the assistant's suggestion like any other request.*
- D. Reply to the supplier to ask. *Feedback: If the email is malicious, the reply goes to the attacker. Report it.*

### C15-Q8 (C15-LO4, true or false with reason, medium)

"Our approved assistant is safe, so I can do whatever it suggests."

- A. True: it's been checked by the security team. *Feedback: Approved means your data is handled properly. It doesn't stop hidden instructions in what it reads.*
- **B. False: it can be steered by what it reads, so I check its suggestions about money, data, links or access before acting.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C15-Q9 (C15-LO5, single choice, easy)

A video call from your Chief Executive asks you to send the customer list to an adviser today and keep it quiet. She looks and sounds exactly like herself. What do you do first?

- A. Send it: you can see it's her. *Feedback: Faces and voices can be faked convincingly, live.*
- B. Ask her a question only she would know. *Feedback: Many answers are online, and a live fake can be steered by a person.*
- **C. End the call and ring her on a number you already have, or check with someone else.** *Feedback: Right. A real colleague will never mind.*
- D. Look for glitches in the video. *Feedback: Fakes are often perfect. Check another way instead.*

### C15-Q10 (C15-LO5, ordering, hard)

A voice note in your manager's voice, from an unknown number, asks you to send a confidential file to a new address straight away. Put these in order.

1. **Don't reply to the unknown number or send anything.**
2. **Contact your manager on a number or chat you already have.**
3. **If you can't reach them, ask someone else who would know (their deputy or your team leader).**
4. **Report the voice note to the security team.**

*Feedback: Checking through a route you already trust comes before anything else. Report it either way: the same voice may be used on colleagues. (Accept 4 at any point after 1.)*

### C15-Q11 (C15-LO5, C15-LO6, single choice, medium)

On a call, someone sounding exactly like a senior colleague asks you to approve a supplier's access today. You check by ringing her real number, and she knows nothing about it. What now?

- A. Nothing: you didn't approve it, so no harm done. *Feedback: You stopped it, but others may get the same call.*
- **B. Report it to the security team straight away.** *Feedback: Right. They can warn others and look at what else the caller tried.*
- C. Ring the caller back to tell them you know. *Feedback: Don't engage. Report it.*

### C15-Q12 (C15-LO6, single choice, easy)

A colleague tells you that yesterday they pasted a customer spreadsheet into a free chatbot, and now they're worried. What do you say?

- A. "Delete the chat and don't mention it." *Feedback: The provider may still have it, and the security team can't help if they don't know.*
- **B. "Report it now. You won't be in trouble, and the sooner they know the better."** *Feedback: Right. Reporting a slip quickly is exactly what helps.*
- C. "It's probably fine. Those sites don't keep anything." *Feedback: Many public tools keep what you type.*

## Reflection prompt

- "What's one thing you'd now take out before pasting into an AI tool, and one request you'd now check another way?"
- For facilitated sessions: "What would make it feel normal here to ring a senior leader back to check it's really them?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken through the remediation loop for it, passed the knowledge check, and opened the reference card.
- **Pass mark:** 5 of 6 (the organisation can change it; 80% is the default).
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Ama replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it, off by default): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. It passes when the check is passed and every critical behaviour is shown first time; otherwise the learner goes through the full module, with no "failed" label. Recommended for annual renewals rather than a first sitting.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Use of approved and public AI tools**, from web filtering or cloud access security tools: visits and uploads to public AI sites should fall as use of the approved tool rises.
- **Data loss prevention alerts** for pastes of classified or personal data into AI tools.
- **Reports of AI slips** (their own or a colleague's), which should rise at first: more reporting, not more slips.
- **Reports of impersonation attempts** by voice, video or message, and the time to report.
- **Call-back checks** recorded for unusual requests, where finance or the service desk keep a log.
- **Corrections to AI-assisted documents** found in review, where teams track them.
- **Staff survey items:** "I know which AI tools I can use and what can go in them" and "I'd feel comfortable ringing a senior leader back to check".
- **The hook-to-Apply change** across the organisation (C15-B1 against C15-B3).

These need data from web filtering, data loss prevention, the security team's reports and staff surveys. The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
