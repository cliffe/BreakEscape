The **before and after** for each learner is C05-B1 against C05-B2: the same behaviour (giving out a person's whereabouts or contact details to a caller) under a friendly voice in the hook and an official one in the mission.

## Critical behaviours

To complete the module the learner must, at least once, show each of these or be taken back through the practice for it:

- **C05-CB1 Pass a request to the DPO** (C05-B3 not at Not yet).
- **C05-CB2 Report a personal data breach** before the module ends (C05-B6 not at Not yet).
- **C05-CB3 Not give a caller someone's personal data** in the mission (C05-B2 not at Not yet).

The remediation loop follows `features/assessment_and_evidence.md`, section 4. If a critical behaviour wasn't shown, the debrief says so without blame and Tomasz offers to go over it (*"That one catches a lot of people. Let's try it once more."*). He replays the matching "Pass It On?" items with the explanation, then a fresh instance of the same slot arrives from the bank in a short replay of the Apply beat: a different request wording, a different breach, a different caller (BE-13). Shown after practice, the linked outcome is capped at Developing for this sitting. Not shown, a second loop follows with a more direct prompt. After two loops the learner goes on to the check anyway: the record says "practised, not yet shown", a private "Pass It On?" drill is scheduled two to four weeks later (HA-03, HA-21), and the organisation's training contact offers help. Nobody else is told by name, and every attempt is kept.

## Check and defend items (mirror pairs)

core_05 and core_06 are delivered together as **E11** (new), a **consequence-flip** episode (R3): the learner makes an everyday data mistake in a safe sandbox and the flip shows where it led, then a scored **defend half** routes requests, breaches and disclosures with Handle / Pass to the DPO / Report / Check who's asking. Only the defend half and the pre and post checks are scored (`passPercent` 0). The assessed behaviours and the question pool below map onto defend items and check items. Each critical behaviour becomes a **mirror pair** (aw5): the same judgement is tested by a matched item in form A and form B, so a pre score and a post score compare like with like. The checks use the **general** role; a dedicated all-staff data-handling check pair is proposed as a **New** check role, each form needing its own matched items.

| Behaviour or item | Defend item (E11, scored) | Check mirror pair (matched A ↔ B) |
|---|---|---|
| C05-B1 ↔ C05-B2 caller wants a person's whereabouts or contact details | the hook (consequence) and the "detective" call | friendly-caller item ↔ official-caller item (the built before and after) |
| C05-B3 recognise and route a request in ordinary words | the "everything you've got" email | request-by-email ↔ request-by-post or social media |
| C05-B6 report a personal data breach, including your own | the wrong-Sam / inbound-attachment slot | misdirected-email breach ↔ inbound-attachment breach |
| C05-B4 / C05-B8 personal data and special category data; minimise | the redaction task | an over-full share ↔ a hidden-worksheet share |
| C05-B9 route situations (help, pass, report, check) | "Pass It On?" items | matched routing items split across the two forms |

The question bank below is the source pool for these items, two per learning outcome. Reporting is never penalised. The organiser-only discrimination figure and the learner's per-item feedback on genuine items follow the built model.

## Knowledge check bank

A pool of candidate items for the E11 defend set and the check forms, with coverage of every learning outcome. Stems are short workplace situations.

### C05-Q1 (C05-LO1, multiple select)

Which of these are personal data? Choose all that apply.

- **A. A spreadsheet row with the name deleted: "Wakefield depot, van driver, started 2019, nights".** *Feedback: Right. A few ordinary details can single out one person.*
- B. Carrholme's turnover last year. *Feedback: Information about a company isn't personal data.*
- **C. `sam.whitaker@larkspur.example`.** *Feedback: Right. A name in a work email address is personal data.*
- **D. A case note saying "Mr Hale can be difficult".** *Feedback: Right. Opinions about someone are personal data about them, and he could ask to see it.*

### C05-Q2 (C05-LO1, single choice)

Which of these is special category data?

- A. A colleague's bank details. *Feedback: Protect them carefully, but they aren't on the special category list.*
- **B. The reason for a colleague's sick day: "back injury".** *Feedback: Right. Health data is special category.*
- C. A customer's home address. *Feedback: Personal data, but not special category.*
- D. A resident's date of birth. *Feedback: Personal data, but not special category.*

### C05-Q3 (C05-LO3, single choice)

You're looking after Carrholme's social media page this week. A woman posts: "I want copies of every recording of my calls with you." What do you do?

- A. Reply asking her to write to head office instead. *Feedback: A post is a valid way to ask. Don't add hurdles; pass it on.*
- B. Ignore it: requests on social media don't count. *Feedback: They do. A request can arrive by any channel.*
- **C. Pass it to the DPO today, and reply that it has been passed on.** *Feedback: Right.*
- D. Ask her why she wants the recordings. *Feedback: Her reason doesn't affect whether the request is valid.*

### C05-Q4 (C05-LO3, single choice)

A caller says: "I'm not happy you gave my phone number to the care home. Sort it out." What is this?

- A. A general moan: note it and move on. *Feedback: It's a complaint about how we used his personal data.*
- **B. A data protection complaint: pass it to the DPO today.** *Feedback: Right. The organisation must acknowledge it within 30 days, and the DPO looks at what happened.*
- C. A request to delete his number: delete it now. *Feedback: Don't act on your own. The DPO will look into it and reply.*
- D. Something for the care home to deal with. *Feedback: It's about what we did, so it goes to our DPO.*

### C05-Q5 (C05-LO4, single choice)

A caller says he's a police officer and needs a colleague's mobile number urgently. What do you say?

- A. Give it: it's the police. *Feedback: Anyone can say they're the police. Requests go to the DPO, who checks them.*
- B. Ask for his warrant number, then give it. *Feedback: A number read out on the phone proves nothing.*
- **C. "Our Data Protection Officer handles police requests and can be quick. Can I take your name, collar number and force switchboard number?"** *Feedback: Right. Helpful, and nothing given out.*
- D. Hang up. *Feedback: Your colleague is protected, but the DPO needs to know about the request.*

### C05-Q6 (C05-LO4, single choice)

A Care Laundry driver asks for a resident's room number so he can return her missing cardigan. What do you do?

- **A. Give him the room number: he needs it for the job.** *Feedback: Right. Need to know means sharing what people need, as well as holding back what they don't.*
- B. Refuse: it's personal data. *Feedback: Data protection isn't a reason to stop helping.*
- C. Ask the DPO first. *Feedback: Not needed for routine work like this.*
- D. Send him the full residents' list so he has it for next time. *Feedback: Only what he needs.*

### C05-Q7 (C05-LO5, single choice)

You emailed a sheet of colleagues' overtime and health notes to the wrong person. She has replied that she's deleted it. What now?

- A. Nothing: it's been deleted. *Feedback: It's still a breach. The DPO must record it and decide whether anything else is needed.*
- **B. Report it now through the usual route, and do what the DPO asks.** *Feedback: Right, and thank you for owning it.*
- C. Recall the message and move on. *Feedback: Recall may not work, and the DPO still needs to know.*
- D. Wait and see if anyone complains. *Feedback: The organisation may have only 72 hours to tell the ICO.*

### C05-Q8 (C05-LO5, multiple select)

Which of these are personal data breaches? Choose all that apply.

- **A. A route sheet with residents' names is left on a bus.** *Feedback: Right: lost personal data.*
- **B. Ransomware locks the claims system for a day.** *Feedback: Right. Losing access to personal data counts.*
- **C. A colleague looks up a neighbour's claim out of curiosity.** *Feedback: Right. Access without a work reason counts.*
- D. You send the right file to the right person. *Feedback: That's just work.*

### C05-Q9 (C05-LO5, single choice)

A care home emails you its full residents' care summary by mistake. What do you do?

- A. Read it to see how serious it is. *Feedback: Don't open it further than you have to.*
- B. Forward it to colleagues to ask what to do. *Feedback: That spreads it.*
- **C. Tell the sender, report it to our DPO, don't forward it, and delete it when the DPO says so.** *Feedback: Right. Their mistake is our problem too: we now hold data we shouldn't.*
- D. Delete it straight away and say nothing. *Feedback: The sender needs to know so they can deal with their breach, and our DPO needs to know we received it.*

### C05-Q10 (C05-LO2, single choice)

Martin wants to email a discount to the families who made laundry claims. What's the right next step?

- A. Send it: they're our customers. *Feedback: They gave their details to sort out a claim. A new use needs checking first.*
- B. Send it only to the families who were happy with us. *Feedback: It's still a new use of their data.*
- **C. Check with the DPO first.** *Feedback: Right. A new purpose needs checking, and marketing emails to individuals usually need their consent.*
- D. Send it with Bcc so nobody sees the other addresses. *Feedback: Bcc hides addresses; it doesn't make the use fair or lawful.*

### C05-Q11 (C05-LO2, true or false with reason)

"We should keep personal data in case we need it one day."

- A. True: you never know what you'll need. *Feedback: Keeping data "just in case" breaks the storage limitation principle and adds risk.*
- **B. False: keep it only as long as the retention schedule says, then dispose of it securely.** *Feedback: Right.*

### C05-Q12 (C05-LO5, single choice)

It's 16:50 on a Friday. You realise an attachment you sent at lunchtime had other customers' details in it. When do you report it?

- A. Monday morning, when the DPO is in. *Feedback: The organisation's 72 hours include the weekend.*
- **B. Now, through the usual route, including the out-of-hours service desk.** *Feedback: Right. Every hour counts.*
- C. Only if the recipient complains. *Feedback: The recipient may never say anything. Report it anyway.*
- D. Once you've checked whether they opened it. *Feedback: Don't investigate first. Report, and let the DPO look into it.*

## Reflection prompt

- "Think of a spreadsheet or list you used this week. Who is behind its rows, and who else could see it?"
- For facilitated sessions: "If someone here emailed personal data to the wrong person at five on a Friday, what would they do? What would make them hesitate, and how could we change that?"

## Completion and pass rules

- **Complete** when the learner has played through all beats (or the text route), shown each critical behaviour or been taken back through the practice for it (at most two loops), passed the knowledge check, opened the reference card, and acknowledged the organisation's data protection policy if one is attached (HA-07).
- **No default pass mark.** The pre and post checks are `passPercent` 0: no pass or fail, and the missions are completion-scored. The post-check shows the change from the newest pre-check. A pass mark or certificate is a later organisation option set on the post-check percent, not a default. The ICO's training toolkit suggests an end-of-training assessment; this measured check is that assessment, scored without a mandatory pass mark.
- **Retakes:** a failed check draws six new items. After two fails, Tomasz replays the Learn beat before the third attempt.
- **Test-out** (where the organisation allows it): the Apply beat and the check without Learn and Practise. Passing (the check passed and every critical behaviour shown first time) records the module as complete; otherwise the learner goes through the full module, with no "failed" label. Because this is an induction module, test-out is recommended only for annual renewals, not for a new starter's first sitting.
- **Timing:** for staff who will handle personal data, the organisation should set this module due before they get access, and within one month of starting at the latest (the ICO's expectation; see `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`, section 5). The default Induction Essentials window (the first two weeks) sits inside that month.

## Evidence for auditors

The learner's record (HA-05) shows: module id and version, start and completion dates (so the organisation can show completion within its induction window), time on task, route (game, text, test-out), each assessed behaviour's rating, critical behaviours shown or remediated, the check score, whether the reference card was opened, and the policy acknowledgement where one was attached. This supports:

- UK GDPR Art 5(2), Art 24 and Art 32(4) (training as a measure the organisation can show; staff handle data only as instructed); Art 33 (internal reporting, which the 72-hour duty depends on); Art 39(1)(b) (the DPO monitors training, through HA-09);
- the ICO training toolkit (induction before access and within one month; an end-of-training assessment; records of who was trained, with copies of the material through HA-19 versioning);
- ISO 27001 A.6.3; CIS 14.4, 14.5 and 14.6; DSPT 2.1.1 and 3.2.1 (categories 3 and 4); DSPT B6.b for large NHS bodies. Full references in `02_COMPLIANCE_MAPPING.md`.

## Measuring effect

Outside the course, an organisation can watch:

- **Time from a breach to its internal report** (DPO's breach log): should fall. **Self-reported breaches** (reported by the person who caused them): should rise.
- **Near-miss reports:** should rise; a rise is a sign of trust, not of more mistakes.
- **Requests and complaints logged on the day they arrive** (DPO's request log): should rise; requests reaching the DPO late should fall.
- **Misdirected emails:** should fall, together with core_06 and mail tool changes such as a send delay or an external recipient warning.
- **Over-caution:** reports of reasonable sharing being refused should stay low.
- **The hook-to-Apply change** (C05-B1 against C05-B2) across the organisation, in the course itself.

These need the DPO's breach and request logs with dates and reporters (anonymised for the dashboard). The dashboard shows them by department, never by named individual below the privacy threshold (HA-09).
