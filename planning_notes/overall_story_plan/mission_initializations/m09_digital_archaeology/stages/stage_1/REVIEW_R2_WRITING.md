## Do the choices read as real stances?

Most do. Taking the choices the brief asked about in turn:

- **The raid team (back Quintero / thank Kowalczyk / nothing).** Yes. These are three attitudes to authority and to colleagues, and both payoffs land at the moment they hurt ("You thanked her this morning. Keep doing it." is the best line in the Recruiter scene).
- **The cage bin.** Mostly. Personal (the player's first case) against practical (a lever on Tran). The practical value is clear when the player chooses. The personal one depends on knowing the hand matters, which the player can't know yet. That's acceptable, because the choice is meant to feel like that, but HaX's line should at least say the handwriting isn't Derek's (N-14).
- **The Recruiter.** Not yet. Two of the four routes are beaten by a third, and the cost that would stop that is never said before the choice (N-01). Once the double-cross is priced, the three routes become three stances: certainty about WREN, her network, or a deal with someone you don't trust. Refusing and pulling nothing stays as a pure principle stance, which is fine.
- **Own the spring / hang up.** Yes. This is honesty under a witness against stonewalling, and it is the strongest carry-over choice in the mission.
- **The confession answer.** Yes, on the page: blame, absolution, and deferral (her own line thrown back). These are three real stances, and the safe-opened "You should have taken it to Netherton." said with him listening is very good. The export flattens deferral into absolution (N-17).
- **The flag-4 realisation.** Not a choice as written (N-06).
- **Kowalczyk in the hangar.** Warm or flat is a real stance only on `sleeper_named`: keeping cover, or letting it show. Nothing notices which (N-08).
- **"Was he right?"** Yes. "About Albion, yes." against "No. Not about any of it." is a concession against a refusal to concede, and it sets up m10's talk-down. The reply needs to sit next to the pen (N-07).
