# m09 Digital Archaeology: stages 1–2, review round 1 (writing and drama)

## Scope and method

Reviewer: fresh, read-only, 2026-10-21. Lens: the writing and the drama only. Structure, objectives, rooms and VM wiring are other reviewers' jobs.

Read: `docs/agents/FINALE_BRIEF.md`, the `npc-dialog-review` skill, `README_ink_best_practices.md` (choices, convergence, callbacks), story_dev_prompts 01 and 02, `stage_1/story_arc.md` (cited as **A:line**) and `stage_2/storytelling_elements.md` (cited as **S:line**). Compared against the shipped ink: m02 and m08 opening briefings, m08 closing debrief, m05 Recruiter phone ink. FINALE_ARC was opened only to check what the Publication's key does (§1.2, l.26). No VM material, SecGen or field guides were opened.

Severity: **blocker** = the episode does not work as drama; **major** = a key scene or choice falls flat, or a fix is needed before stage 3/7 build on it; **minor** = polish.

## Overall judgement

This is a good penultimate episode on paper. The premise is strong: a records keeper ordered to destroy records, the player's own file in the building, a plant on the team they met that morning, and a villain who has left the one machine that matters running for them. Several things are excellent: Sorensen's pencil, the Reading quoting the player's own choices back in the villain's hand, the Nightshade file ("Will wait if asked properly. Ask properly."), the Recruiter's "She's generous. It's how we hired her.", and the Albion memo, which puts the reasonable man in front of the monster in the right order.

The weaknesses cluster in three places:

1. **HaX's arc spends itself early.** The quiet stretch repeats what she said in m08's debrief, opens her up an act before the confession, and leaves the confession without its real cost: two agents died in m07 while the name was in her drawer, and Netherton is listening.
2. **The Recruiter's offer is not a real dilemma** for a thorough player, because the optional cross-reference gives the same name and refusing her while pulling her file comes out ahead.
3. **The big surprise is explained to the player three times** (flag 4, HaX's line, the call) when it should be the player's own realisation. The briefing has the opposite fault: too many carry-overs that are only name-checks.

The sample dialogue mostly sounds like the shipped missions: short, dry, cost first. About a dozen lines slip into AI patterns ("It wasn't a guess. It was a hope.", "It doesn't. It means...", "Sorry. That was a lot of words for me."), and a few stock thriller lines need replacing ("Then we finish what we started.", "Don't call me that.").

## What already works (keep)

- **Sorensen.** A clerk ordered to destroy records for the first time in her life, stamping each box "destroyed", then asking for a pencil to write down what she shredded, accurately. The debrief's "a lawyer and a pencil" pays it off. Don't let stage 7 add a speech to her.
- **The Reading.** The villain reading the player's own choices back is the best carry-over device in the finale, and it works because the player feels judged, which a name-check never does. Best entries: "Half is how we arrived here." "They buried it. They understand me better than they admit." "Their first days are not in my file. Someone at SAFETYNET is careful with them."
- **The documents in Tesseract's hand.** Nightshade's file ("Will wait if asked properly. Ask properly."), Derek's costing, the casualty sheet's "An acceptable price for the last arms race", and the Albion memo in plain civil-service prose. Reading the memo before the casualty sheet is the right order.
- **The Recruiter's spine.** "You're standing in my filing system. I thought it polite to ring." "One of your four is mine. Was mine." "She's generous. It's how we hired her." "Then we'll both lose something tonight. I've had more practice." All in her m05 voice (people as line items).
- **The sold-and-lied opening.** The spring's secret rings the phone with HaX on the line. It is the strongest carry-over in the mission.
- **Netherton.** "I am sorry about that. I am not sorry enough to change it." "Bring me a name I can say in court." The two names read before the signature, with no comment after. "They come. You watch them."
- **Smaller voices.** Noor's "Was I going to say yes? Don't answer. I know I was." Gary's lanyard. Old Coyote's "Auditors bring coffee." Each has its own voice in one or two lines.
- **The "not X" rules in §1 of stage 2** (no exposition, fixed strings, one voice per scene) are the right rules. The fixes above mostly enforce them on the arc's own samples.

## Verdict

**Revise before stage 3 builds on it. No blockers.** The episode's shape and cast are right, and most of the sample writing is at the shipped standard. Fix these first, because stages 3 and 7 will inherit them:

1. W-01, W-02, W-03: HaX's arc, so that the confession is the one time she opens up and it carries the m07 dead.
2. W-04 and W-12: the two purge choices, so a careful player still pays a price.
3. W-05 and W-06: give the realisation to the player and put the question of the key, so the hook into m10 holds.
4. W-07 and W-08: give Kowalczyk her face-to-face moment in the hangar and her one human fact.
5. W-09: cut the briefing's name-checks.

The minors can go in with stage 7, apart from W-15 and W-17, which are one-line cuts and should be made now.
