# m09 stage 3, review round 1: do the choices matter?

## Scope and method

Reviewer: fresh read-only reviewer, 2026-10-21. Target: `moral_choices.md` (first draft), read against stage 1 `story_arc.md` (Beats 2.1–2.7, §7–8), stage 2, `FINALE_BRIEF.md`, `03_moral_choices.md` and the `npc-dialog-review` skill §2d. I compared it with the shipped choice scenes that set the standard: m01 Kevin's frame-up (`m01_phone_agent0x99.ink`, `contingency_options`), m02's press terminal (`m02_object_press_terminal.ink`, `confirm_upload` / `confirm_stay_quiet`) and m05's Recruiter call (`m05_phone_recruiter.ink`, `recruiter_deal` to `recruiter_deal_refused`).

What the gold standard does, and what this review measures against:

- **One dilemma per scene, two to four buttons.** m05's call asks one question. m02's terminal asks one.
- **The person in the scene says the cost, in their own voice.** The m05 Recruiter: "Forty-seven is a number that changes weekly." m02's confirm screen says plainly what each side loses, including "You told Ghost this would go out. Keeping it internal breaks that promise."
- **The consequence is felt straight away**, then echoed in the debrief.

Orchestrator rulings taken as settled, not re-argued: the Recruiter's price is the whole deck; the new exports `cage_bin` and `legacy_files_saved`; Noor's cost stands.

Severity: **blocker** stops stage 7 working from this document; **major** breaks one of the document's own five tests, or lets a thoughtful player see through a dilemma; **minor** is wording or polish.

## Findings

No blockers.

### Major

**C-01 (major). The Recruiter's opening "He" points the whodunit at the men.** §3.2, the offer: "One of your four is mine. Was mine. He took my placement for Montana and didn't ask." On first hearing, "He" reads as WREN, the subject of the sentence before it. A refusing player now looks at the two men on the team and away from Kowalczyk and Quintero. The document goes to some trouble (§3.2, "No pronoun for WREN before the name") to keep WREN ungendered, and this line breaks that rule through a pronoun meant for the Architect. It is a fairness problem for m10's accusation. **Fix:** name him. "One of your four is mine. Was mine. Your Architect took my placement for Montana and didn't ask." (Or "Your teacher", if the Recruiter is allowed to know that.) Check every Recruiter and HaX line before "Halina Kowalczyk" for any "he" whose antecedent could be one of the four.

**C-02 (major). On material terms, double-cross beats take; take wins only on honour.** §3.2's route table and its dominance notes. Take gets the name. Double-cross gets the name and half her network. What double-cross costs is a text in the tape room ("It hasn't left."), her 06:00 text in m10 and "About half" on the closing card. None of these changes anything the player has to deal with. The document leans on "keeping a bargain with a criminal is a real position (m02's Ghost deal)", but m02 put a price on breaking that promise, on the confirm screen, at the moment of choosing. Here a thoughtful player sees that breaking their word to an ENTROPY recruiter costs them nothing, and double-crosses. Its own test 2 asks that take "wins on at least one thing the player can see"; it doesn't. **Fix (pick one):** (a) give the double-cross a cost that bites: the half of her people who move include someone m10 needs, or she sells the double-cross to Tesseract along with the invoice, so his ledger sting in m10 also carries one real piece of knowledge (for example, which way 0x00 is coming in); or (b) give keeping the bargain something she pays: on take, she adds one fact she would only give a buyer who kept faith, in m09 and on the same call ("Since you kept it clean: she reports Thursdays."). (b) is cheaper and lands in m09.

**C-03 (major). What refusing actually does is only in an unvoiced text that arrives mid-call.** §3.2, "How the cost is signposted". The whole decision depends on knowing that saying no still saves one tape. That fact is in a phone text that lands while the Recruiter is speaking, and the document lists it (§11 item 34) as an engine check it hasn't yet passed: a text arriving while another phone conversation is open. A player reading her lines will miss it, and if the engine can't do it, the text is gone. **Fix:** put the cost of refusing in the Recruiter's mouth, where m05 put her pressure: "Say no and I wipe it myself. Same deck, no name." That is a threat she would make, it costs no engine work, and it is voiced. HaX's secret then shrinks to one sentence, which can be an aside in the call ("I can get one tape out before she does.") said once the Recruiter has gone quiet, or the text if the engine allows it, with the voiced aside as the fallback.

**C-04 (major). HaX prices the invoice as a strategic risk, but it pays back only as a taunt.** §3.2: HaX's text, "if we buy from her, it's in her books. He reads her books." That tells the player the Architect will learn something that hurts them. In m10 the invoice is "one ledger sting in his evidence, deciding nothing" (§9 item 33). It breaks test 3 ("nothing promises an effect that never comes"). **Fix:** either price it as what it is, a moral cost ("Buy from her and SAFETYNET's in an ENTROPY ledger. He'll quote it at you.") so it doesn't promise a tactical effect, or give it the tactical bite of C-02 (a). The ledger line booking the sale in m09 is good, and it should stay.

**C-05 (major). Noor's choice has a right answer, and her stated cost doesn't hold up.** §4.2. Told before the cage opens: she learns the truth, the legacy files are saved, and she loses her job. Left alone: she "keeps her job tonight". But the player is in the middle of dismantling her employer. Sorensen is arrested, knocked out or in custody by morning, Quintero's people are in the building, and the archive is an ENTROPY front. The job is gone whichever button is pressed, and a thoughtful player will see that. The debrief line for leaving her ("went home at the end of her shift, to the same job and the same list") is the weakest line in the section, because the job isn't there any more. With the files attached, telling her early is plainly the best move: a timing puzzle, not a dilemma. Noor's cost stands (ruling); the fix is to make it a cost that is really hers. **Fix:** (1) keep the cost of telling her as the one that's already written: she becomes a witness, goes to a hotel in Great Falls with Arakawa's people, away from her father's care in Dearborn. Say that before the button, not the job: HaX, "Tell her and she's a witness. She goes tonight, and she doesn't go home to her dad for a while." (2) Give leaving her something real: she goes home to her father tonight, not knowing, still on the list. Debrief: "Miss Haddad went home to her father. She is still on a list, and she doesn't know it." (3) Decouple the legacy files from her (C-07), so telling her is a choice about her, not about loot.

**C-06 (major). The legacy files are lost on a trigger the player is never told about.** §4.3: Sorensen "reaches them when the player opens the plan chest at the back of the cage, the next thing on the critical path." The cage-opening signpost ("Her next trolley is the legacy files. If you want them, she won't stop for asking.") says what is at risk but not when. A player who heads straight for the chest, which is where the mission points them, loses the files without knowing a clock was running. That is test 3's trap. There is also a hole: the trolley is an object in the room, and the first thing most players will try is to wheel it away or take the files off it, not to hit a 66-year-old. **Fix:** HaX says when: "She gets to that trolley the moment you go past her to the chest." Give the fiction a reason the trolley can't be taken: it is already locked into the shredder's feed, or the boxes sit behind her on the shredder line, so only stopping her stops it. If neither fits the staging, let the player take the trolley and move the cost elsewhere (see C-07).

**C-07 (major). The legacy-files chain is overloaded, and it turns two moral choices into a sequencing puzzle.** §4.2, §4.3, §6 "Choices m09 makes inside itself". One prize (the legacy files) has three routes (warned in time in m05; Noor told before the cage; force on Sorensen before the chest), a hidden timer (C-06) and a new export, and it sits in the same eight minutes as the cage bin, the recognition and the card clone. Players won't follow it, and once they do, Noor and Sorensen are no longer about Noor and Sorensen. **Fix:** give the files to one choice only. My recommendation: the files belong to E (Sorensen), where the question is plain and hard: hit a 66-year-old clerk to save eleven placements, or don't. Noor (B) becomes truth against her father (C-05), with no loot. Keep the m05 warned-in-time route as a pure reward ("I didn't send the last one. It's under the desk."), which is a payoff, not a choice. That cuts one route, cuts B's timing dependency, and leaves `legacy_files_saved` with the same meaning.

**C-08 (major). The cage bin is a choice between two records, not a moral choice, and one side can't be read at the hatch.** §3.1. Derek's file has a clear pull: it answers m01, and "it's the only place anyone wrote down why he did what he did" is a good line. The letters are "Tran's whole trade". At the hatch most players haven't met Tran (he is optional and later, Beat 2.6), so the line names a stranger's business. What the letters buy is a talk-down of an optional NPC, which saves optional lore, plus one HaX line in m10. On the default path a thoughtful player isn't torn: they take Derek, which is also what doing nothing gets them. The pattern in `03_moral_choices.md` (discovery, personal stakes, intervention) is missing. **Fix:** give the letters a living person. For example, the latest letter is Social Fabric's next smear job, a named local journalist or union rep the Initiative is about to ruin, and taking the letters gets them warned. HaX: "The other's the Social Fabric letters. Last one's about a woman in Butte they're about to bury. One of them's ash in a minute." Now the choice is the answer to your first case against someone you can still help: past against present. Tran's talk-down can stay as a side benefit. On `m08_nightshade_tidied` the copy keeps its own pull (his words on what he took).

**C-09 (major). Sending the notice shows its cost in m09 and hides its gain.** §3.5. Before the button, HaX names what sending loses (the watch, the inquiry). After it: "Gone." then "Critical Mass watch just went dark. Analysis will ring me." Nobody in m09 says who is still exposed to the flaw or who is safer once it's patched. The gain shows up only as an m10 record answer. Now that every player has to decide (the debrief question), a player who sends feels punished on the night, and the finale's own argument is weighted towards hoarding. **Fix:** one line before the button naming who is exposed, plain and specific: "That flaw's still in every substation that bought Albion's kit. Four hundred, give or take." And one line after a send that is a gain: the vendor's auto-reply, or HaX: "Vendor's acknowledged. Patch window, ninety days." Netherton's debrief question should carry both sides in the same plain terms (see the worst lines).

### Minor

**C-10 (minor). Act 2 asks too much at once.** §2 table, Act 2 rows. Between the cage and the office the player meets D, E (with its timer), the tail of B, F on its branch, G in two or three button presses, H on its branch, and I if they go to the closet: up to seven decisions in about 30 minutes, four of them linked. C-07's merge takes out the worst of it. Beyond that, keep Tran (I) as small as it is, and consider cutting Sorensen's talk-down list (talk against leave differs only by a lore object) down to talk as the quiet way to stop her, paid with one debrief line. In the debrief the memo stance and the notice now come back to back on the same subject. That's acceptable, because they are different questions, but keep each to one exchange.

**C-11 (minor). Tran's cost comes after the button.** §4.4: "He walks. Social Fabric knows we have their letters by morning." is said after the talk-down. Test 3 says the cost comes first. **Fix:** move it to before the choice, from Tran himself, as the price of his deal: "Fine. It's off. I walk out that door, and I make a phone call on the way." The button then reads as a trade.

**C-12 (minor). The sold-and-lied branch has no way to lie.** §3.3. HaX has already heard the receipt on the open line, so hanging up hides nothing from her. It is a refusal to answer. The obvious reply for a player with a secret, a denial ("She's fishing. I never took her money."), isn't offered, and that is the real moral choice here: own it, lie to HaX's face, or hang up. **Fix (optional):** add the denial as a third button. It sets `kept`, lets the call go on to the offer (so a liar can still buy the name), and costs more in m10 than a hang-up, because Tesseract's line exposes a lie, not a silence. If three buttons feels like too many, swap the denial in for the hang-up.

**C-13 (minor). Two notice buttons pass judgement.** §3.5: **[Send the notice. Nine years late.]** puts the verdict in the player's mouth before they act, and spends a phrase m10 gives Tesseract ("Nine years late."), where it lands harder from him. Netherton's debrief question has the same problem (worst lines, 5). **Fix:** **[Send his notice.]** / **[Not yet.]**.

**C-14 (minor). The talk-down lever with Sorensen is hard to parse.** §4.3: **[He kept his own off your list. You're burning yours.]** "He" (the Architect), "his own" (his own what?) and "yours" (her own what?) all have to be worked out at the button. See the worst lines.

**C-15 (minor). Netherton asks a field agent "Do we send it?"** §3.5, debrief. A Director asking permission of an agent reads oddly against HaX's archive line ("It isn't my call. It isn't yours either."). Better: he hands the decision over on purpose, which also explains why he's asking. "You read it. You decide. I'll sign whichever." Then the two buttons.

**Preachiness.** Mostly avoided, and that is a credit to the document. HaX and Netherton say what things cost and move on; the debrief judges nothing. The places that tip over are the button "[Send the notice. Nine years late.]" and Netherton's "he has one more true thing to say to you" (C-13, worst lines). §7's four moral questions are design notes and nobody says them aloud, which is right.

**Are the moral questions asked through play?** Disclose or hoard: yes, four times, through things the player does (send, show, tell, pull), and C-08's fix would add a fifth from the past against the present. HaX's silence: yes, through K and J. Dealing with the Recruiter: yes, once C-02 makes keeping your word cost or pay something. Kowalczyk: yes, through A and N, face to face. Force on people who can't fight back: yes, through E and C, and E is the one where the question is genuinely open.

**What could go.** If the mission needs a lighter Act 2: Sorensen's talk-down list (fold it into one debrief line); Old Coyote's "tell him the truth" (it pays only on `m01_maya_ko`). Neither is wrong; both are cheap to drop. I would keep everything else.

## Worst lines

1. **HaX's private text** (§3.2). *"Say yes and both tapes go. Say no and she wipes it anyway. I can beat her to one. And if we buy from her, it's in her books. He reads her books."* The "it" is unclear (the deck? the file?), "He reads her books" is cryptic and promises a tactical cost that never comes (C-04), and it carries the most important fact in the call in an unvoiced text (C-03). **Rewrite** (with the cost of refusing moved to the Recruiter): *"No, and I can get one tape out before she wipes it. Yes, and SAFETYNET's in her ledger. He'll quote it at you."*

2. **The Recruiter** (§3.2). *"I won't be watching which tape. I'll be watching the deck."* It sounds neat, but it makes the listener work out that she can tell something was pulled and not what. **Rewrite:** *"I can't see which tape you pull. I can see that you pulled one."*

3. **Talk-down button with Sorensen** (§4.3). *"[He kept his own off your list. You're burning yours.]"* Three pronouns to sort out at the button. **Rewrite:** *"[He kept his own papers off your list. Only yours burn.]"*

4. **HaX on Nightshade** (§4.1). *"Keep it, and he stays useful. And he goes on not knowing what he was to him."* "He" and "him" are two different men in one clause. **Rewrite:** *"Keep it, and he stays useful. He never finds out what his teacher wrote about him."*

5. **Netherton's notice question** (§3.5). *"His notice is still on that box. Send it, and we lose the Critical Mass watch and answer to the inquiry. Hold it, and he has one more true thing to say to you. Do we send it?"* The last clause talks about m10's argument from outside the story, and "Do we send it?" asks a subordinate's permission (C-15). **Rewrite:** *"His notice is still on that box. Send it, and the Critical Mass watch goes dark and the inquiry asks when we knew. Hold it, and he can call us hoarders, and be right. You read it. You decide."*

Lines that work and should survive stage 7 untouched: "Bin by the hatch is Derek Lawson's file, and it isn't Derek's handwriting on it."; "When I've finished, dear. You'll want the list to be complete."; "She's sixty-six."; "Was I going to say yes? Don't answer. I know I was."; "...That's mine. You don't get to use it on me."; "You took the name and the file. I said I'd know before your plane did. It hasn't left."

## Verdict

**Revise, then ready for stage 7.** No blockers, nine majors, six minors.

The forced choices mostly work. The price ruling made the Recruiter's deck a real three-way dilemma. K and F are as good as anything in m01–m05, and the debrief no longer reads the night back as a list. The problems sit in two places. First, the Recruiter's call: one pronoun misdirect (C-01), a double-cross that costs nothing (C-02), and the refusal's key fact left in an unvoiced text (C-03, C-04). Second, the cage chain: the legacy files are tied across Noor and Sorensen behind a hidden timer (C-05 to C-07), and the bin is a choice between records rather than between people (C-08). Fix those, and show what sending the notice protects (C-09).

Top five, in order: C-01, C-02, C-07 (with C-05 and C-06), C-03, C-08.
