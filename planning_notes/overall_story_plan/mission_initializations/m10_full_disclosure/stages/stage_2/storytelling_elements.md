Companion to `../stage_1/story_arc.md` ("arc §n"), which has the structure, order and branches. This file has the people (voice, wants, secret, arc, sample lines) and the feel of the night. FINALE_ARC is cited "FA §n" and its thread rows "row n"; a bare "§n" is a section of this file.

Player choices are first-person spoken words in brackets; no printed variables in voiced lines; asterisk cues sparingly and never on a line with a number; no "Not X. **House style for the finale.** The last episode of a TV spy thriller. People say less than they mean, and nobody states the theme. The player's history is said to them by someone with a reason to say it, at the moment it matters, in a line or two. Villains are calm. Violence is a line.

## 2. Dr Adrian Tesseract

Speaker prefix `Dr Adrian Tesseract` (the m07 handset's `The Architect` is not reused in person; the name is the reveal paying off). Sprite `adrian_tesseract`, new; his look is the user's call (FA §8 risk 9). A man in his sixties, origin unplaced. His m07 voice without the distortion.

**Who he is.** SAFETYNET's chief strategist for most of a career, who trained half the field, HaX among them. He walked out seven years ago over a real disagreement (the arms race speeds up the collapse it claims to prevent), and the walk-out was also cover: he had been recruiting for ENTROPY's ends since before Nightshade's intake (FA §8 risk 17). Since then he has been the Architect.

**What he wants tonight.** To be proved right by the people he left: a SAFETYNET hand on the key, so the service that hoarded the map gives it away itself. Not any hand: the agent his old student runs, whose file he has read. He also wants to be argued with. A teacher who has been alone for seven years waits at the console, with hours in hand, for one more good student in the room, and would never say so.

**His secrets**, each one the player can reach:

1. **He lost the argument once and didn't finish it.** Nine years ago he argued the Albion flaw should go to the vendor, lost, drafted the notice and never sent it (m09). The Publication is that notice sent to everyone, with interest. The second rebuttal goes here.
2. **He signed a figure he knew was low.** His systems lead corrected the sheet; he initialled the correction and signed again without changing the number. The first rebuttal.
3. **He needs the player's hand so that his own stays clean.** "Let it hurt afterwards, not during" was his. He has spent seven years handing the hurt to strangers, and tonight he wants a stranger's hand on the key. The third rebuttal.
4. **He doesn't want Kowalczyk to press it.** She has been his since he took her tasking from the Initiative; if she presses it, it proves nothing. That refusal is the one moment the player hears his motive from his own mouth.

**His arc tonight.** Certain and courteous, a host. Each landed point takes a little certainty and none of the courtesy. It ends as the player decides: argued down far enough to stand up empty-handed and offer his cooperation; arrested, unmoved, looking forward to a trial; dead by the console, reaching; dead in his chair, unarmed; gone in the dark with a card that says "Afterwards, then."; or vindicated, walking out with the agent his file said would press it.

### 2.1 How he speaks, and how he takes an argument

The m07 voice without the distortion, and a little more of him: still short, exact and unhurried, but he finishes his thoughts, as a teacher does with a student he rates. Most lines under fifteen words; his case may run to twenty-five, never more. No jargon. One image per scene at most.

- **He grades.** "Better." / "That's a slogan." / "Closer."
- **He asks questions he knows the answer to.**
- **He uses numbers exactly** and never rounds aloud in his own favour. He did on paper; that is the point.
- **He calls the player "Agent" or "0x00", and HaX "Ninety-nine"**, the way an instructor uses a number. Nobody else does. She refused it on his m09 call ("It's HaX. It has been for seven years."), and tonight she answers him again, shorter (§3.1).
- **He is courteous to the end.** He never interrupts, raises his voice or gloats, and he threatens nobody but the player, once, as a courtesy (§2.4, surrender). When he concedes, he gives up exactly what was proved.
- **He never says** "you don't understand", "join me", his motive in a speech, or an apology.

**A landed point.** He stops and looks at the console, not the player. A beat. Then a short concession, exact and unhappy. The narrator marks it the first time only ("He looks at the console for a long moment."); after that the pause does it.

**A miss.** He answers at once, dry, without looking away, and moves on. It is visibly a miss and never a humiliation. He has a small range, so none repeats in a run:

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: That answers a question I didn't ask.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: That's an argument about speed. I asked about a price.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: True, and beside the point.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: You're arguing with someone else.

**From his ledger** (`m04_crew_lost`, `m02_patient_bed4_deceased`, `m06_satoshi_stance`) he reads the player's record back first, quietly. It stings and decides nothing: a well-aimed answer still lands after it (row 47).

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: Nine of the night crew at Albion. I've read the inquiry.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: *pause* Go on. You were saying nobody died for the truth.

### 2.2 His case

When the player walks to the console (arc §9.3 step 1). Three parts, in this order (W9): the Publication, the offer, then the player's record as his evidence.

**The Publication**, six lines at most:

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: At six, everything SAFETYNET ever found goes to everyone. Free. With working detail.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: Eleven hundred to twenty-six hundred, the first month. Hospitals, dispatch, water. People who never had the skill before.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: Then it stops, because every vendor on earth patches at once, and nobody can hoard what everybody has.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: Every year your service keeps its map, it costs more than that. Nobody counts it. I did.

**The offer**, which every player hears (D-21). Plain, and an ask for one action:

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: It has to be one of you. If I press it, it's a crime. If you press it, it's a confession.

You know what it costs, and you know you could."); on `m06_satoshi_stance = wondered` ("You told a man in handcuffs that you sometimes wonder whether we're right. I liked that."); colder on `rejected` ("You told him no without thinking. Think now.").

### 2.3 The three exchanges

One argument in three steps. Claims 2 and 3 open on the exchange just before (W1, N1): a landed point moves him to new ground, a miss lets him press, and he never concedes something that wasn't proved. Each exchange has four fixed slots (S2-12): the rebuttal; an **evidence decoy**, which cites something found tonight against the wrong claim; and two record slots, where a plain decoy fills any slot the player's record doesn't. The rebuttal is in slot 2, then 1, then 3. Rebuttal and evidence decoy are one sentence each and about the same length (N3), so a cold-start player's three buttons can't be told apart by length.

**Exchange 1. "The cost is acceptable."**

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: You've seen the sheet. You think the number's obscene.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: That's the cheapest it will ever be. Wait a year and the number goes up.

**Join**, the three steps. The narration carries the cost; he says little:

> Narrator: Pressing it publishes the database to everyone, everywhere, in the same minute. Eleven hundred to twenty-six hundred people, the first month, by his own figure. You can still step back.
> (the press) Dr Adrian Tesseract: *quietly* Thank you, Agent.

## 3. The rest of the cast

### 3.1 Agent HaX, to the end

`Agent HaX`. RP; short lines, fact then reason then the human bit; drier, never louder, under pressure; care shown by what she does (voice bible).

### 3.3 Halina Kowalczyk (WREN)

`Agent 0x5E 'Lantern'` (stage 9 confirms the prefix form). 47, Polish-British, grew up in Ealing in a Polish family. Task Force Null's breaching lead. Light West London with her family's vowels; warm, quick, a laugh in it.

## 5. Atmosphere, act by act

The bunker was built in the 1960s to outlast a war and refitted last year with ENTROPY's money. Every room shows both: Cold War concrete, steel doors with wheel locks, painted stencils (FALLOUT SHELTER, NO SMOKING, a civil-defence yellow), and on top of it new cable trays, new readers, new screens, all slightly too clean. Stage 5 tries m04's `room_aes_*` plant, workshop and security tiles and m07's `room_pnw_*` first (stage 0 §4). Lighting is for the lighting skill; the notes below say what each room should feel like.

**Act 1: outside, then the first room below.**

- **The forward post.** Night, cold, open sky, wind on the fence. Two vans with their side doors open and the inside lights on, a folding table, a screen with two times on it (the first of the countdown objects, one per main-path room). The gate at the far end under one sodium lamp. Everyone talks a little quieter than they need to. The only warm thing is the vans' light.
- **The blast door and the tunnel.** The door grinds. The tunnel is long, damp, lit every ten metres by caged bulbs; the guard post is a desk, a kettle, a rota sheet. Sound carries.
- **The security office.** Fluorescent, low ceiling, a wall of monitors and a duty board, the player's own photo pinned among the shift notes. It should feel like walking into a room where people were talking about you a minute ago.

**Act 2: the floors. Busy, hot, loud.**

- **The workshop floor.** Benches, solder fumes, racks of half-built crackers, a whiteboard of part numbers. The casualty sheet pinned above the bench where the people who build his tools can see it. It looks like any small electronics firm, and the people at the benches look like anyone.
- **The power room.** The oldest room: Bakelite switches, dials with needles, a battery room behind mesh, a hum the player feels. The new feed bolted onto the old plant with cable ties.
- **The server hall.** The old command room. The 1960s plotting board still on the wall, a map of a war that never came; racks where the map tables were; the schedule on the console. The sealed section's hatch in one corner, painted over and then opened.
- **The media room.** Dark, or lit only by the crew's screens and a story running. On the dark branch, one desk lamp on the drafted story and the player's face.

**Act 3: quiet.**

- **The gallery.** A walkway above the core's roof, narrow, with slit windows down into it. No HaX, no music, the guards' footsteps on steel. Through the slits, the top of a grey head at a console.
- **The outer lock.** A steel door with a countdown object beside it, which reads 04:00 as the lock opens, and on a live head start, "EARLY ACCESS: RELEASED" with no voice to read it.
- **The core.** Plain concrete, one console, one terminal, one chair he is sitting in and one he isn't. Nothing on the walls. The countdown object holds at the last beat's time; his hand rests beside the key. The quietest room in the game.
- **06:00 at the forward post.** Floodlights now, engines running, people sitting on bumpers. The countdown object on the table reaching six and nothing happening.

## 7. Pacing and tension

**The shape.** A slow cold open, a long rising siege, a sudden drop into quiet, then a conversation that is the climax. The risk is spending the tension in Act 2 and arriving at the core tired; the quiet gallery is there to reset the player before the most important ten minutes of the season.

| Stretch | Tension | What carries it |
|---|---|---|
| Briefing and the door | low, rising | the two times on the screen; the invitation; Quintero's objection; the first returning face |
| Flag 1 and the split, 02:10 | held | a small human beat (Arakawa's call) while two people walk into the dark; the player doesn't know they are watching the crime |
| The blast door | rising | the door opening for the player by name |
| Security office | high | the photo on the wall; guards; Ghost's call; clue 1 |
| Workshop, power room | high, varied | two full scenes, each a conversation in a room full of hostiles |
| Server hall | peak | the schedule read aloud until HaX stops reading; the head start's warning; Kowalczyk going ahead |
| Gallery and the lock | drop to silence | HaX gone, no music, one room, one person; the release crossing the lock's screen, or not |
| The core: arrival and flag 4 | quiet, intent | the release set for six, and his hand by the key; his idle lines; the player working while he watches |
| His case, the exchanges | the climax, very quiet | a landed point takes a little sound away; a miss changes nothing |
| The console, WREN, the database, the end | sharp spikes in the quiet | WREN's entrance is the only loud moment; his hand on the key the only fast one |
| 06:00 | release | the hour arriving with nothing published |

**Rules for stage 7.** Full scenes are two or three minutes, hubs with a sticky exit, never monologues. One returning face per room. The clock is said in lines ("ninety minutes", "fifteen minutes to their head start") and shown on the countdown objects, and nothing ends the mission on time. His case is short; the exchanges carry the weight. After a big moment, a short line.
