## 2. Dr Adrian Tesseract

Speaker prefix `Dr Adrian Tesseract` (the m07 handset's `The Architect` is not reused in person; the name is the reveal paying off). Sprite `adrian_tesseract`, new; his look is the user's call (FA §8 risk 9). A man in his sixties, origin unplaced. His m07 voice without the distortion.

**Who he is.** SAFETYNET's chief strategist for most of a career, who trained half the field, HaX among them. He walked out seven years ago over a real disagreement (the arms race speeds up the collapse it claims to prevent), and the walk-out was also cover: he had been recruiting for ENTROPY's ends since before Nightshade's intake (FA §8 risk 17). Since then he has been the Architect.

**What he wants tonight.** To be proved right by the people he left: a SAFETYNET hand on the key, so the service that hoarded the map gives it away itself. Not any hand: the agent his old student runs, whose file he has read. He also wants to be argued with. A teacher who has been alone for seven years waits at the console, with hours in hand, for one more good student in the room, and would never say so.

**His secrets**, each one the player can reach:

1. **He lost the argument once and didn't finish it.** Nine years ago he argued the Albion flaw should go to the vendor, lost, drafted the notice and never sent it (m09). The Publication is that notice sent to everyone, with interest. The second rebuttal goes here.
2. **He signed a figure he knew was low.** His systems lead, Asante, corrected the sheet and initialled her correction; he signed again under it without changing the number (milestone A35: her initials, his signature). The first rebuttal.
3. **He needs the player's hand so that his own stays clean.** "Let it hurt afterwards, not during" was his. He has spent seven years handing the hurt to strangers, and tonight he wants a stranger's hand on the key. The third rebuttal.
4. **He doesn't want Kowalczyk to press it.** She has been his since he took her tasking from the Initiative; if she presses it, it proves nothing. That refusal is the one moment the player hears his motive from his own mouth.

**His arc tonight.** Certain and courteous, a host. Each landed point takes a little certainty and none of the courtesy. It ends as the player decides: argued down far enough to stand up empty-handed and offer his cooperation; arrested, unmoved, looking forward to a trial; dead by the console, reaching; shot unarmed; gone in the dark with a card that says "Afterwards, then."; or vindicated, walking out with the agent his file said would press it.

### 2.1 How he speaks, and how he takes an argument

The m07 voice without the distortion, and a little more of him: still short, exact and unhurried, but he finishes his thoughts, as a teacher does with a student he rates. Most lines under fifteen words; his case may run to twenty-five, never more. No jargon. One image per scene at most.

- **He grades.** "Better." / "That's a slogan." / "Closer."
- **He asks questions he knows the answer to.**
- **He uses numbers exactly** and never rounds aloud in his own favour. He did on paper; that is the point.
- **He calls the player "Agent" or "0x00", and HaX "Ninety-nine"**, the way an instructor uses a number. Nobody outside SAFETYNET names the player; Netherton and the team may also say "Agent" or "0x00" (stage 7, R-1), and nobody but him calls HaX "Ninety-nine". She refused it on his m09 call ("It's HaX. It has been for seven years."), and tonight she answers him again, shorter (§3.1).
- **He is courteous to the end.** He never interrupts, raises his voice or gloats, and he threatens nobody but the player, once, as a courtesy (§2.4, surrender). When he concedes, he gives up exactly what was proved.
- **He never says** "you don't understand", "join me", his motive in a speech, or an apology.

**A landed point.** He stops and looks at the console, not the player. A beat. Then a short concession, exact and unhappy. The narrator marks it the first time only ("He looks at the console for a long moment."); after that the pause does it.

**A miss.** He answers at once, dry, without looking away, and moves on. It is visibly a miss and never a humiliation. He has a small range, so none repeats in a run:

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: That answers a question I didn't ask.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: That's an argument about speed. I asked about a price.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: True, and beside the point.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: You're arguing with someone else.

**From his ledger** (`m04_crew_lost`, `m02_patient_bed4_deceased`, `m06_satoshi_stance`) he reads the player's record back first, quietly. It stings and decides nothing: a well-aimed answer still lands after it (row 47).

> Dr Adrian Tesseract: Nine of the night crew at Albion. I've read the inquiry.
> Dr Adrian Tesseract: *pause* Go on. You were saying nobody died for the truth.

## 3. The rest of the cast

### 3.1 Agent HaX, to the end

`Agent HaX`. RP; short lines, fact then reason then the human bit; drier, never louder, under pressure; care shown by what she does (voice bible).

**Wants.** Her agent out alive and her teacher stopped, in that order, and not to be the one who decides what happens to him. **Secret.** By m10 the name is out (m09's confession). What is left is "the rest" she promised in Helena, when this is over: the night he walked out seven years ago he asked her to come with him. ## 5. Atmosphere, act by act

The bunker was built in the 1960s to outlast a war and refitted last year with ENTROPY's money. Every room shows both: Cold War concrete, steel doors with wheel locks, painted stencils (FALLOUT SHELTER, NO SMOKING, a civil-defence yellow), and on top of it new cable trays, new readers, new screens, all slightly too clean. Stage 5 drafts every room on m04's `room_aes_*` and m07's `room_pnw_*` maps at final size; the forward post, the gallery and the core get new maps and the server hall a dressed or new one at dressing, with bunker props first on the reused maps: blast doors, Bakelite boards, the plotting board, caged lamps, stencilled level markers, FALLOUT SHELTER and civil-defence yellow (RL §2; S5-7). The eight rooms run in one corridor: forward post, tunnel, security office, down the stair to the workshop floor, power room, server hall, gallery, core (RL §1). Lighting is for the lighting skill; the notes below say what each room should feel like.

**Act 1: outside, then the first room below.**

- **The forward post.** Night, cold, open sky, wind on the fence. Two vans with their side doors open and the inside lights on, a folding table, a screen with two times on it (the first of the countdown objects, one per main-path room). The gate at the far end under one sodium lamp. Everyone talks a little quieter than they need to. The only warm thing is the vans' light.
- **The blast door and the tunnel.** The door grinds. The tunnel is long, damp, lit every ten metres by caged bulbs; the guard post is a desk, a kettle, a rota sheet. Sound carries.
- **The security office.** Fluorescent, low ceiling, a wall of monitors and a duty board, the player's own photo pinned among the shift notes, the duty handset ringing on the desk. Along the west wall, an old records cupboard with a keypad and the shift fitter's dented locker. It should feel like walking into a room where people were talking about you a minute ago.

**Act 2: the floors. Busy, hot, loud.**

- **The workshop floor.** Benches, solder fumes, racks of half-built crackers, a whiteboard of part numbers. The casualty sheet pinned above the bench where the people who build his tools can see it. It looks like any small electronics firm, and the people at the benches look like anyone.
- **The power room.** The oldest room: Bakelite switches, dials with needles, a battery room behind mesh, a hum the player feels. The new feed bolted onto the old plant with cable ties, and the refit's bypass panel beside it.
- **The server hall.** The old command room, and the largest room in the bunker. The 1960s plotting board still on the wall, a map of a war that never came; racks where the map tables were; the schedule on the release console. The sealed section's hatch at the east end, painted over and then opened, with the field kit's link clipped on. On the uplink printer by the west door, Social Fabric's story with the player's face on it (the media room is cut; this prop carries it).

**Act 3: quiet.**

- **The gallery.** A straight steel walkway above the core's roof, the stair at one end and the outer lock at the other, with slit windows down into it. No HaX, no music, the guards' footsteps on steel, the lock guard's slow walk to the stair end and back. Through the slits, the top of a grey head at a console.
- **The outer lock.** A steel door with a keypad, its face plate worn on four keys, and the lock panel on the wall three paces short of it, which reads 04:00 as the lock opens, and on a live head start, "EARLY ACCESS: RELEASED" with no voice to read it. He says it instead, as the player walks in.
- **The core.** Plain concrete, his key console, one terminal, one chair he is sitting in and one he isn't. Nothing on the walls but a service door with a dummy camera above it. The key console's readout holds at 04:00; his hand rests beside the key. The quietest room in the game.
- **06:00 at the forward post.** Floodlights now, engines running, people sitting on bumpers. The countdown object on the table reaching six and nothing happening.

## 7. Pacing and tension

**The shape.** A slow cold open, a long rising siege, a sudden drop into quiet, then a conversation that is the climax. The risk is spending the tension in Act 2 and arriving at the core tired; the quiet gallery is there to reset the player before the most important ten minutes of the season.

**Rules for stage 7.** Full scenes are two or three minutes, hubs with a sticky exit, never monologues. One returning face per room. The clock is said in lines ("ninety minutes", "fifteen minutes to their head start") and shown on the countdown objects, and nothing ends the mission on time. His case is short; the exchanges carry the weight. After a big moment, a short line.
