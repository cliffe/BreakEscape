## The talk-down, played three ways

**A thoughtful player with a full save.** Exchange 1 offers four buttons and three of them land (rebuttal, `hardened`, bed four). That is generous and fine for "fun, not hard". The concessions are the best writing in the file: "*pause* I initialled it.", "I had him as rounding. He wasn't." Exchange 2 is where the argument sags. He has just put a real victim on the table (the pumps), and the rebuttal answers with his own past failure: he lost the Albion argument and didn't send the notice. That is true and it stings, but as written it is about him, not about his claim. The link the player needs is that "only a shock fixes it" is untested, because he never tried the slow way to the end. The button doesn't say that (N3, worst line 1). Exchange 3 lands the clean-hands point squarely, and then he answers "Yes. That's why it had to be you.", which agrees with the player and also restates his plan. A thoughtful player hears him confirm the hypocrisy without feeling it cost him anything (N10).

**A cold-start player.** No record answers, so each exchange has two buttons: one decoy and the rebuttal. The rebuttals are always the longer button and always cite something found tonight (the sheet, the Albion memo HaX read off his box, his own offer); the decoys are short and general. Pick the long, specific one three times and you win. R1's coin with labelled faces is still there, with the labels moved from content to length (N3). Exchange 2 makes it worse, because its decoy ("Then why steal it? You could have gone to the press.") is a fair answer to "only a shock fixes it" (N2).

**A player who picks the strong-sounding decoys.** "Hospitals can't patch in a week" is the best-sounding line in exchange 1, and his miss reply teaches the player something: "That's an argument about speed. I asked about a price." That is how a decoy should work. "Then why steal it?" draws "That answers a question I didn't ask.", which is unfair when going to the press is exactly the bloodless shock the `m02_exposed_hospital` answer wins with. "You're not clean. You stole the database." draws "I'll answer for the theft. I asked who should answer for the rest.", and he didn't ask that; he claimed someone has to make it hurt. With three misses the missed openers press well ("You can't tell me who hoards. Then let me tell you who pays."), and "Arrest me, then." followed by the reach is a good payoff for losing: the man you failed to persuade was never going to come quietly.

**Tesseract's voice overall.** Close to m07's handset voice and better for being in the room: short, exact, courteous ("You found her. I wondered whether you would." is a fair echo of m07's "You moved them. I had wondered whether you would."). Two slips: "I read files for a living." contradicts the line every m07 player heard from him (N7), and his claim-3 landed opener concedes things that may not have been proved (N1). He never says "join me", never gloats and never makes a speech, which is right.

## Verdict

**Not clean: revise and re-review (R3).** 0 blockers, 8 majors, 13 minors. R1's structural complaints are fixed and the draft is much better for it. The majors left are single lines, mostly at the core, where small logic slips are what a viewer notices first.

The areas the brief asked about, one line each:

- **The talk-down as an argument:** it now moves with the player and exchanges 1 and 3 have honest decoys. Exchange 2 is the weak link (N2, N3), and the deciding concession doesn't concede (N10).
- **Tesseract's voice:** right, apart from the m07 contradiction (N7) and the over-conceding opener (N1).
- **The accusation in three beats:** works as drama: she lies, throws Arakawa under, cracks, and he looks away. It needs the player's cards placed, and her own "never been through stores" as the last one (N15).
- **HaX and Tesseract:** "It's HaX. It has been for seven years." is earned on every branch; the speaker branch's "Ninety-nine." in reply is the best silence in the file. "The rest" is the weak spot (N4).
- **Netherton and Tesseract:** "Magnus." / "Adrian." at the database is right and restrained. Do it once (N13).
- **The two lethal endings:** TV level is right for both: one narrated line each, no blood, people answering for it. The justified one reads as a split-second act with HaX a second behind. The cold one carries its weight without gloating: he says nothing, HaX says the designation, Quintero takes the weapon, Netherton refuses to sign. Make the cost concrete with the media room's story (N16) and fix the standing/sitting button (N11).
- **Join:** chilling and clean. "It's a key. You press it. Everything I've told you happens." then "Thank you, Agent." and the curve climbing. The narrator's "by his own figure" is a quiet irony for a player who read Asante's correction. Nothing to change.
- **Scorched Earth:** good. "Afterwards, then." on a card in his hand is the right last word for him, provided the other "afterwards" uses are cut (N4). Netherton's line needs its first clause firmed up (worst line 9).
- **Closing card and credits:** five lines and "In this operation" in the order met is the TV shape. Line 4 is still a list (N21). "In memory" remains the most moving item.

Top five to fix first: N10 and N3 (the argument's deciding point and its weakest exchange), N4 (the "afterwards" pile-up and the rest), N5 (the pause), N7 (the m07 contradiction).
