## 3. The round-2 changes, judged fresh

**Five slots on every combination.** The structure is right, and the slot table in §5.3 is the clearest thing in the plan: each slot has a won, a lost and a default occupant. Under the user's question ("do the won-branch versions feel as strong as the lost-branch ones?"), the answer is no, not yet, for the reason in R3-01. Lost branches get the confrontation, the proof, the talk-round. Won branches get a helpful voice. The door slot shows how to do it: the arrested Nightshade is in cuffs, in person, offering a deal the player takes or refuses. Use that template for the other four. A careful player's run should be different, not quieter.

**The core as talk and choices.** Much better. Pausing the clock is the plan's best single line, because it shows the motive instead of explaining it. The order (flag 4, case, three exchanges, console, database, his fate) is clean, and the WREN interruption fits in step 3 once its timing is settled (R3-14). The remaining gap is R3-17: flag 4 still puts VM work in his room. That's acceptable, but it needs idle lines that run on VM progress.

**The WREN whodunit.** Fair and solvable in principle: clue 1 points wrong (Malhotra), clue 2 clears him and narrows the field to two, and clue 3 ties the cracker the player is carrying to whoever handed it over. That is a real deduction with a red herring, and the "check the log again" line is a good hint for a wrong guess. Two things stop it being fair as written. The positions and timeline contradict each other (R3-03), and one branch hands the player the answer (R3-06). The undo (recall on new evidence) is proportionate; make it a radio call, not a walk (R3-15). The bigger risk is a mystery about people the player hasn't met (R3-13).

**Secure vs disclose.** Better balanced than round 2: secure now has a gain and disclose has a cost. The cost still doesn't land (R3-05). For a course about coordinated disclosure it's fine for disclose to be the answer the course favours, but a choice is only felt if the cost is a scene, not a closing-card line. The m09 vendor notice is the round's best educational fix: the player does coordinated disclosure once, small and optional, before the big menu.

**The talk-down.** Three exchanges and two of three is right. The record answers sit under the claim they actually answer, and decoys are the same answers under the wrong claim, so a player who listens wins and one who grabs their best line can miss. The table is well made. Two small things: the first rebuttal's dependency on a find (R3-08), and the third exchange holds four record options, so check at stage 3 that it shows two at most. Lethal force and hold-fire-arrest will now be rare (only decoy-pickers reach them). That follows from "fun, not hard"; stage 3 should accept it knowingly.

**The purge on story events.** Right for learners: slow VM work no longer costs anything in m09. Removing time pressure, though, removed the reason to choose (R3-04). The fix keeps it event-driven but makes the act of opening the cage, or the end of the Recruiter's call, the moment the other item goes.

**The cold starts.** m09's wall of cards is good, and the never-played cards now match the defaults. m10's briefing has the facts but not the man (R3-09). Every never-played row in §3 holds together.

**m10 in ~100 minutes with nine rooms.** It's tight. Count: a briefing with the door scene; four VM flags, two of them the season's hardest; five 2–3-minute scenes; a three-clue mystery with an accusation; two optional console tasks; a core sequence of about 10–12 minutes; one of five ending scenes. m07 ships at 90 with less. It's feasible if the VM stages are sized to it, and if the server hall is relieved (R3-12). Stage 4 should time each act against m07's numbers and name what moves to optional if the estimate passes ~110 minutes. The likeliest candidates are the media room and the backdoor task, which are already optional.

**What works and should be kept as is.** The keyed blast door. "They think you're here to steal their head start. You are." His refusal of WREN. Scorched Earth as his own dead-man switch. The Join signposting. The HaX confession answer. Pieter Vos filling Victoria's desk. Netherton's "You watch them." Torres ringing in from protection. HaX at the fence on the freeze.
