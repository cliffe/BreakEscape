## Strongest ideas to protect

- **The Publication.** It grows out of his signature ("I merely publish the schedule"), puts the m02 Ghost and the m04 disclosure call into one argument, and gives Scorched Earth and Join a reason to exist. Much better than the old auction.
- **Tesseract rings HaX, and the player hears one side.** The best TV beat in either document. Keep it short.
- **HaX tells the player what "afterwards, not during" means to her in m09, and the player can use it against him in m10.** Setup and payoff across the two missions, on the line the season turns on.
- **Nightshade's recruitment file**: found in m09, sent or kept, and it decides which way he tips the board at the door in m10. A finale-internal choice with a felt payoff.
- **Partial release tied to the m07 Austin choice, with a clock that never ends the mission.** Cost from the player's past, not from failing now.
- **Default occupants for every presence slot** (§5.3). This is what makes the carry-overs safe to build.
- **Join in three deliberate steps with a labelled key.** Clear, fair, and follows m01's LAUNCH precedent.
- **"Disclose" with Netherton signing on the line.** Worth the new canon once both it and "secure" have a cost (D-05).
- **Noor Haddad**: the forty-seven made into one person the player can help or warn.
- **The asset freeze as empty guard posts and a gap in the fence**, with Tesseract noticing. A carry-over the player sees on the map.
- **Task Force Null's wall of cards**: the player's own season on one wall, and the right place for the cold-start recap (D-15).
- **Netherton's suspension line** on Scorched Earth.

## Verdict

**Another round needed.** Blockers 0, major 13, minor 11.

The premise is right, and the ending design is careful about fairness. Where it falls short is the brief's main ask, that the player *feels* their choices. Too much of the carry-over budget goes on one-line callbacks in a terminal and a villain's opening speech, and too little on a few big reunions that get room to play. m10 tries to hold every thread at once in less time than m07 had, and its two central twists (the invitation and the sleeper) don't yet make sense from the villain's side or the player's.

Fix first, in this order: settle the durations and cut m10 to a tiered cast (D-01, D-07); give Tesseract a reason to want 0x00 at the console (D-02), which also fixes the Reading's purpose (D-06) and the lethal-force framing (D-11); make the sleeper a whodunit seeded in m09 (D-03); then give every player a mid-mission choice and an in-mission clock in m09 (D-09, D-10) and put the disclosure evidence into play (D-04, D-05).
