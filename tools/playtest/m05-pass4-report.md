## The detective experience (Run A, in-game clues only; I did not read the guide, walkthrough, erb, design review or ink before naming; the walkthrough was read only after the first naming attempt, to find the server-room password)

I saw him in the server hallway the other night... looking wrecked", and the badge log (his badge on the main entrance six minutes before "R. HALLORAN (spare)" on the server hallway, four nights; her primary never) took it to about 90%. Her Zurich dates include 23 Sept, so the spare was not in her hands. - Evidence that moved me, in order: vetting files, flyer, Owen's odd-hours line, badge log, Halloran's alibi, Lisa, leaflet, journal.
- Was the red herring convincing and fair: fair, yes (the log really does say HALLORAN; Patricia herself says Halloran's badge opens the server side; Lisa mentions her row with the CEO; HaX seeds "ask her where it lives"). Convincing, only if you do NOT ask Patricia for the vetting files first. In run B (no files) it was convincing enough that accusing her felt like a reasonable mistake, and the consequences land well ("I watched them cut her badge", the debrief complaint).

Torres", schedule, TalentStack calls) and Patricia's first answer "That's why he might. It isn't proof that he did. Show me it leaving the building" makes the server work feel like proof rather than a chore. A person playing it would likely need 30 to 45 minutes for the whole thing and 10 to 15 for the whodunnit proper.
- Was it fun: the best beats are Halloran's "Oh. Oh, no.", the naming scene built from your own evidence, the mug and print, and Torres in the data centre. The investigation itself is a short, guided funnel rather than a puzzle: each NPC points at the next, HaX's chat names Torres from the moment the files are read, and the to-do list renames itself around him. It is pleasant, not hard.

## Top 5 player-experience notes

1. The vetting-files ask is the whole case in one conversation. If the player asks Patricia for the files first (the obvious thing to ask a security chief), the red herring is dead on arrival and Torres is obvious within minutes. Consider making the files arrive later, or having the Halloran file be less tidy, if the Halloran detour is meant to be a real trap.
2. The game tells you who it is from several directions at once: Patricia's suspect list names two people, the naming menu has only two names, HaX's phone options say "Torres is buried in debt", the to-do list says "Torres' office", Owen says "David's thumb". The other six crypto staff are never in play, so "whodunnit" is closer to "which of these two".
3. The badge log is a good clue: legible, honest, and the "six minutes earlier on the main entrance" pattern is satisfying to spot. The date overlap with Halloran's declared Zurich dates is a nice second-order catch for a careful player.
4. Wrong accusation has teeth. Patricia's "If you're wrong, that's her career", HaX's nudge about the twenty-third and the front door, Torres' "I watched them cut her badge" and the debrief complaint make the mistake feel costly and fair, and the two ways of recovering (Halloran still answers, Torres is still reachable) work.
5. The naming scene (player lines built from the evidence you hold, "Show me it leaving the building" as the next goal) and the confrontation are the strongest part; the argument menu changes sensibly with what you hold (no dollar figure without the bills, no Architect line without flag 4, journal quote when read).
