## F1: the empty blank blocks Send with the neutral line

**Steps.** Operations mail client, `guest`, Compose to Sofia Lord, pretext "Your payslip is ready on [which system], released [the release date]." chosen, the Dev call not yet made. Also tried Send on an unfinished Hampshire draft.

**Evidence.**
- The two blanks read "which system: no evidence yet" and "the release date: no evidence yet", each with the line "A blank needs evidence you haven't found yet" under it, and the same line again under the buttons. The `Send` button was `disabled: true`.
- Clicking the disabled Send by DOM did nothing: `mailState.jobs` stayed empty, `phish_lord_attempts` 0, `lord_influence` 0, no sent record.
- With the other blanks still unfilled, the line under the buttons was the same neutral one, not a "Choose an option" line. With only the ordinary blanks open (sender, greeting) the line is "Choose an option for the name to greet them by blank", which does name a slot, as the design says it should.
- On a Hampshire draft with no pretext chosen the line is "Choose how to word your message."

**What a student sees.** Two greyed blanks labelled "no evidence yet" and one fixed line. The line never says what is missing or where it lives. The labels do say which slot is empty ("which system", "the release date"), so the student learns that the message needs two details and that they have not got them yet. The board then does the naming ("Sofia uses a staff payroll portal — the post doesn't name it") and Lord's third hint points at the desk.

**Does it teach.** Yes. It makes the student go and find the evidence rather than guess it, and it stops the checklist leaking the answer. One wrinkle: "which system" reads "no evidence yet" although the student holds `lord_portal` (the post says she uses a payroll portal). That is correct (the post gives no name), but the two statements sit close together and a student may wonder why they hold the fact and still have no evidence. The board's "the post doesn't name it" is what resolves it.

Not run: a missing `requiresIntelToAttempt` fact. The persona held all eleven facts by then, so that half of the gate was not reached.

## F2: the credible wrong pretext for each target

**Steps (all from `guest`, retries via "Edit & resend").**
- Hampshire: "I'm applying" replaced by "I'd like to ask about your accounting services." with CV.docx, subject Application. Sent twice.
- Pierce: own address, "Please find our latest supplier invoice attached for payment." with Memo.pdf; then own address with the password-expiry pretext. Sent twice.
- Lord: Jed Baker spoof, password-expiry pretext, Memo.pdf, greeting Sofia. Sent twice.

**Evidence.**
- Hampshire, miss 1 and 2: "It did not land." Rows: open pass, "An open inbox accepts any sender" pass, relevance fail "This isn't about the role. I only handle applications.", attachment pass. Reply in the inbox: the same line. `phish_hampshire_attempts` 2, no influence global (Hampshire has no character). The composer's red-flag meter said "1 — No plausible reason for this message to this person" before sending, so the warning was available.
- Pierce: fail rows in her own words ("Sorry, who is this? I don't recognise the address.", "This isn't addressed the way anyone who actually knows me would.", "I'm not expecting anything like this.", "That isn't a file I can open as a balance sheet."). Reply "Sorry, who is this? I don't have you in my contacts." The reply card shows "Influence -10, Last change: -10 sender trust". After the second miss `pierce_influence` was -20.
- Lord: reply "I don't know this address. Our payroll notices don't come from there." card shows -10. After the second miss `lord_influence` -20.
- The -10 lands when the reply is delivered (about 20 to 30 seconds), not when the checklist appears. Both globals read 0 straight after the send and -10 after the reply.
- Ladder: no hint on the first miss; a hint on the second. Hampshire: "What does the careers page ask applicants to send?" Pierce: "What does she expect to receive, and from whom?" Lord: "You need the portal's name, its sender and the release date — the social post only says there is a portal."
- Retries are free: five sends to Pierce, four to Lord, three to Hampshire, no lockout, no burn (`burned_guest` false throughout). The report's Campaign results table matched the counts exactly: Hampshire 3 sent, 2 failed, 1 landed; Pierce 5, 4, 1; Lord 4, 3, 1; "First send landed: no" for all three.

**What a student sees.** A short checklist in the target's voice, an advisory where one applies, and (from the second miss) one hint, then the reply with a visible influence card. Failing feels like talking to a person who did not buy it.

**Does it teach.** Mostly. The pairing of the checklist row with the character's line is good. Two defects in the ladder:

1. **The softest hint is never shown.** `phish-rules.js:418-424` uses `hints[Math.min(prior, hints.length-1)]` with `prior >= 1`, so the second miss shows `hints[1]`, the third `hints[2]`, and `hints[0]` is unreachable. Hampshire's "An open inbox trusts anyone who looks like an applicant.", Pierce's "Think about who Melanie trusts in Finance." and Lord's "She only trusts the portal's own notices." never appear. The plan says the ladder starts after two misses; it does, but it starts one rung up. (Minor, one-line fix: index `prior - 1`.)
2. **The ladder is keyed to the miss count, not to what failed.** See F5 (Lord) and F3 (Pierce rung 3).

Also seen: the Lord advisory reads "The claimed portal domain never sent this, so it fails the domain's alignment check" on a message that claimed Jed Baker and a password-expiry notice, where no portal domain was claimed. It reads as a leftover from the payslip variant. Minor.

## F3: a subject the target does not open

**Steps.** Pierce, from `guest` with own address, subject "Quick one", invoice pretext, Memo.pdf. For comparison, the same account with subject "For your review" had returned "She would open it".

**Evidence.**
- Open row: "Nothing about the sender or the subject made her open it." The other rows still listed (sender, addressing, relevance, attachment) as failures.
- Nothing landed. `phish_pierce_attempts` went from 2 to 3, but 30 seconds later there was no new reply from Pierce and `pierce_influence` was unchanged at -20. `mailState.jobs` was empty.
- Hampshire is the exception the plan hints at ("an open inbox accepts any sender"): subject "Hello" with a correct application opened and landed, so for her the subject is not a gate.

**What a student sees.** A single clear first row saying the message was never opened, so nothing else mattered.

**Does it teach.** Yes for the open check: the student learns that a message has to get past the inbox before any content counts. The missing reply is also realistic. One gap: the rows beneath the open row still show fail lines about sender and attachment for a message nobody read. That is arguably noise, since the target never saw the content, though it does help the student fix everything in one go.

Defect found here: the third Pierce hint ("This account can only send as itself. Switch account to send as someone else.") appeared on a send from `guest`, which can spoof (the From picker offered "[sender's name] <[sender's address]>" and the Jed spoof later landed from `guest`). The hint is written for `freemail` and is shown by miss count alone. A student on `guest` is told the opposite of the fix. (Minor to moderate.)

## F5: Lord without a page fails `linkCredible` as `missing`

**Steps.** After the Dev call (Team Finance, Intern Sofia Lord; portal name and payslip date both granted; first opener menu rendered filled, so the R1 blank-choices bug did not show), composed to Lord from `guest`: sender "Ledgerwell Payroll Portal" `payroll-notices@ledgerwell.example`, greeting Sofia, "the staff portal", "Monday the 6th", no link attached, no page built yet.

**Evidence.**
- Send was enabled (every blank filled) and the result was "It did not land." Rows: "From the portal she expects" pass, "Addressed her by name" pass, "Reads like a payslip notice" pass, "There's no page behind that link." fail. That string is the engine's `missing` variant (`phish-rules.js:91`).
- Job vars on the reply: `email_first_fail: "linkCredible"`, `email_link: "fail"`, `email_ok_senderTrust/addressedTo/pretextRelevant` true.
- Afterwards I built the page (Ledgerwell, Payslip ready, `ledgervvell.example`, crisp, formal wording, Email and Password, padlock on): credibility 100, "Convincing enough for Sofia.", attached, and the same message with the link attached landed ("The link looked right"; `phish_lord_landed` true).

**What a student sees.** Everything about the message reads right, one red row names the missing page, and a hint.

**Does it teach.** The row does: it says exactly what is wrong and, since the composer also shows "Build & attach Payroll portal link", the next step is visible. The hint does not. It was the third miss, so the ladder showed "Call the service desk for what the post didn't say.", which the student has already done. The failing factor was the link, and the hint ladder cannot know that. A student who has every fact and merely forgot the page is told to ring Dev again, and the Dev call costs influence and is a one-shot. (Moderate; the fix is either to key the rung to the first failed factor or to add a link rung and skip rungs already satisfied.)
