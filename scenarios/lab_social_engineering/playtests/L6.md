## Player-experience notes

- **Opening.** Ruth's briefing, then Fairbairn's "Is it going to make my people look stupid?" with two real answers, is a good start. The hub that follows loops on "What am I actually allowed to do here?" and leaves cleanly with "I'll get on." The tutorial prompt then appears over the world.
- **Stage 0 and 1.** Fast and clear. The explanation on a wrong pick is specific. Fairbairn's condition ("No names in anything I read") sets up the report well.
- **Stage 2 (OSINT).** The best-feeling stage. Reading pages, selecting what matters, the Hampshire profile appearing after the careers inbox, the namesake lookup and the PDF Author field all read as detective work. The board's readiness flips are satisfying. Ruth's "properties" nudge in her stage line matters because the metadata fact is invisible on the board.
- **Stage 3 (mail).** Creating the throwaway address and landing Hampshire is smooth. Pierce is the highlight: the picker offers only the facts held, so spoofing Jed with the Q3 sheet on a Friday is something you assemble, not guess. The "Advisory" after the send (DMARC p=none, delivered anyway) teaches the right thing.
- **Stage 5 (call).** Tense and readable (influence bar, tells panel, CALM/ASK lines) but F4 and F5 make a first call confusing.
- **Stage 4 (builder).** With F3 the student has no honest route to a pass and gets contradictory messages ("close, but the extra part gives it away" and a credibility of 75 against a threshold of 70 that still says "Not Sofia"). It is the point where a real student would stall for minutes.
- **Stage 6 (report).** Short and satisfying. The call line in the results table now shows (P6-T0's RP2 problem is not present here). The `why` text on each pick is good and the no-blame framing is consistent.
- **Stage 7.** Triage immediate feedback is the strongest teaching moment in the run: seeing my own Pierce message come up as a catchable phish with the header panel explaining each tell. "Weakest skill: Links (caught 2 of 3). Practise it in the mail client, then look it up in your lab sheet." is a fair result line. Tomasz is dry and kind. His closing line echoes Fairbairn's. Then nothing happens (F2).
- **Pace.** About 20 minutes wall clock, 879 commands, with several clumsy turns. This is an AI pace, not a student's; it says nothing about the 60 to 90 minute target.
- **Five best lines.** (1) Fairbairn: "If someone clicks, that's my training's fault, not theirs." (2) Tomasz: "We're writing Dev a script, not a warning." (3) Triage: "A pass means consistent, not trustworthy." (4) Pierce reply: "Thanks Jed, that's the one I was chasing. Opening it now." (5) Report: "Failed sends are not wasted: in a live test each one is a chance for a member of staff to report you."
- **Five worst lines.** (1) "I'm helping , your new starter in ." (2) "I'd just want to check her name on the new-starter list before I read that out." (a dead end for the player) (3) "Some people would fall for it. Not Sofia." next to a 75 that beats the threshold. (4) Ruth's "Something else?" with one choice and no key. (5) "Sofia uses a staff payroll portal (name not given)" after it was given.
- **What the run taught.** The Hampshire/Pierce/Lord ladder (open inbox, trusted name, careful target needing page plus call facts) and the defender flip land clearly. A student who reads the explanations learns that a spoof beat a careful person and that a pass on SPF, DKIM and DMARC proves consistency, not honesty.
