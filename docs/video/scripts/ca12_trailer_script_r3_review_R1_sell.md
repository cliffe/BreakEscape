## Physical-security accuracy (awareness level)

- **Tailgating** (v8a "slipping in behind a badge-holder"; Jo; the flip line): correct. ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control 7.2 (Physical entry) covers controlled entry points and preventing unauthorised access through them. The UK National Protective Security Authority (NPSA, formerly CPNI) access-control guidance names tailgating as the common way past a badge door.
- **Hi-vis pretext** ("dress the part", Jo's "A uniform..."): this is a well-known pattern in physical tests, and the scenario's own factor text states the right lesson ("A hi-vis, a clipboard or a job title are easy to put on and prove nothing on their own", line 297). The trailer shows the pattern only, with no method beyond what any member of staff sees. A2 holds.
- **Visitor process** (v10 "Check the list. Escort them."): matches ISO/IEC 27002:2022 7.2 (visitors authenticated, logged and escorted where appropriate). "Lock your screen." matches 7.7 (Clear desk and clear screen).
- **No-blame reporting** (Lorna: "Nobody's in trouble for slowing a pushy visitor down."): matches the NCSC's Cyber security culture principles (2023), which ask organisations to build safety and trust so people report openly. The rubric's own sources (Renaud 2020; ISACA Journal 2023 vol. 6) warn that blame makes staff hide problems. Finding 4 matters for the same reason: the trailer should not make Yusuf the punchline.
- **Authorised testing**: a signed authorisation and an agreed scope before any physical test are standard practice. The Penetration Testing Execution Standard (PTES) pre-engagement section covers scope, rules of engagement and written permission. The scenario follows it (brief line 5, letter line 1870, `scopeNote` line 659). The trailer needs finding 5 to show it.
